134 rulings in 129 cases to 94 towns, Jan 6, 2022 to Oct 7, 2026. A case can have more than one letter, and Gill’s one letter rules on two bylaws. 1 bylaw awaiting a decision is listed at the end.

  • PetershamCourt ruling, solarRuled for the developer; sent back to the board, Jul 14, 2026

    Sunpin Energy Services, LLC v. Zoning Board of Appeals of Petersham, 497 Mass. 794 (2026)

    The Supreme Judicial Court held that a town must give solar reasonable opportunities and may deny a special permit for a protected solar project only when necessary to protect public health, safety or welfare. It vacated the Land Court judgment that had upheld Petersham’s denial and sent the matter back to the zoning board to decide under that test. The Appeals Court (105 Mass. App. Ct. 641, July 9, 2025) had ordered the permit issued.

    Source

    Everything about Petersham

  • WendellCourt ruling, battery storageAttorney General disapproval upheld, Mar 12, 2026

    Town of Wendell v. Campbell, No. 25 MISC 000014 (LER) (Mass. Land Ct. Mar 12, 2026)

    The Land Court upheld the Attorney General’s Nov 14, 2024 disapproval of Wendell’s general (licensing) bylaw for battery storage: its effect on land use was predominant, so it had to be adopted as a zoning bylaw under G.L. c. 40A, § 5, and a licensing bylaw could not be used to get around the solar protections in § 3. Decided March 12, 2026.

  • DuxburyCourt ruling, battery storageTown denial annulled, Jun 23, 2025

    Duxbury Energy Storage, LLC v. Town of Duxbury Zoning Board of Appeals, No. 23 MISC 000643 (Mass. Land Ct. Jun 23, 2025)

    The Land Court held that a standalone battery facility not connected to any solar is a structure that facilitates the collection of solar energy, so it is protected by § 3, and annulled the town’s denial. The Attorney General has cited it in battery storage decisions since September 2025 (Rochester, Case #11532).

    Attorney General’s Rochester decision (case 11532), which cites the ruling

    NextSun Energy LLC v. Fernandes, No. 19 MISC 000230 (RBF) (Mass. Land Ct. May 9, 2023)

    The Land Court held that a battery energy storage system built with a solar project in Norton is a structure that facilitates the collection of solar energy, so it has the § 3 solar protections (decision May 9, 2023; amended judgment entered June 23, 2023).   • Waltham (city)Court ruling, solarSolar protection enforced, Jun 2, 2022

    Tracer Lane II Realty, LLC v. City of Waltham, 489 Mass. 775 (2022)

    The Supreme Judicial Court held that Waltham’s ban on large-scale solar in all but 1-2% of the city was impermissible, and that § 3 claims require balancing the town’s interest against the burden on solar. Every Attorney General solar or battery storage decision since mid-2022 relies on it.
