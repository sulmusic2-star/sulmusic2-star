State law and court rulings on solar and battery storage

State zoning law (G.L. c. 40A, § 3) says a town may not prohibit solar energy systems, or regulate them unreasonably, except where needed to protect public health, safety or welfare. Battery storage has that protection only through two Land Court rulings, and the Land Court is a trial court, so for batteries this isn’t settled law the way it is for solar. The rulings are NextSun Energy LLC v. Fernandes (2023) and Duxbury Energy Storage, LLC v. Town of Duxbury Zoning Board of Appeals (June 23, 2025); both applied the reasoning of the Supreme Judicial Court in Tracer Lane II Realty, LLC v. City of Waltham (2022).

Since October 1, 2026, every Massachusetts city and town has had to accept consolidated permit applications for small clean-energy projects and decide them within 12 months.

  1. Nov 20, 2024

    2024 Climate Act siting reforms (Chapter 239 of the Acts of 2024)

    The law created a single consolidated local permit for small clean-energy projects (generation under 25 MW, storage under 100 MWh) with a 12-month municipal decision deadline, and moved larger projects to a consolidated Energy Facilities Siting Board permit. It was approved Nov 20, 2024.

    • Chapter 239 of the Acts of 2024 (session law)
    • House bill summary for H.5641 (includes AG disapproval of Becket bylaw)

  2. Jun 23, 2025

    Duxbury Energy Storage v. Duxbury Zoning Board of Appeals (Land Court)

    On June 23, 2025 the Land Court held that a standalone battery storage facility is protected by the solar provision of G.L. c. 40A, § 3 and annulled the town's denial. Towns have cited it as a reason they cannot ban battery storage outright.

    • Climate Case Chart: Duxbury Energy Storage, LLC v. Town of Duxbury ZBA
    • Sherin and Lodgen: Battery storage moves forward in Massachusetts as barriers fall (Mar. 25, 2026)

  3. Feb 27, 2026

    225 CMR 29.00: consolidated local permit for small clean energy infrastructure

    The state energy department’s final rule, promulgated Feb 27, 2026, lets towns accept consolidated local permit applications starting July 1, 2026 and requires them to accept them by Oct 1, 2026. Towns must decide within 12 months of a complete application; if they do not, the permit is treated as approved subject to standard conditions.

    • 225 CMR 29.00 final regulation (DOER)
    • Foley Hoag: Massachusetts finalizes clean energy siting and permitting rules (Mar. 11, 2026)
    • DOER Model Small Clean Energy Infrastructure Facility Siting and Permitting Bylaw (May 22, 2026)

  4. Feb 27, 2026

    Energy Facilities Siting Board rules (980 CMR 13.00, 14.00, 16.00)

    Siting Board rules on consolidated permits for large facilities, de novo review of local permit applications, and pre-filing engagement were published Feb 27, 2026. Under 225 CMR 29.00, a town that lacks capacity may ask the Siting Board, within 60 days of receiving an application, to review it instead.

    • Foley Hoag: Massachusetts finalizes clean energy siting and permitting rules (Mar. 11, 2026)
    • 225 CMR 29.00 final regulation (DOER)

  5. Apr 6, 2026

    Statewide lithium battery siting moratorium bill (H.4690)

    H.4690 (Rep. Kelly Pease) proposed a statewide temporary moratorium on siting lithium battery storage facilities. The Telecommunications, Utilities and Energy committee sent it to a study order (H.5323) on April 6, 2026.

    • malegislature.gov: H.4690 bill history

  6. May 22, 2026

    The state energy department model consolidated permitting bylaw

    The state energy department’s May 22, 2026 model bylaw shows towns how to fold the 225 CMR 29.00 process into local zoning. The state energy department states that adopting it is not required for the regulation to apply.

    • DOER Model Small Clean Energy Infrastructure Facility Siting and Permitting Bylaw (May 22, 2026)

  7. Jul 14, 2026

    Sunpin Energy Services v. Petersham Zoning Board of Appeals (Supreme Judicial Court)

    On July 14, 2026 the Supreme Judicial Court annulled Petersham's denial of a solar special permit, holding that a town may deny a protected solar project only where needed to protect public health, safety or welfare. The case went back to the zoning board.

    • Foley Hoag: SJC's Sunpin decision a win for solar development (July 17, 2026)

  8. Oct 1, 2026

    Oct 1, 2026 deadline and the state energy department application guideline

    As of Oct 1, 2026 every city and town must accept consolidated local permit applications using the state energy department’s required forms (pre-filing checklist, application, completeness checklist and decision form). The state energy department’s guideline on the application took effect that day.

    • DOER Guideline on the Consolidated Local Permit Application
    • 225 CMR 29.00 final regulation (DOER)

  9. No single date

    The state energy department model zoning bylaws for solar and battery storage

    The state energy department issued final model zoning bylaws for solar photovoltaic installations and for battery energy storage systems in August 2026, after releasing drafts in October 2025. Towns may adopt them as written or modify them, and the state energy department recommends review by municipal counsel.

    • DOER Model Zoning Bylaw: Solar Photovoltaic Installations (August 2026)
    • DOER Model Zoning Bylaw: Battery Energy Storage Systems (August 2026)
    • DOER model bylaws public webinar slides (Oct. 21, 2025)

  10. No single date

    Attorney General review of town moratoriums (G.L. c. 40A, § 3)

    Since the Supreme Judicial Court's 2022 Tracer Lane II decision, the Attorney General's Municipal Law Unit has disapproved town moratoriums on solar and battery storage, including Carver (2022), Ware (2023), Northfield (2024), and Blandford, Becket and Worthington (2026), for lacking a specific health, safety or welfare justification. The office has approved battery storage zoning that regulates rather than prohibits, such as Wilbraham, Hadley and Petersham.

    • Attorney General decision, Blandford Case #12153 (Mar. 11, 2026)
    • Foley Hoag: Massachusetts AGO rejects yet another municipal solar and battery storage moratorium (Mar. 2026)
