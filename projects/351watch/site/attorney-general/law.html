State law

State zoning law (G.L. c. 40A, § 3, ninth paragraph) says no zoning bylaw or ordinance may prohibit or unreasonably regulate solar energy systems, or the structures that facilitate collecting solar energy, except where necessary to protect public health, safety or welfare. It binds cities as well as towns.

Checked Oct 10, 2026 from the Attorney General’s decision letters and the towns’ postings.

Battery storage has that protection only through Land Court rulings that read a battery as a structure that facilitates collecting solar energy, and the Land Court is a trial court, so for batteries this isn’t settled law the way it is for solar. The rulings are NextSun Energy LLC v. Fernandes (Norton, May 9, 2023), Duxbury Energy Storage, LLC v. Town of Duxbury Zoning Board of Appeals (June 23, 2025) and Town of Wendell v. Campbell (March 12, 2026), which upheld the Attorney General’s disapproval of a licensing bylaw for battery storage; all three follow the Supreme Judicial Court’s reasoning in Tracer Lane II Realty, LLC v. City of Waltham (2022).

What the courts and the Legislature have said, in date order

Since October 1, 2026, every Massachusetts city and town has had to accept consolidated permit applications for small clean-energy projects and decide them within 12 months.

  1. Aug 24, 2004

    Zuckerman v. Town of Hadley, 442 Mass. 511 (Supreme Judicial Court)

    The Supreme Judicial Court held that a zoning bylaw limiting the rate of development with no end date is unconstitutional absent exceptional circumstances; a temporary moratorium tied to planning can stand (Sturges v. Chilmark). It is the test the Attorney General applies to every moratorium, including data-center ones.

    • Opinion text (copy)

  2. Jun 2, 2022

    Tracer Lane II Realty, LLC v. City of Waltham, 489 Mass. 775 (Supreme Judicial Court)

    The Supreme Judicial Court held that zoning allowing large-scale solar in only 1 to 2 percent of a city unreasonably regulated solar under G.L. c. 40A, § 3: a town may regulate solar but not effectively prohibit it, and § 3 claims weigh the town’s interest against the burden on solar.

    • Attorney General’s Carver decision (Nov 14, 2022), the first to apply it

  3. May 9, 2023

    NextSun Energy LLC v. Fernandes (Land Court, Norton)

    The Land Court held that a battery energy storage system built with a solar project is a structure that facilitates the collection of solar energy, so it has the § 3 protections. The Attorney General has cited it in battery-storage decisions since September 2023.

    • NextSun Energy LLC v. Fernandes, No. 19 MISC 000230 (RBF)

  4. Nov 20, 2024

    2024 Climate Act siting reforms (Chapter 239 of the Acts of 2024)

    The law created a single consolidated local permit for small clean-energy projects (generation under 25 MW, storage under 100 MWh) with a 12-month municipal decision deadline, and moved larger projects to a consolidated Energy Facilities Siting Board permit. It was approved Nov 20, 2024.

    • Chapter 239 of the Acts of 2024 (session law)

  5. Jun 23, 2025

    Duxbury Energy Storage v. Duxbury Zoning Board of Appeals (Land Court)

    On June 23, 2025 the Land Court held that a standalone battery storage facility is protected by the solar provision of G.L. c. 40A, § 3 and annulled the town's denial. Towns have cited it as a reason they cannot ban battery storage outright.

    • Climate Case Chart: Duxbury Energy Storage, LLC v. Town of Duxbury ZBA
    • Sherin and Lodgen: Battery storage moves forward in Massachusetts as barriers fall (Mar. 25, 2026)

  6. Jul 9, 2025

    Sunpin Energy Services v. Petersham Zoning Board of Appeals (Appeals Court)

    The Appeals Court reversed the Land Court and ordered Petersham to issue the special permit for a 4.3 MW solar array, holding that the board’s tree-clearing reason did not justify a denial under § 3. The Supreme Judicial Court took the case and ruled on July 14, 2026.

    • Appeals Court slip opinion, 24-P-18

  7. Feb 27, 2026

    225 CMR 29.00: consolidated local permit for small clean energy infrastructure

    The state energy department’s final rule, promulgated Feb 27, 2026, lets towns accept consolidated local permit applications starting July 1, 2026 and requires them to accept them by Oct 1, 2026. Towns must decide within 12 months of a complete application; if they do not, the permit is treated as approved subject to standard conditions.

    • 225 CMR 29.00 final regulation (DOER)
    • Foley Hoag: Massachusetts finalizes clean energy siting and permitting rules (Mar. 11, 2026)
    • DOER Model Small Clean Energy Infrastructure Facility Siting and Permitting Bylaw (May 22, 2026)

  8. Feb 27, 2026

    Energy Facilities Siting Board rules (980 CMR 13.00, 14.00, 16.00)

    Siting Board rules on consolidated permits for large facilities, de novo review of local permit applications, and pre-filing engagement were published Feb 27, 2026. Under 225 CMR 29.00, a town that lacks capacity may ask the Siting Board, within 60 days of receiving an application, to review it instead.

    • Foley Hoag: Massachusetts finalizes clean energy siting and permitting rules (Mar. 11, 2026)
    • 225 CMR 29.00 final regulation (DOER)

  9. Mar 12, 2026

    Town of Wendell v. Campbell (Land Court)

    The Land Court upheld the Attorney General’s disapproval of Wendell’s general (licensing) bylaw for battery storage: its effect on land use was predominant, so it had to be adopted as zoning under G.L. c. 40A, § 5, and a licensing bylaw could not be used to get around § 3.

    • Decision, 25 MISC 000014 (LER)
    • Greenfield Recorder report (Mar. 16, 2026)

  10. Apr 6, 2026

    Statewide lithium battery siting moratorium bill (H.4690)

    H.4690 (Rep. Kelly Pease) proposed a statewide temporary moratorium on siting lithium battery storage facilities. The Telecommunications, Utilities and Energy committee sent it to a study order (H.5323) on April 6, 2026.

    • malegislature.gov: H.4690 bill history

  11. May 22, 2026

    The state energy department model consolidated permitting bylaw

    The state energy department’s May 22, 2026 model bylaw shows towns how to fold the 225 CMR 29.00 process into local zoning. The state energy department states that adopting it is not required for the regulation to apply.

    • DOER Model Small Clean Energy Infrastructure Facility Siting and Permitting Bylaw (May 22, 2026)

  12. Jun 25, 2026

    Data-center Statement of Expectations (Governor’s office)

    The administration’s framework for large data centers: energy supply and efficiency, air quality, water use, labor, community benefits and transparency. It first applied to the state sales-tax exemption and was made a permitting condition by Executive Order 658 in September.

    • Pierce Atwood summary of the framework and the order

  13. Jul 14, 2026

    Sunpin Energy Services v. Zoning Board of Appeals of Petersham (Supreme Judicial Court)

    On July 14, 2026 the Supreme Judicial Court ruled for the developer: a town may deny a special permit for a protected solar project only when necessary to protect public health, safety or welfare. It vacated the Land Court judgment that had upheld Petersham’s denial and sent the matter back to the zoning board to decide under that test.

    • Foley Hoag: SJC's Sunpin decision a win for solar development (July 17, 2026)
    • Climate Case Chart: case history

  14. Aug 2026

    The state energy department model zoning bylaws for solar and battery storage

    The state energy department issued final model zoning bylaws for solar photovoltaic installations and for battery energy storage systems in August 2026, after releasing drafts in October 2025. Towns may adopt them as written or modify them, and the state energy department recommends review by municipal counsel.

    • DOER Model Zoning Bylaw: Solar Photovoltaic Installations (August 2026)
    • DOER Model Zoning Bylaw: Battery Energy Storage Systems (August 2026)
    • DOER model bylaws public webinar slides (Oct. 21, 2025)

  15. Sep 8, 2026

    Executive Order 658 on data centers

    State agencies may not permit a data center above 25 MW of peak demand until the developer shows it meets the June framework and files a community benefits agreement with the host community, reviewed by the Office of Environmental Justice and Equity. The order also bars non-disclosure agreements with permitting agencies and sets up ratepayer protections. Town bylaws and moratoriums are unaffected; the Attorney General’s Gill and Templeton letters note the order.

    • Boston.com report (Sept. 8, 2026)
    • Mintz summary of the order

  16. Oct 1, 2026

    Oct 1, 2026 deadline and the state energy department application guideline

    As of Oct 1, 2026 every city and town must accept consolidated local permit applications using the state energy department’s required forms (pre-filing checklist, application, completeness checklist and decision form). The state energy department’s guideline on the application took effect that day.

    • DOER Guideline on the Consolidated Local Permit Application
    • 225 CMR 29.00 final regulation (DOER)

  17. No single date

    Attorney General review of town moratoriums (G.L. c. 40A, § 3)

    Since the Supreme Judicial Court's 2022 Tracer Lane II decision, the Attorney General's Municipal Law Unit has disapproved town moratoriums on solar and battery storage, including Carver (2022), Ware (2023), Northfield (2024), and Blandford, Becket and Worthington (2026), for lacking a specific health, safety or welfare justification. The office has approved battery storage zoning that regulates rather than prohibits, such as Wilbraham, Hadley and Petersham.

    • Attorney General decision, Blandford Case #12153 (Mar. 11, 2026)
    • Foley Hoag: Massachusetts AGO rejects yet another municipal solar and battery storage moratorium (Mar. 2026)
