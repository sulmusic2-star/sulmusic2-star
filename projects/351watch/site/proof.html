How far ahead agendas are posted

Massachusetts law requires notice 48 hours before a meeting, not counting weekends and holidays. We measured 4,116 agendas from 103 towns whose platforms publish a posting time, for meetings from Apr 13, 2026 to Oct 9, 2026. The first posting went up a median 5 days before the meeting. 82% were online at least 48 hours before the meeting day, and 94% at least 24 hours before.

The Attorney General's record on moratoria

A Massachusetts town bylaw takes effect only after the Attorney General reviews it; city ordinances are not reviewed. We read all 2,719 decision letters issued from January 2022 to October 10, 2026 and found 131 on solar or battery storage. Since 2024 the Attorney General has ruled on 15 solar or battery-storage moratorium articles in 12 towns and disapproved 15, citing the zoning protection for solar and storage in G.L. c. 40A, §3. Bylaws that regulate rather than ban fared differently: 109 of 114 were approved in whole or in part. Data-center moratoria have been approved, because data centers are not a protected use. Crawler rules

The crawler reads only publicly posted agenda pages and identifies itself as 351WatchBot. It follows each site's robots.txt, checking again on every redirect, makes at most one request per second to a site, never logs in and never gets around bot protection. Scanned agendas are read with OCR. Every item links to the original agenda so you can check it yourself.

351 Watch compiles public records: agendas and warrants that towns post under state open meeting laws, Attorney General decision letters, court rulings and legislative records. It is informational only and is not legal advice. Confirm dates and terms with the town clerk or the linked source before relying on them.
