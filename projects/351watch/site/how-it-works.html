How it works

Towns post their meeting agendas online. Every morning we check the new ones for solar, battery storage, data centers, zoning, affordable housing and cell towers, and write each one up in a plain sentence with a link to the town’s own agenda.

It’s free during the beta. It’s for information only, not legal advice. Always check dates with the town clerk.

Which boards

Planning boards, zoning boards of appeals, conservation commissions, select boards and city councils, and town meeting warrants, wherever the town posts them online.

We also include wetlands permit requests for solar and battery projects filed with the state.

How early you hear

Most agendas go up about 5 days before the meeting. Massachusetts law asks for 48 hours’ notice, not counting weekends and holidays.

How we know

We looked at 4,116 agendas from 103 towns, for meetings from Apr 13, 2026 to Oct 9, 2026. Half of them were posted 5 days or more before the meeting.

Towns we can’t check yet

Some town websites don’t allow automated reading, and some don’t post agendas with dates. We don’t get around those limits. We ask the town hall instead, and you can ask us to add a town from its page.

State review of moratoriums

In Massachusetts, a town bylaw takes effect only after the Attorney General reviews it. Since 2024 the Attorney General has turned down all 15 solar and battery moratoriums that towns passed.

What that means

State law (G.L. c. 40A, §3) protects solar and battery storage. Towns may set rules for them but can’t ban them. City ordinances don’t go to the Attorney General.

We went through all 2,719 decision letters from January 2022 to October 10, 2026. 131 were about solar or batteries. Bylaws that set rules did better than bans: 109 of 114 were approved in whole or in part.

Data-center moratoriums have been approved, because state law doesn’t protect data centers.

How we collect agendas

Our program, AgendaAheadBot, visits town websites once a day and reads only public agenda pages. It follows each website’s robots.txt rules.

If you run a town website and want it to stay away, add this to your robots.txt:

User-agent: AgendaAheadBot
Disallow: /
Details for website managers

It identifies itself on every visit as:

AgendaAheadBot/0.1 (+https://agendaahead.com/bot)

Visits are signed with Web Bot Auth. The public key is at /.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory.

  • It checks robots.txt before every visit, including redirects.
  • It asks a website for at most one page a second, or less often if the website says so.
  • It opens each agenda page about once a day and each agenda once.
  • It never logs in, fills in forms or gets past a bot check.
  • It skips files larger than 40 MB.

Want something different? Send us a note.

Privacy

We don’t use accounts, cookies or tracking. We keep only what your alerts need.

What we keep and why

For your alerts we keep your towns and subjects, and, if you give them, your email address, a name for the alerts and a chat webhook link.

Your alerts have two links. The private link can change or delete them. The share link can only show them.

We email only the alerts you asked for: at most once a day, only when something new is posted, and only after you confirm your address.

Our email provider, Resend, sends the emails. We never sell or share your email. We delete it when you unsubscribe or delete your alerts. Every email has a one-click unsubscribe link.

This browser remembers the alerts you set up here. Clearing this site’s data removes them.

If paid plans start, Stripe will handle payment. We would keep only the Stripe customer and subscription IDs.

Some people joined an earlier waitlist. We delete each entry on request, or 12 months after signup.
