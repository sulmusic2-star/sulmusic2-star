How Agenda Ahead works

Every morning our crawler reads the agendas that town boards post online. We flag items about solar, battery storage, data centers, moratoria and other land-use topics, and link each one to the agenda it came from. Alerts for your town and its neighbors are free. It’s informational only, not legal advice.

What we read

Every day we read the agendas of 240 of 351 Massachusetts cities and towns, 33 of the 57 New York towns we list and all 39 Rhode Island towns. That means planning boards, zoning boards of appeals, conservation commissions, select boards and city or town councils, plus town meeting warrants where towns post them. The Massachusetts towns we read are home to 73% of the state’s people (measured Oct 10, 2026). We also add MassDEP wetlands filings for solar and battery projects.

How early you hear

Massachusetts law requires notice 48 hours before a meeting, not counting weekends and holidays. We measured 4,116 agendas from 103 towns whose platforms record a posting time, for meetings from Apr 13, 2026 to Oct 9, 2026. The first version went up a median 5 days before the meeting, and 82% were online at least 48 hours before the meeting day.

Of the 1,169 items in the current data, 164 are for meetings still ahead. We first saw those a median 3 days before the meeting.

Towns we can’t read yet

111 Massachusetts towns aren’t read automatically yet. Most block automated visitors or ask crawlers not to visit. We respect both and don’t get around them; we ask those towns and their vendors for access instead.

  • Site blocks automated visitors: 50 towns, such as Aquinnah, Ashburnham and Athol.
  • No current dated agendas found online: 17 towns, such as Abington, Acton and Clarksburg.
  • Site firewall blocks automated visitors: 11 towns, such as Arlington, Berkley and Dedham.
  • Agendas load only with JavaScript: 9 towns, such as Ashfield, Cheshire and Foxborough.
  • Site asks crawlers not to visit: 6 towns, such as Barre, Cummington and Easton.
  • Agenda platform not supported yet: 6 towns, such as Everett, New Ashford and North Andover.
  • No agenda listing found: 4 towns, such as Hancock, Monroe and Peabody.
  • Agenda vendor blocks automated visitors: 3 towns, such as Cambridge, Framingham and Revere.
  • Agenda vendor asks crawlers not to visit: 3 towns, such as Hopkinton, Williamstown and Worcester.
  • Site asks for slower crawling than we support yet: 2 towns, such as Harwich and Watertown.

How the Attorney General rules on moratoria

A Massachusetts town bylaw takes effect only after the Attorney General reviews it; city ordinances are not reviewed. We read all 2,719 decision letters issued from January 2022 to October 10, 2026 and found 131 on solar or battery storage. Since 2024 the Attorney General has ruled on 15 solar or battery-storage moratorium articles in 12 towns and disapproved 15, citing the zoning protection for solar and storage in G.L. c. 40A, §3. Bylaws that regulate rather than ban did better: 109 of 114 were approved in whole or in part. Data-center moratoria have been approved, because data centers are not a protected use.

Our crawler, AgendaAheadBot

AgendaAheadBot reads only public agenda pages and identifies itself on every request with this User-Agent:

AgendaAheadBot/0.1 (+https://agendaahead.com/bot)

Requests are also signed with Web Bot Auth (HTTP Message Signatures, Ed25519). The public key is at /.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory, and each request names it in a Signature-Agent header.

  • It checks robots.txt before every request, including each redirect, and skips anything disallowed for AgendaAheadBot or *.
  • It makes at most one request per second to a site, or waits longer if robots.txt sets a Crawl-delay.
  • It reads each meeting listing about once a day and downloads each agenda once. Scanned agendas are read with OCR.
  • It never logs in, never submits forms and never tries to get past a CAPTCHA or other bot check. It skips files larger than 40 MB.

To stop it, add this to your robots.txt. It takes effect on the next visit:

User-agent: AgendaAheadBot
Disallow: /

To slow it down, add a Crawl-delay line with the number of seconds to wait. If you run a town website and want a different arrangement, send us a note with your site’s address. The last daily run, on Oct 12, 2026, read 240 Massachusetts towns; none failed three or more days in a row.

Privacy

Agenda Ahead has no accounts or passwords. We don’t use cookies or analytics.

For an alert list we store the towns and topics you chose, its name, the webhook URL and email address if you give them, when it was made and changed, and whether each daily message went through. It is stored privately at our host, Vercel. Each list has two random links: a private link that can view, change or delete it, and a share link that can only view it and its feeds. Deleting a list removes it from our storage. We show webhook URLs only in shortened form, because anyone with the full URL can post to your channel.

We store an email address only if you give one. We use it only to send the alerts you asked for, after you confirm it, and we never share or sell it. It is deleted when you unsubscribe (every email has a one-click link) or delete the alert list.

Your browser keeps the links of alert lists you made or opened, so you can find them again. Clearing this site’s data in your browser removes that list.

Alerts for one town and its neighbors are free. If paid plans open, Stripe will handle payment, and we would store only the Stripe customer and subscription IDs. Before alert lists existed, some people joined a waitlist; we keep those entries privately and delete each one on request or 12 months after signup, whichever comes first.

Corrections and contact

We read every message. The form doesn’t ask for contact details, so we can’t reply; a correction shows up on the site once we’ve checked it.

Agenda Ahead reads the agendas that towns post under state open meeting laws. It is informational only, not legal advice. Check dates and details with the town clerk or the linked agenda.
