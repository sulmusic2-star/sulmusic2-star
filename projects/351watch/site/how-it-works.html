How it works

Every morning we read the agendas towns post online. We flag solar, battery, data center and zoning items. Each one links to its agenda.

It’s informational only, not legal advice.

What we read

We read 312 towns’ agendas every day.

Which towns and boards
  • 240 of 351 Massachusetts cities and towns.
  • 33 of the 57 New York towns we list.
  • All 39 Rhode Island towns.

The Massachusetts towns we read are home to 73% of the state’s people.

How early you hear

Agendas usually go up 5 days before the meeting.

How we measured it

Massachusetts requires notice 48 hours ahead, not counting weekends and holidays.

We timed 4,116 agendas from 103 towns. The meetings ran from Apr 13, 2026 to Oct 9, 2026.

The first version went up a median 5 days ahead. 82% were online 48 hours before the meeting day.

164 of our 1,169 current items are for meetings ahead. We saw those a median 3 days before the meeting.

Towns we can’t read yet

111 Massachusetts towns block automated readers or post no current agendas.

Why, town by town

We respect blocks and don’t get around them. We ask those towns for access instead.

  • Site blocks automated visitors: 50 towns, such as Aquinnah, Ashburnham and Athol.
  • No current dated agendas found online: 17 towns, such as Abington, Acton and Clarksburg.
  • Site firewall blocks automated visitors: 11 towns, such as Arlington, Berkley and Dedham.
  • Agendas load only with JavaScript: 9 towns, such as Ashfield, Cheshire and Foxborough.
  • Site asks crawlers not to visit: 6 towns, such as Barre, Cummington and Easton.
  • Agenda platform not supported yet: 6 towns, such as Everett, New Ashford and North Andover.
  • No agenda listing found: 4 towns, such as Hancock, Monroe and Peabody.
  • Agenda vendor blocks automated visitors: 3 towns, such as Cambridge, Framingham and Revere.
  • Agenda vendor asks crawlers not to visit: 3 towns, such as Hopkinton, Williamstown and Worcester.
  • Site wants slower crawling than we support: 2 towns, such as Harwich and Watertown.

Moratoriums and the Attorney General

Since 2024, the Attorney General rejected all 15 solar and battery moratorium articles.

What that means

A Massachusetts town bylaw takes effect only after the Attorney General reviews it. City ordinances are not reviewed.

State law (G.L. c. 40A, §3) protects solar and battery storage. Towns may regulate them but not ban them.

We read all 2,719 decision letters from January 2022 to October 10, 2026. 131 were about solar or batteries.

The 15 moratorium articles came from 12 towns. Bylaws that regulate did better: 109 of 114 were approved in whole or in part.

Data-center moratoriums have been approved, because state law doesn’t protect data centers.

Our crawler, AgendaAheadBot

It reads public agenda pages only, and it follows each site’s robots.txt.

To block it, add this to your robots.txt:

User-agent: AgendaAheadBot
Disallow: /
How it behaves

It sends this User-Agent on every request:

AgendaAheadBot/0.1 (+https://agendaahead.com/bot)

Requests are signed with Web Bot Auth. The public key is at /.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory.

  • It checks robots.txt before every request, including redirects.
  • It makes at most one request per second to a site, or follows Crawl-delay.
  • It reads each listing about once a day and each agenda once.
  • It never logs in, fills in forms or gets around a bot check.
  • It skips files larger than 40 MB.

The last daily run, on Oct 12, 2026, read 240 Massachusetts towns. None failed three days in a row.

Privacy

No accounts, no cookies, no analytics. We store only what your alerts need.

What we store

For an alert list we store your towns, topics and list name. We also store a webhook URL or email address if you give one.

Each list has two links. The private link can change or delete it. The share link can only view it.

If you give an email address, we store it with your alert list. We send it only the alerts you asked for. That’s once a day, and only when there’s something new. First we ask you to confirm the address.

We send email through our provider, Resend. We never share or sell your address. We delete it when you unsubscribe or delete the alert list. Every email has a one-click unsubscribe link.

Your browser remembers the links of alert lists you made. Clearing this site’s data removes them.

If paid plans open, Stripe will handle payment. We would store only the Stripe customer and subscription IDs.

Some people joined an earlier waitlist. We delete each entry on request, or 12 months after signup.

Corrections and contact

We read every message. We can’t reply, because the form asks for no contact details.
