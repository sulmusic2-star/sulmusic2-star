Follow a town

How it works

Towns post their meeting agendas online. Every morning we check the new ones for solar, battery storage, data centers, zoning, affordable housing and cell towers, and write each one up in a plain sentence with a link to the town’s own agenda.

Everything here is for information only, not legal advice. Always check dates with the town clerk.

Which towns we check

243 of the 351 cities and towns in Massachusetts, 33 towns in New York and all 39 cities and towns in Rhode Island.

Which boards and topics

Planning boards, zoning boards of appeals, conservation commissions, select boards and city councils, and Town Meeting warrants, wherever the town posts them online. We also include wetlands permit requests for solar and battery projects filed with the state.

Topics: Solar · Battery storage · Data centers · Moratoriums · Clean-energy permit rules · Zoning changes · Affordable housing (40B) · Cell towers · Wetlands permits · all topics.

How early you hear

Half of agendas go up 5 or more days before the meeting. Massachusetts law requires at least 48 hours’ notice, not counting weekends and holidays.

How we know

We looked at 4,116 agendas from 103 towns, for meetings from Apr 13, 2026 to Oct 9, 2026, and counted the days from when each was first posted to the meeting.

What the labels mean

Each item carries one word for where it stands, taken from the agenda’s own wording.

Vote
The board is listed to decide it at this meeting.
Hearing
A public hearing, under the agenda’s own heading: anyone can attend and ask to speak.
Possible vote
The agenda says the board may vote, or lists it for a decision without saying so outright.
Discussion
On the agenda to talk about, with no vote listed.
Mentioned
The subject comes up in the agenda’s words, with nothing to discuss or decide.
Filing
A wetlands permit application filed with the state, before the Conservation Commission hears it.
Moved to Tue Nov 10
The agenda itself moves the item to a later meeting. "Asked to move": the applicant asked and the board hasn’t said.
Canceled
The town canceled the meeting or withdrew the item.
New on an agenda
A project no agenda we have read listed before. An item continued or moved from an earlier meeting is never new.

A meeting with No time listed has no time on its agenda; Warrant not posted yet means the Town Meeting date is known from an earlier agenda and the warrant isn’t up.

Towns we can’t check yet

Some town websites turn away programs like ours, and some don’t post agendas with dates. We don’t get around those limits. We ask the town hall instead, and you can ask us to add a town from its page.

Why, town by town
  • the town’s website shows a check meant to stop programs like ours, and we don’t get around it: 50 towns, such as Aquinnah, Ashburnham and Athol.
  • the town’s website has no recent agendas with dates on them: 17 towns, such as Abington, Acton and Clarksburg.
  • the town’s website turns away programs like ours: 11 towns, such as Arlington, Berkley and Dedham.
  • the town’s agenda page only works in a full web browser, which our program can’t use yet: 9 towns, such as Ashfield, Cheshire and Foxborough.
  • the town’s website asks programs like ours to stay away, and we respect that: 6 towns, such as Barre, Cummington and Easton.
  • the town posts agendas on a system our program can’t open yet: 6 towns, such as Everett, New Ashford and North Andover.
  • We couldn’t find where the town posts its agendas: 4 towns, such as Hancock, Monroe and Peabody.
  • The company that hosts the town’s agendas turns away programs like ours: 3 towns, such as Cambridge, Framingham and Revere.
  • The company that hosts the town’s agendas asks programs like ours to stay away, and we respect that: 3 towns, such as Hopkinton, Williamstown and Worcester.
  • the town’s website asks programs like ours to visit less than once a day: 2 towns, such as Harwich and Watertown.

Attorney General rulings

In Massachusetts, the Attorney General reviews every town bylaw before it can take effect. Since the Supreme Judicial Court’s Tracer Lane II decision (June 2, 2022), the Attorney General has ruled on 15 town moratoriums on solar or battery storage and disapproved all 15 (18 Town Meeting articles in 14 towns). Every ruling, with its letter.

How we collect agendas

Our program, AgendaAheadBot, visits town websites once a day and reads only public agenda pages. It follows each website’s rules for programs like ours. For website managers.

What it costs

Following a town and the towns next to it is free, with no account. There’s nothing to buy. If we ever charge businesses that watch many towns, we’ll say so here first.
