What the state has said about Wendell’s bylaws

  • Court ruling: attorney general disapproval upheld (decided Mar 12, 2026). Town of Wendell v. Campbell, No. 25 MISC 000014 (LER) (Mass. Land Ct. Mar 12, 2026), court ruling.

    The Land Court upheld the Attorney General’s Nov 14, 2024 disapproval of Wendell’s general (licensing) bylaw for battery storage: its effect on land use was predominant, so it had to be adopted as a zoning bylaw under G.L. c. 40A, § 5, and a licensing bylaw could not be used to get around the solar protections in § 3. Decided March 12, 2026. Source

  • Attorney General: disapproved (letter dated Nov 14, 2024). Article 1 (May 1, 2024 Special Town Meeting), general bylaw (licensing).

    Wendell's citizen-petitioned general bylaw to license battery storage was disapproved because it regulated land use and should have been adopted as zoning. The Land Court upheld this in March 2026. Decision letter, case 11380

  • Attorney General: approved in part (letter dated Mar 1, 2023). Article 30 (June 4, 2022 Annual Town Meeting), zoning regulation.

    Wendell's ground-mounted solar and energy-facility bylaw was approved except its herbicide rules and its complete ban on stand-alone battery storage in all districts. The Attorney General said the ban was not backed by health, safety or welfare evidence. Decision letter, case 10721
