Attorney General reviews of town bylaws (4)

  • Zoning bylaw, battery storage. Disapproved by AG, Nov 14, 2024.

    A May 1, 2024 Special Town Meeting adopted a general bylaw to license battery energy storage systems; the Attorney General disapproved it on Nov. 14, 2024, ruling it regulated land use and had to be adopted as a zoning bylaw. The town sued, and a Land Court decision posted in March 2026 affirmed the disapproval.

    • Land Court decision, Town of Wendell v. Campbell, 25 MISC 000014 (posted Mar. 2026)
    • Athol Daily News: No Assault and Batteries asks Land Court to overturn AG's decision (May 28, 2025)

  • Attorney General: ag disapproval upheld, 2026-03. Town of Wendell v. Campbell, Land Ct. 25 MISC 000014 (LER), court ruling.

    The Land Court upheld the AG's Nov. 14, 2024 disapproval of Wendell's general bylaw licensing battery storage, finding that its effect on land use was predominant, so it had to be adopted as zoning. Licensing decisions could otherwise evade s. 3 review. (Exact date not printed in the copy reviewed; it was reported Mar. 16, 2026.) Source

  • Attorney General: disapproved, Nov 14, 2024. Article 1 (May 1, 2024 STM), general bylaw (licensing).

    Wendell's citizen-petitioned general bylaw to license battery storage was disapproved because it regulated land use and should have been adopted as zoning. The Land Court upheld this in March 2026. Decision letter, case 11380

  • Attorney General: approved in part, Mar 1, 2023. Article 30 (June 4, 2022 ATM), zoning regulation.

    Wendell's ground-mounted solar and energy-facility bylaw was approved except its herbicide rules and its complete ban on stand-alone battery storage in all districts. The AG said the ban was not backed by health, safety or welfare evidence. Decision letter, case 10721
