# Broad scan: proven software-delivered services with no good Massachusetts equivalent

Scope note (Oct 10, 2026): This was a broad, one-pass scan. The shared web-search budget ran out partway through. After that, only direct fetches of primary pages were possible, and several of those (winnie.com, mass.gov EEC, mass.gov DPU, outdoorithm.com, newsfromthestates.com) returned 403 or 429. Every claim below is labeled as a verified fact (with a source) or as an inference or estimate. Topics excluded by the assignment were not researched.

## Q1. Which of the suggested ideas are already served in Massachusetts and should be dropped?

### Takeaway
Six of the suggested ideas already have working MA coverage, and so does one I added (court docket alerts). Heating-oil price comparison and buying groups, campsite cancellation alerts, street-sweeping and tow alerts, heat-pump installer quotes, and court docket alerts all have active competitors. Electricity supplier comparison already exists too (EnergyBot), and residential supply is also a legal risk. A newcomer would not be filling a gap in any of these.

### Cited Findings
**Heating oil comparison and buying groups (drop)**
- HeatFleet covers Massachusetts. It shows ZIP-level MA prices (for example, "from $5.15/gal" for 150 gal at ZIP 01005) and lists MA regions (Cape Cod, Western Mass, South Shore) and counties. It reports 113,815 searches in the last year, a 4.9 Google rating and "hundreds of local oil company deals." Dealers get free accounts to update prices. Its site does not say how it makes money. — [HeatFleet](https://heatfleet.com/)
- Green Energy Consumers Alliance runs an active nonprofit heating-oil buying group in MA. Its member price was $6.01/gal prompt-pay on 10/09/26, and it says members save about 10–50¢/gal compared with the MA and RI price surveys. — [Green Energy Consumers](https://greenenergyconsumers.org/prices)
- The state DOER publishes heating-oil price surveys. The latest entry seen showed a $5.24/gal average with a 10th–90th percentile range of $4.68–$5.90 (the date was not confirmed in the fetch). The same page links to a "Find a heating oil dealer" tool. — [mass.gov heating fuel prices](https://www.mass.gov/service-details/massachusetts-retail-propane-prices)

**Campsite cancellation alerts (drop)**
- Campnab has MA park pages (for example, Nickerson State Park and Massasoit State Park). — [Campnab Nickerson](https://campnab.com/nickerson-state-park-cancelation-app); [Campnab Massasoit](https://campnab.com/massasoit-state-park-cancelation-app)
- Campnab pricing: memberships at $10, $20 or $30/mo ($90, $180 or $270/yr), or one-time scans at $10, $15 or $20. It says it monitors 3,200+ parks and 7,000+ campgrounds and can scan 650+ Recreation.gov permits and tickets. It reported more than 3.5 million alerts sent. — [Campnab pricing](https://campnab.com/pricing); [Sports Digest press item](https://sportsdigest.in/campnab-celebrates-sending-3-5-million-campsite-availability-alerts/82222/)
- Outdoorithm advertises free cancellation alerts for "113+ state parks" in MA. This comes from a search snippet; a direct fetch returned 429. — [Outdoorithm MA](https://outdoorithm.com/campground-alerts/states/massachusetts)
- MA DCR itself offers free email alerts for cabin and yurt cancellations. — [Boston Magazine](https://bostonmagazine.com/?p=2649752)

**Street-sweeping and tow alerts (drop)**
- Tow Zone Alerts was founded in 2022 by Cambridge residents. It is free and sends text alerts for street cleaning and street-occupancy permits in Boston and Cambridge, plus peer tow alerts in Somerville and Chelsea. It makes money from opt-in "What's Happening" local business messages (some sponsored, at most one per week). Cambridge sees about 6,000 street-occupancy permits a year. — [Cambridge Day, Dec 17 2024](https://www.cambridgeday.com/2024/12/17/service-helps-its-users-prevent-a-ticket-or-a-tow-keeping-drivers-from-spaces-too-good-to-be-true/)
- SpotAngels has offered street-cleaning reminders in Boston since 2018. The Toad app says it covers Boston, Cambridge, Somerville, Chelsea and Malden. The City of Boston runs a free No-Tow email reminder. — [Boston.com 2018](https://www.boston.com/cars/local-news/2018/12/04/spotangels-parking-boston/); [Toad listing](https://apkcombo.com/toad-parking-alerts/app.heytoad.toad/); [boston.gov street sweeping](https://www.boston.gov/es/departments/public-works/street-sweeping-city)

**Heat-pump / Mass Save installer quotes (drop)**
- EnergySage is based in Boston and received MassCEC funding. It launched a Heat Pump Marketplace in October 2022. Installers "pay us a small fee to participate… only after they are vetted." Its 2013 solar model was a per-watt commission from the winning installer. Third-party revenue estimates ($5–10M; $10M) are unofficial. — [Wikipedia: EnergySage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EnergySage); [EnergySage about](https://energysage.com/about); [Renewable Energy World 2013](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/articles/2013/02/energysage-offering-one-stop-shopping-for-solar-pv.html); [prospeo estimate](https://prospeo.io/c/energysage-revenue)

**Competitive electricity supply comparison (drop: legal risk and already served)**
- The AG's January 2025 report found $73.7M in net losses for individual residential customers on competitive supply from July 2023 to June 2024. The AG continues to call for ending the individual residential market. — [search summary of mass.gov / WBUR coverage](https://Www.wbur.org/news/2024/04/03/competitive-electricity-suppliers-massachusetts-cost)
- The House passed an energy bill in March 2026. It would let individual municipalities ban competitive suppliers from signing new contracts or renewing existing ones, end automatic renewals and variable-rate contracts, and add licensing requirements. It then went to the Senate. Earlier Senate versions would have barred any "supplier, energy marketer or energy broker" from signing new residential contracts (municipal aggregation exempt). I could not confirm a final enacted law. — [Green Energy Consumers](https://www.greenenergyconsumers.org/news/tuckedhousebillbigbreakthroughcompetitiveelectricsuppliers); [Renewable Energy World](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/energy-business/policy-and-regulation/massachusetts-could-soon-ban-retail-electricity-providers/)
- An electricity broker must hold a DPU license before serving customers. The license costs $100, requires attending a DPU broker training session, is renewed annually by July 1, and needs a separate expansion filing to serve residential customers. — [mass.gov DPU how-to](https://mass.gov/how-to/apply-for-a-competitive-supplier-or-electricity-broker-license); [Harbor Compliance](https://harborcompliance.com/massachusetts-energy-licensing)
- EnergyBot already compares MA residential and business electricity plans (page updated Oct 9, 2026). — [EnergyBot MA](https://www.energybot.com/electricity-rates/massachusetts/)

**Court docket alerts (an extra idea I checked; drop)**
- Trellis covers MA state court dockets, which Social Law Library lists among 45 jurisdictions as of August 2026, and offers case-tracker alerts. — [Social Law Library Aug 2026](https://www.socialaw.com/about/news-and-noteworthy/2026/august-2026/trellis-search-state-court-dockets/); [Trellis alerts](https://support.trellis.law/migration/alerts-dashboard-overview)

### Inferences
- Heating oil is the clearest "proven elsewhere" model, but HeatFleet plus an active nonprofit co-op leave little room in MA. A newcomer would need its own angle, such as auto-ordering, and that was not researched.
- Free tools (Outdoorithm, Tow Zone Alerts, Boston's own reminders) undercut paid alerts on campsites and parking in MA.
- Comparing residential electricity offers carries regulatory risk in MA: possible local bans, possible limits on brokers, and an AG who is hostile to the market. Business comparison is already served by EnergyBot and others.

### Gaps
- HeatFleet's revenue model (dealer fees or commissions) is not disclosed. Search ran out before other sources could be checked.
- Final status of the 2026 MA energy bill (conference committee or signature) is unconfirmed. newsfromthestates and mass.gov fetches returned 403.

## Q2. Candidate A: kids' activity registration alerts plus a statewide MA town-rec, swim and camp aggregator

### Takeaway
This is the strongest candidate. MA demand evidence is good: swim lessons and camps sell out at fixed online registration times, and DCR says most Learn to Swim applications will be rejected. Two proven models exist: marketplace fees (ActivityHero takes 15% of paid registrations) and paid availability alerts (Campnab charges $10–$30/mo). I found no MA tool that lists all 351 towns' recreation programs with registration-open and spot-opened alerts, though I did not run a dedicated search for one.

### Cited Findings
- A Boston Globe essay (2024) says that in the Boston area "demand always seems to exceed availability" for kids' swim lessons. Sign-up windows "seem to always fall during work hours and be online only," and registration dates are hard to find. The author refreshed the registration page on two devices at 10 a.m., and her first attempt failed. Camps with swim training are "their own competitive rat race for spots." — [Boston Globe, Jun 4 2024](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2024/06/04/magazine/why-are-swim-lessons-for-kids-so-hard-to-find)
- MA DCR's Learn to Swim program fills first-come, first-served, and "with such high demand, most applications will not be accepted." — [DCR 2025 Learn to Swim form](https://app.smartsheet.com/b/form/8ccac316ef164e4b97e2b99d16886886)
- Town example: Sutton, MA says all of its 2025 summer swim classes are full. — [Town of Sutton](https://suttonma.gov/638/Swim-Lessons---Levels)
- A February 17, 2026 Boston Globe opinion essay describes Boston-area summer camps filling in minutes. This comes from a search summary; the essay was not fetched. — [Boston Globe, Feb 17 2026](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/02/17/opinion/summer-camp-registration-essay/)
- ActivityHero pricing: free directory listing; "MarketingHero" at $0/mo plus a 15% fee on paid registrations through ActivityHero.com; "Starter" at $49/mo for 150 engagements. — [ActivityHero business pricing](https://business.activityhero.com/pricing)
- ActivityHero scale: founded in 2012 in Palo Alto, 4M+ families reached, 10,000+ camps and teachers listed, about $2.2M raised. Its Boston provider page showed no listings in the fetched content. — [yespress profile](https://yespress.io/activityhero); [ActivityHero Boston](https://www.activityhero.com/in/boston-ma/activity-providers)
- Sawyer (Brooklyn) raised $11M in 2019. Providers pay it a share of registration fees. — [EdSurge 2019](https://edsurge.com/news/2019-05-14-sawyer-raises-11m-to-connect-parents-and-providers-for-educational-playtime)
- Campnab's alert pricing ($10–$30/mo, or $10–$20 per scan) shows that parents and consumers will pay for "notify me when a spot opens" on scarce public inventory. — [Campnab pricing](https://campnab.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- How software could run it: crawl each town's public program catalog (towns use various rec platforms). Parse registration-open dates and remaining capacity, send alerts by email, push or SMS, and bill through Stripe as a per-season pass or per-program scan. A second layer could take affiliate or 15%-style referral fees from private camps. No per-customer manual work is needed. The main ongoing cost is maintaining scrapers across many platforms, which is suited to an AI coding agent.
- Legal and operational: alert only and do not auto-register, which avoids most booking-site terms-of-service conflicts and fairness complaints. SMS requires prior express consent under the TCPA (general knowledge; not researched here). Check each platform's terms on automated access. Serving children's activities means collecting parent data, not data from children, but data minimization is still wise.
- Revenue estimate (**estimate only**): at a Campnab-like $10–$20 per scan or about $30 per family per season, 1,000–3,000 paying MA families gives roughly $30k–$90k/yr from alerts. Provider-side fees would add to that if private camps and swim schools list. Demand is seasonal, peaking January–March for summer camp and before each swim session.

### Gaps
- I did not verify that no MA-wide municipal rec aggregator exists. Local parent sites (Mommy Poppins Boston, Macaroni KID) list camps but were not checked for alerts.
- The platform each town uses and its terms of service were not catalogued.
- No data on how many MA families compete for municipal program spots.

## Q3. Candidate B: MA childcare openings finder and waitlist aggregator

### Takeaway
MA demand is severe: about 70% of infants live in childcare "access deserts," infant center care averages $27,000/yr, and the subsidy waitlist is about 31,000. The gap is real-time openings. Winnie already lists MA licensed providers, but the sampled Cambridge and Somerville profiles were unclaimed with no openings shown, and Mass 211 says it has no real-time openings data. The weak point is money: Winnie moved from pay-per-lead to provider SaaS, and parents' willingness to pay for alerts is unproven.

### Cited Findings
- About 59,000 MA infants (70%), 43,000 toddlers (43%) and 10,000 preschoolers (5%) live in an access desert. — [Yahoo/State House News: "70% of Mass. infants live in child care deserts"](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/70-mass-infants-live-child-135935054.html); [Governing](https://www.governing.com/management-and-administration/vast-majority-of-massachusetts-infants-live-in-areas-lacking-child-care)
- A March 2026 UMass Boston report found severe and extreme shortfalls in infant and toddler capacity. The worst areas are around Lowell, Revere/Chelsea/Winthrop and Brockton/Stoughton/Avon. — [UMass Boston](https://www.umb.edu/news/recent-news/umass-boston-report-shows-variation-in-child-care-shortfalls-across-state-/); [Hoodline, Mar 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/03/lowell-revere-and-brockton-scramble-as-child-care-seats-run-dry/)
- WBUR (Sept 4, 2026): the waitlist for financial assistance reached about 31,000 children, and a $31.2M supplemental budget will help about 2,000 more. Infant center care averages $27,000/yr. Income eligibility now goes up to 85% of state median income (about $141k for a family of four). — [WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2026/09/04/massachusetts-childcare-families-income-financial-help-waitlist)
- Mass 211 can help families find programs but has no real-time information on openings. — [Mass211](https://mass211.org/node/144)
- In coverage of the access-desert data, state officials acknowledged data gaps for families on waitlists for licensed programs. This comes from a search summary; the exact source page is uncertain. — [Yahoo/State House News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/70-mass-infants-live-child-135935054.html)
- Winnie covers MA. Sampled Cambridge and Somerville profiles show license type and capacity (for example, a home daycare licensed for 6, a center licensed for 38) but are marked "Unclaimed" and say to contact the provider for price and availability. Some Somerville-area search pages show "Openings Now Verified" tags for a few providers in other towns. — [Winnie Somerville before-school](https://winnie.com/somerville-ma/before-school-care); [Winnie: Cambridge Preschool of the Arts](https://winnie.com/place/cambridge-preschool-of-the-arts-cambridge-8)
- Winnie (2019) aggregated licensing data from 20 states with more than 150,000 providers and raised a $9M Series A. — [Business Insider NL, Oct 2019](https://www.businessinsider.nl/childcare-finder-winnie-raises-9-million-series-a-funding-2019-10/)
- Winnie's revenue model: free and paid provider listings plus promotion (Dealroom). Its pay-per-lead revenue grew 8x during COVID but "was not scalable," so it moved to monthly SaaS fees for centers (SP-Edge). Winnie Pro launched March 2022. Both are secondary sources and they partly conflict. — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/winnie/); [SP-Edge](https://sp-edge.com/companies/348464)

### Inferences
- The gap that software can fill is an openings feed. Seed it from EEC license data, then send providers automated SMS or email check-ins ("Any infant openings this month? Reply Y/N"). Sell parents alerts by age, town and start date, and offer providers a free tool to fill seats. No human is needed per customer.
- Revenue risk is the main obstacle. Proven paths are provider SaaS or featured listings (Winnie) and employer-paid benefits (Kinside; not researched in depth). A parent subscription (**estimate**: $10–$20/mo for 2–4 months of searching) is plausible given $27k/yr tuition, but I found no comparable that proves it.
- Legal: a pure information service needs no license. Avoid implying EEC endorsement, and check the terms for reusing EEC data.

### Gaps
- The EEC search tool's fields, vacancy data and open-data availability were not confirmed (the mass.gov fetch returned 403).
- No MA-specific data on how many providers update their openings anywhere, or on paid parent alert products elsewhere.
- Kinside, Wonderschool and Tootris were not researched because the search budget ran out.

## Q4. Candidate C: MA building-permit data and lead feed for contractors, solar installers, insurers and suppliers

### Takeaway
The model is proven: Shovels reportedly went from about $6K ARR (August 2023) to about $3M ARR (August 2026) selling normalized permit data at $599 and $999/mo. MA is a plausible gap because it has 351 fragmented municipalities, and Shovels names "smaller municipalities and historically weak states" as expansion targets. However, I could not verify Shovels' or BuildZoom's actual MA coverage. Boston publishes daily permit data in the public domain.

### Cited Findings
- Shovels: plans are Free (500 credits/mo), Basic $599/mo, Pro $999/mo, plus Enterprise licenses. ARR grew from about $6K (Aug 2023) to $500K (end 2024), $1M (May 2025), $1.5M (end 2025) and about $250K MRR (Aug 2026). Coverage is more than 178M permits from 2,770+ jurisdictions, about 85% of the U.S. population. It targets "rural areas, smaller municipalities, and historically weak states." All figures are Sacra estimates; there is no mention of MA. — [Sacra: Shovels](https://sacra.com/c/shovels/)
- Shovels' coverage page did not render any state data in the fetch. — [Shovels coverage](https://www.shovels.ai/coverage)
- Uses: the GTM Directory says Shovels tells you "who pulled a roofing permit last Tuesday," and its "Audiences" product builds homeowner marketing segments. — [GTM Directory](https://thegtmdirectory.com/tools/shovels/md)
- BuildZoom has MA contractor profiles with permit records and scores 139,240 MA contractors, but its full permit database is gated. — [BuildZoom profile example](https://www.buildzoom.com/contractor/evan-markham)
- Boston's Approved Building Permits dataset covers 2009 to the present across 13 permit types. It is updated daily, available as CSV through a CKAN API, and licensed PDDL (public domain). — [Analyze Boston](https://data.boston.gov/dataset/approved-building-permits)

### Inferences
- How software could run it: scrape or ingest permit portals and open data for MA towns, normalize the records, and sell (a) self-serve filtered alerts ("new roofing, solar, HVAC, pool or addition permits in my towns this week") at **estimated** $99–$299/mo, and (b) CSV/API access. **Estimate:** 50–200 subscribers × $150/mo ≈ $90k–$360k ARR. No manual work per customer, but scrapers need ongoing upkeep across vendors.
- This is a different dataset from the excluded "new-business lead lists." Using permit-holder addresses to mail homeowners may be restricted by town terms or marketing laws, so targeting contractors (B2B) is the safer path.
- Before building, check Shovels' and Construction Monitor's MA coverage town by town. If Shovels already covers the large MA towns, the gap shrinks to small towns with less value.

### Gaps
- Shovels' and BuildZoom's actual MA coverage is unverified.
- Construction Monitor, BuildFax and PermitData pricing and MA coverage were not researched.
- No census of which MA towns publish permits online, or on which platforms.

## Q5. Candidate D (conditional): Steamship Authority (Martha's Vineyard and Nantucket) cross-sailing availability alerts

### Takeaway
The demand is distinctively MA: summer car reservations are scarce and disrupted often. But the Steamship Authority already runs an automatic wait list that matches freed space and notifies by email, phone or text. A third-party service would only add value for flexible multi-date or multi-route scanning, or for travelers without a base reservation. The gap is narrow.

### Cited Findings
- The SSA wait list uses "a computerized search" that matches cancellations "on a first-come, first-served basis" up to the travel date. It notifies by email or mail when the match is more than a week out, and by email and phone inside a week. — [SSA wait list](https://www.steamshipauthority.com/reservations/wait_list)
- An older SSA FAQ (about 13 years old) says text notifications for waitlist matches require a vehicle reservation plus a profile account. — [SSA FAQ](https://www.steamshipauthority.com/about/faqs)
- The SSA car availability search page loads without a login and covers four routes; results load dynamically. — [SSA availability](https://www.steamshipauthority.com/schedules/availability)
- Disruptions: the SSA postponed its summer vehicle reservation openings in 2024 after technical problems. A 2026 Memorial Day staffing shortage cut trips and shut online booking for the day. Complaints about ferry service were "coming to a head" in 2021. — [Boston 25](https://www.boston25news.com/news/local/steamship-authority-postpones-summer-vehicle-reservation-openings/YF3JE7K3FJFF7H7YWAYI6CFANA); [WHDH](https://whdh.com/news/staff-shortages-lead-to-service-cuts-on-marthas-vineyard-ferry-over-memorial-day-weekend/); [Boston Globe 2021](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2021/08/28/metro/complaints-about-ferry-service-marthas-vineyard-nantucket-coming-head-amid-surging-tourism/)
- Comparable model: Campnab sells per-scan alerts at $10–$20. — [Campnab pricing](https://campnab.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- **Estimate:** a seasonal per-scan product ($10–$20) selling to a few thousand travelers gives roughly $20k–$60k/yr. That is small, and the official wait list limits it. It might be worth bundling with Candidate G as a "MA scarce-slot alerts" brand.
- Risk: SSA terms on automated access were not reviewed. The SSA could also improve its own wait list.

### Gaps
- SSA terms of use, and whether a wait-list request needs an existing reservation, were not confirmed.

## Q6. Candidate E (conditional): Boston "verified no-fee" rental listing alerts for the September 1 crunch

### Takeaway
The 2025 broker-fee law created confusion, and tenant-paid fees persist, which supports a "verified no-fee" alert product. But Boston rental search is crowded (Zillow, Apartments.com, BostonPads, and SpotEasy already has a no-fee filter). This counts only as a niche add-on, not a gap.

### Cited Findings
- Gov. Healey signed the law in July 2025. It requires whoever first engages the broker to pay the fee. The law took effect August 1, 2025, and applies statewide. — [Boston.com/Globe, Jun 1 2026](https://www.boston.com/real-estate/the-boston-globe/2026/06/01/massachusetts-finally-banned-broker-fees-why-are-renters-still-stuck-paying-them/); [SpotEasy guide](https://www.spoteasy.com/blog/boston-broker-fee-law-the-complete-renters-guide)
- The Globe found "dozens" of listings on Zillow, Apartments.com and Craigslist with tenant-paid fees (often one month's rent). Some brokers cite an "open listing" loophole, and advocates say enforcement is under-resourced. — [Boston.com/Globe](https://www.boston.com/real-estate/the-boston-globe/2026/06/01/massachusetts-finally-banned-broker-fees-why-are-renters-still-stuck-paying-them/)
- SpotEasy already offers a "No broker fee" filter and publishes fee-loophole content for 2026. — [SpotEasy no-fee guide](https://www.spoteasy.com/blog/no-broker-fee-apartments-in-boston-neighborhood-guide)

### Inferences
- A software-only version would scan listing text for fee language and send alerts to renters for $10–$30 per search season (**estimate**). Competition and listing-site terms of service on scraping are the main obstacles.
- MA broker licensing: listing or advertising sites like Zillow are not generally treated as brokers. Charging fees to "negotiate" rentals would likely trigger licensing. This was not verified.

### Gaps
- MA real-estate broker licensing rules for listing aggregators were not researched.
- No data on how many listings carry fees.

## Q7. Candidate F (conditional): Title 5 septic inspection directory and quote-request marketplace

### Takeaway
Title 5 is a requirement unique to MA (an inspection at most property sales, done by state-approved inspectors, with a certificate valid 2–3 years). Pricing is unregulated ($300–$800), which suits a quote-comparison service. But HomeAdvisor/Angi already has a Title 5 cost guide, and I found no data on per-lead prices. Treat this as a programmatic-SEO directory plus lead resale, with weak-to-moderate evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Title 5 inspection cost is roughly $300–$500 (several sources) or $400–$800 (HomeAdvisor). Price is not regulated. Pumping can add about $400. A certificate is valid for 2 years, or 3 with documented annual pumping. Inspectors must be state-approved, and NEIWPCC publishes a list. — [HomeAdvisor Title 5](https://cost-guide-ssr.homeadvisor.com/cost/inspectors-and-appraisers/title-5-inspection); [HomeServe](https://www.homeserve.com/blog/home-improvement/title-5-septic-inspections); [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/title-5-septic-inspection-requirements-costs-and-results/)
- Example of a town filing fee: West Boylston charges $50 to file a Title 5 report. — [West Boylston](https://www.westboylston-ma.gov/node/662)

### Inferences
- Software could build a directory from the NEIWPCC list, add town-specific rules pages, collect quote requests, and route them to inspectors who sign up and pay per lead or a monthly fee through Stripe. The inspectors are licensed; the operator is not, so no license is needed to run the site. Sign-up must be self-serve to avoid manual sales work.
- **Estimate:** revenue depends on capturing seller and buyer search traffic. With no lead-price benchmark found, revenue cannot be estimated with confidence.

### Gaps
- Angi/HomeAdvisor per-lead pricing for septic inspection, and how many MA home sales involve septic, were not found.

## Q8. Candidate G (weak): government appointment availability alerts (RMV road tests and similar)

### Takeaway
The model works at small scale elsewhere: a North Carolina DMV alert site charged $5 per alert and had about 500 paying customers (July 2025). The MA evidence of an RMV road-test backlog is mostly from 2020–2023, so current demand is unproven. Fold this into a broader "MA scarce-slot alerts" product only if fresh demand appears.

### Cited Findings
- ncdmvappointments.com, built by an anonymous Charlotte resident, scans the NC DMV scheduling site continuously and emails a $5 alert when a slot opens. It does not book appointments and reported about 500 paying customers. NC DMV had no comment. — [Yahoo/Queen City News, Jul 31 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/website-aims-help-people-beat-025404617.html)
- MA backlog evidence is dated: about 9,200 people waited for road tests after the pandemic (CBS Boston). Waits ran 2–3 months, and examiners were short (Boston 25). In 2023 the RMV advised widening location preferences during the surge driven by the Work and Family Mobility Act. — [CBS Boston](https://www.cbsnews.com/boston/news/mass-rmv-backlog-drivers-license-road-test-coronavirus/); [Boston 25](https://www.boston25news.com/news/25-investigates-drivers-license-test-appointments-unavailable/GO744BHJW5EEDPC6KPCBUESGBQ); [mass.gov RMV update 2023](https://www.mass.gov/doc/rmv-update-presented-to-the-board-on-july-22-2023/download)
- PageCrawl, a generic page monitor, already markets DMV appointment alerts for any state. — [PageCrawl](https://pagecrawl.io/tools/dmv-road-test-appointment-alerts.html)

### Inferences
- MA road-test scheduling goes through myRMV, which may require a login, so scraping could mean handling user credentials. That is a legal and security risk. **Estimate:** at NC-like volumes, revenue is a few thousand dollars a year, too small to justify on its own.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 data on MA road-test wait times, and the myRMV terms of use were not reviewed.

## Q9. How do the candidates rank, and what is the revenue logic?

### Takeaway
Ranked shortlist (my judgment, based on the cited evidence):
1. **A. Kids' activity and town-rec registration alerts plus aggregator.** Strong MA demand; proven alert and marketplace pricing; fully automatable.
2. **C. MA permit data and lead feed (B2B).** The strongest proven revenue (Shovels at about $3M ARR, $599+/mo), but the MA gap is unverified.
