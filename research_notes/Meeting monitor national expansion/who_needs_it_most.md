# Who needs early town- and township-level siting alerts most (US), ranked

Prepared 2026-10-11 for the 351 Watch owner. This extends `research_notes/Product strategy/where_we_win.md` (MA-only segments) to the US. About 60 searches and fetches this session. Constraint carried throughout: no email product and no email outreach, so every channel listed is a platform, community, venue, feed, marketplace, search surface or citation.

**Labels used on every claim**
- **[V] verified**: read on the primary source this session (the organisation's own page, filing, dataset or minutes).
- **[V-prior]**: verified on a primary source in `where_we_win.md` (Oct 10-11, 2026); the URL is repeated here.
- **[S] secondary**: trade press or an aggregator reporting someone else's data, or a search-engine summary of a primary page that blocked direct fetch (noted where that happened).
- **[E] estimate**: my judgment or arithmetic on cited figures. Scores and ranks are all [E].

**How the ranks were scored [E].** Each segment gets 1-5 on six factors:
- Pain: how often decisions hit them, times the cost of missing one (25%).
- WTP: willingness to pay, from published price anchors (25%).
- Breadth: how many places they must watch at once (15%).
- Town fit: how much of the decision happens at the town or township level rather than at the county or state (15%).
- Reach: how reachable they are without email (10%).
- Volume: the number of US buyers (10%).

No price has been tested with any buyer, so WTP is anchored, not measured.

## 1. Which segments gain most from early town/township alerts? Ranked, paying vs free

### Takeaway
Nationally, the four segments that gain most and can pay are:
1. Data-center developers and the teams around them (hyperscaler real-estate, site selectors).
2. Community-scale and distributed solar and battery (BESS) developers.
3. Utility-scale solar, wind and BESS developers (IPPs).
4. Developer-side energy and land-use law firms.

All four must watch many jurisdictions at once. A missed vote costs them millions: a data center loses about $14.2M per month of delay at 60 MW, and a cancelled solar project sinks more than $2M. They already pay $9k-$50k a year for adjacent tools.

The siting-intelligence platforms (Heatmap, Spark, Paces, Cleanview, Carina, LandGate) form a fifth paying segment, as data licensees. Today they phone counties or hand-review agendas to get this data.

Everyone else is one of two things:
- **Own-town, high-volume groups.** These are free distribution that brings in traffic, citations and crawl permission: residents and opposition groups, landowners and farmers, real-estate agents, town officials, local journalists, land trusts and pro-renewable advocates.
- **A later low-price tier.** These are land agents, town counsel, tower firms, aggregates, housing and EV.

### Cited Findings (segment cards)

#### P1. Data-center developers, hyperscaler real-estate teams, site selectors
- **Size**
  - "There are 389 unique data center developers with projects in the US"; 1,339 operating data centers (63,064 MW); 2,522 planned projects (379,260 MW), Cleanview, "Last updated: October 2026" [V] — [Cleanview](https://cleanview.co/data-centers/us). Cleanview's stated total (459,511 MW) does not equal its own components (442,324 MW) [V, same page].
  - Site Selectors Guild: 65 members in 2024, up from 43 in 2017 [S] — [BCI Global](https://www.bciglobal.com/annual-conference-site-selectors-guild-2024).
  - Data Center Coalition general membership requires steps toward ≥75 MW in North America; board seats require ≥150 MW in multiple markets [S] — [DCC join page](https://datacentercoalition.org/cpages/join).
- **Cost of a missed decision**
  - STL Partners: a typical 60 MW US data center loses "USD14.2 million of lost revenue, cost overruns and contractual penalties" per month of delay. IRR falls from 17.1% to 15.5% after one month and to 12.6% after three [S] — [STL Partners](https://stlpartners.com/press/delays-in-data-centre-construction/).
  - Heatmap Pro: "At least 20 proposed data center projects were canceled after local pushback" in Q1 2026, worth ">$41.7 billion" and "at least 3.5 gigawatts". About 25 were cancelled in 2025, and "at least $85 billion" has been cancelled over three years [V] — [Heatmap, May 6, 2026](https://heatmap.news/politics/local-opposition-data-center-cancellations).
  - Data Center Watch: "At least 75 data center projects worth approximately $130 billion were blocked or delayed in a single quarter" (Q1 2026) [V] — [Data Center Watch](https://datacenterwatch.org/q1-2026). Q2 2026: 45 projects, $68B [S, aggregator only] — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/data-center-watch-45-us-ai-data-center-projects-worth-68b-blocked-or-delayed-in).
  - More than 530 counties and municipalities have restricted or banned data-center construction (Heatmap Pro data, Aug 2026) [V] — [Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/daily/data-center-opposition-poll-collapse).
  - Developers withdraw after local zoning changes. Example: a Wake County, NC developer pulled its rezoning applications [S] — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2026/06/16/data-center-opposition-construction-delays-blocks-report/).
- **What they pay now**
  - Cleanview data-center platform $9,000/yr (5 users), API +$5,000/yr [V-prior] — [Cleanview pricing](https://cleanview.co/pricing).
  - Heatmap Pro and Paces: demo only, no published price [V-prior] — [Heatmap Pro](https://heatmap.news/pro), [Paces](https://www.paces.com/).
  - Hamlet's first paying customers were real-estate and data-center developers [V-prior] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3073247).
- **WTP and job-posting evidence**
  - AWS postings describe teams that "identify and successfully navigate the required real estate entitlements" and ask for "5+ years experience in site due diligence... entitlement agreements" [S] — [amazon.jobs](https://amazon.jobs/jobs/2823735).
  - A data-center development director role at 1st Avenue Power oversees "entitlement processes, zoning approvals, and permitting requirements" [S] — [Greenhouse](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/1stavenuepower/jobs/4902973008).
  - A CloudHQ development manager tracks "industry-relevant developments for target markets" [S] (same search; posting not opened).
- **Topics and regions**
  - Moratoria, use-table definitions, rezonings, host-community agreements, noise and water ordinances [E].
  - Regions: NJ had the most data-center measures (119, Savrn) [V-prior] — [where_we_win.md §4](../Product%20strategy/where_we_win.md); 53 MA towns had data-center agenda items in the Oct 10 scan [V-prior, own data].
  - 14 states saw statewide moratorium proposals in early 2026 [V] — [Data Center Watch](https://datacenterwatch.org/q1-2026).
- **Demand signal from the public**: 75% of registered voters would oppose a data center near them (Heatmap Pro/Embold, Aug 8–13, 2026, n=2,045, ±2.3) [V] — [Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/daily/data-center-opposition-poll-collapse). 71% in May 2026 (n=4,118) [V] — [Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/politics/americans-oppose-data-centers-poll). Gallup: 71% (Mar 2026) [S] — [KuCoin summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/71-of-americans-oppose-local-ai-data-center-construction-gallup-poll-reveals).

## 2. How often does local permitting or opposition delay or cancel projects, and what does it cost?

### Takeaway
Local decisions are a leading cause of failure, not an edge case:
- **Renewables:** about a third of recent wind and solar siting applications were cancelled and about half significantly delayed. Local ordinances and zoning are the top cited cause. Opposition adds 11–14 months, and a cancelled project sinks $2M (solar) to $7.5M (wind).
- **Restrictions on the books:** Sabin now counts 865 local restrictions.
- **Data centers:** cancellations and delays are running at $40B–$130B a quarter, and one month of delay on a 60 MW site costs about $14M.

### Cited Findings
**LBNL developer survey (Jan 2024; 123 respondents, 62 companies)**
- About one-third of wind and solar siting applications submitted in the past five years were cancelled. About half were "delayed significantly." Top causes, in order: local ordinances/zoning, interconnection, community opposition [S] — [Latitude Media, Jan 29, 2024](https://latitudemedia.com/news/heres-whats-holding-back-solar-and-wind-developers).
- Community opposition adds about 11 months (solar) and 14 months (wind). 83% of solar and 87% of wind developers say opposition is a bigger problem than five years ago [S] — [Latitude Media](https://latitudemedia.com/news/heres-whats-holding-back-solar-and-wind-developers).
- Average sunk cost per cancelled project: more than $2M (solar) and $7.5M (wind). About half of projects are delayed 6+ months [S, search-engine summary of the LBNL release; direct fetch blocked by Cloudflare] — [LBNL](https://emp.lbl.gov/news/larg-e-scale-wind-and-solar).
- A follow-up LBNL study (Sept 2024): at least 30% of projects cancelled. Developers spend under 0.1% of capex on community engagement, under a third of what they spend on site control [S] — [Utility Dive](https://utilitydive.com/news/community-engagement-locals-renewable-energy-developers-berkeley-survey/726042); report: [LBNL "Halfway up the ladder"](https://emp.lbl.gov/publications/halfway-ladder-developer-practices).

**LBNL Queued Up (2025 edition, data to end-2024)**
- Only 13% of capacity that requested interconnection in 2000–2019 reached commercial operation; 77% withdrew. The median time from request to operation is now over 4 years [S, search summary of the PDF] — [Queued Up 2025](https://emp.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2025-12/Queued%20Up%202025%20Edition%20-%2012.15.2025.pdf).
- A 2026 edition (data to end-2025) exists [S] — [LBNL 2026](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2026-06/queued_up_2026_edition.pdf). Its figures were not extracted (gap).

**Sabin Center (Sept 2026, 6th edition)**
- 567 contested projects since 2004 (322 solar, 217 wind, 41 BESS, 17 transmission). At least 227 (~40%) were cancelled.
- 2025 cancellations: 22 projects, ~9.8 GW, the most in any year (the prior high was 3.26 GW in 2024). The largest was the 6.2 GW Esmeralda 7 project in Nevada, cancelled by BLM, so this is not purely local.
- Restrictions: 865 local plus 23 state actions, 888 in all. New restrictions per year: 165 (2023), 112 (2024), 70 (2025), so the pace is slowing. Ohio: 30 of 88 counties have bans.

  [S] — [pv magazine, Sept 8, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/09/08/the-u-s-saw-a-14-percent-increase-in-contested-renewable-projects-9-8-gw-in-cancellations-in-2025/); Sabin blog: [Columbia](https://blogs.law.columbia.edu/climatechange/2026/09/03/widespread-local-opposition-often-driven-by-misinformation-continues-to-hamper-renewable-energy-development/).
- The prior (June 2025) edition counted 459 localities in 44 states with severe restrictions, up 16% from 395 [S] — [Sabin 2025](https://climate.law.columbia.edu/content/opposition-renewable-energy-facilities-united-states-june-2025-edition).
- **Methods differ across editions.** The 2026 "865 local restrictions" and the 2025 "459 localities" are not the same unit (restrictions vs localities) [E].

**Heatmap**
- Wind and solar are "heavily restricted or outright banned in about one in five counties." Separate rules restrict renewables in 605 counties. The count has "nearly doubled since 2022" [V] — [Heatmap, Jul 7, 2025](https://heatmap.news/politics/laws-restricting-renewable-energy).
- Heatmap Pro analysed 8,500+ projects: a county's 2008→2016 swing is among the biggest predictors of contest [S] — [Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/politics/obama-trump-shift).
- Data centers: see P1 (≥20 cancellations in Q1 2026, >$41.7B, 3.5 GW; >530 restricting localities; 75% voter opposition) [V].

**MIT research (the "MIT" half of the brief)**
- No joint Heatmap–MIT poll was found. Heatmap's polls are fielded by Embold Research [V] — [Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/politics/americans-oppose-data-centers-poll).
- MIT's Renewable Energy Clinic (Susskind et al., 2022) studied 53 delayed or blocked wind, solar and geothermal projects in 28 states (2008–2021): 49% were cancelled permanently, ~4,600 MW of potential capacity lost [S] — [MIT Renewable Energy Clinic](https://renewable-energy.mit.edu/research).

**BESS**: 174 jurisdictions in 16 states [V]; 150+ in 17 states in March 2026 [S]. PNNL counted 37 moratoria in 12 states in effect as of July 2024 [S] — [PNNL](https://www.pnnl.gov/sites/default/files/media/file/Supporting%20Energy%20Storage%20in%20Siting%20Proceedings.pdf). The growth is fast, roughly 37 → 174 in about two years, though the methods differ [E].

**Data centers**: Data Center Watch Q1 2026: 75 projects, ~$130B blocked or delayed [V]; STL: $14.2M per month of delay at 60 MW [S]; CBRE's national average wholesale asking lease of $194.95/kW/month (2025) appeared in a search summary [S, attribution uncertain; it may come from the ArchiLabs or Archdesk post, so confirm before use] — [ArchiLabs](https://archilabs.ai/posts/tiny-schedule-slips-cost-millions-in-ai-data-centers).

**Housing**: 10–34 months of approval time across CA cities; 523 days in SF [S] (see P7).

### Inferences
- **The value of an early alert is time, not the project itself [E].** If an alert moves discovery of a hostile article from the vote (or weeks after it, per Carina) to the agenda posting 3–14 days earlier, the developer can do three things:
  1. File before the moratorium takes effect, where state vesting rules allow.
  2. Show up and testify.
  3. Pull spend early.
- **Worked example [E].** At a $2M sunk cost per cancelled solar project and a one-third cancellation rate, one avoided late discovery per year is worth more than any price in §4. This is arithmetic on [S] figures, not a measured effect.
- **Restrictions are slowing but disputes are not [E].** New renewable restrictions are slowing (165 → 112 → 70 a year), while BESS and data-center restrictions are rising fast. The growth market for alerts is therefore BESS and data centers, not legacy solar and wind setbacks.

### Gaps
- No study measures how often late *discovery* (as opposed to the decision itself) causes the loss. That is the direct claim 351 Watch would make, and it needs customer evidence.
- The LBNL sunk-cost and "6+ months" figures could not be read on the primary page (Cloudflare block). They are consistent across secondary summaries but should be re-checked from the PDF.
- Queued Up 2026 figures were not extracted.

**Labour cost of doing it by hand.** Transect's calculator assumes a $150,000/yr fully loaded employee (2,000 hours) [S] — [Transect calculator](https://www.transect.com/resources/cost-savings-calculator). ### Inferences
- **Price anchors [E].**
  - Small developers, law firms and consultants anchor to Docket Alarm, Zoneomics and Shovels ($100–1,000/mo).
  - Data-center and IPP teams anchor to Cleanview and Quorum ($9k–$25k/yr).
  - The $349/mo Siting Desk and $499/mo data-center feed proposed in `where_we_win.md` sit inside both bands.
- **Being the only published price is a channel in itself [E].** No siting-intelligence rival publishes a price. That makes 351 Watch's price page findable on G2, Capterra, Vendr and search, which buyers already use for Quorum and FiscalNote.
- **The real competitor for small developers is free [E].** Carina's public BESS map and NLR's datasets are free, so the paid line has to be speed (pre-meeting), town grain and delivery (Slack, iCal, webhooks).

### Gaps
- Prices could not be found for Heatmap Pro, Paces, Spark, Kite, LandGate PowerTools, Transect, Carina's subscription, Hamlet or Enverus's power products, despite searches.
- G2 and Capterra had no reviews for Paces or LandGate.
- No public procurement record was found for any siting platform. Searching state and utility procurement portals could fill this.

## 5. Which segments want many places at once (high value) vs only their own town (low value, high volume)?

### Takeaway
High-value "many places" buyers (portfolio watchers) are:
- data-center, BESS and solar developers;
- siting platforms;
- energy law firms and consultants;
- investors and utilities;
- tower and aggregates firms.

"Own town" users are residents, opposition groups, landowners, realtors, town officials, local reporters and land trusts. They should get free single-town watchlists that feed search traffic, sharing and citations into the paid tiers.

### Cited Findings
- **Portfolio scale for developers.** BESS restrictions sit in 174 jurisdictions across 16 states [V] — [Carina](https://carina.energy/bess-moratoriums/). A single developer's queue position can span several ISOs; there are 2,824 BESS projects across 5 ISOs [S] — [US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/battery-storage-interconnection-queue).
- **Data centers.** 2,522 planned facilities across 389 developers [V] — [Cleanview](https://cleanview.co/data-centers/us), about 6.5 planned sites per developer on average [E, arithmetic].
- **Free per-town alerts already exist** (CivicPlus Notify Me: one subscription per board per town, no keyword filter) [V-prior] — [Notify Me example](https://pa-southfayette.civicplus.com/notifyme). Single-town users are therefore the least underserved group.
- **Opposition groups organise per project and per town.** Data Center Watch says "communities have internalized an opposition playbook" [V] — [Data Center Watch](https://datacenterwatch.org/q1-2026).

| Watches many places (high value) | Watches a region or a few towns (medium) | Watches own town (low value, high volume) |
|---|---|---|
| Data-center developers and site selectors; hyperscaler RE teams; BESS/community-solar developers; utility-scale IPPs; siting platforms (licensees); energy law firms; tax equity, lenders, insurers (portfolio diligence); tower companies; researchers (national datasets) | Engineering consultants; utilities (service territory); aggregates companies; land agents; regional planning agencies; advocacy groups (state); town counsel (several client towns); homebuilders (metro) | Residents and opposition groups; landowners and farmers; realtors and buyers; select boards and planning boards; local reporters; land trusts |

### Inferences
- **Pricing should scale with the number of places watched, not with seats [E].** Single-town use stays free forever, which is the distribution engine. The paywall starts at more than 5 towns, company watch or Slack, as in `where_we_win.md`.
- **The middle column decides growth [E].** Consultants and town counsel each serve many developers or towns, so a free collaborator seat exposes the product to many paying firms.

### Gaps
- No data on how many jurisdictions a typical developer watches. Five user interviews, booked through site sign-ups, would answer this.

## 6. Channels that reach each segment without email

### Takeaway
Ranked by expected yield [E]:
1. **Search** (town × topic pages and company/LLC pages).
2. **Platform presence the buyer already uses:** a data licence to Heatmap, Spark, Paces, Cleanview, LandGate or Carina; G2, Capterra and Vendr listings; an API and agent endpoint.
3. **Public data citations:** Sabin, NLR, Heatmap, trade press and journalists.
4. **Slack/Teams permalinks** that spread inside developer–consultant–counsel channels.
5. **Association venues and resource pages:** RE+, CCSA, Clean Grid Alliance, state SEIA chapters, AAPL Terrawatt, IMLA, APA, NSSGA, NAHB.

### Cited Findings
- **Conferences**
  - RE+ 2025 drew ~37,000 attendees and 1,325 exhibitors (Las Vegas) [S] — [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2025/09/re-2025-clocks-37k-attendees-over-1000-exhibitors/).
  - The IMLA annual conference drew ~700 in 2014 [S] — [Daily Record](https://thedailyrecord.com/2014/09/11/imla-conference-brings-700-municipal-lawyers-to-city/).
  - The Site Selectors Guild holds an annual conference [S] — [BCI Global](https://www.bciglobal.com/annual-conference-site-selectors-guild-2024).

  - AAPL's 43+ local associations and Terrawatt Institute [S] — [landman.org](https://www.landman.org/product/terrawatt-institute-education-supporter-sponsor-2027.html).
  - NAHB's 700+ local associations [S]; NSSGA [S]; ACEC [S]; APA [S].

### Inferences
- **The most efficient single channel is the platform licence [E].** One agreement with a platform such as Heatmap or Cleanview puts 351 Watch data, with attribution, in front of the P1–P3 buyers who already pay those platforms. - **Free segments are the citation layer [E].** Every journalist or researcher who cites a town page creates the public evidence that the paid segments trust and search for.
