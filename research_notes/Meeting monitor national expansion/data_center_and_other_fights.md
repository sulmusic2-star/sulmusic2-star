# Data-center and other local land-use fights: where US local decisions are surging and the stakes are highest (2025–2027)

Prepared 2026-10-11 for the 351 Watch national-expansion report. Scope: US only; subjects other than solar and BESS, led by data centers. Builds on `Product strategy/where_we_win.md` and `Meeting monitor deep dive/expansion_markets.md`, which already hold the Pew planned-data-center counts, the Savrn/Electric Choice state counts, the Sabin renewable counts and the dcmap "0 MA" finding. This note does not repeat them except where a number is needed for a ranking.

**Labels used on every claim**
- **[V] verified:** read this session on the primary source (the organization's own report, tracker page, filing or government page). A tracker's page is primary for *its own* counts; the local events it lists are secondary unless a county record was read.
- **[S] secondary:** news report, aggregator, other tracker, or a search-engine summary of a page that was not opened (marked "search summary").
- **[E] estimate:** my arithmetic on cited figures, or judgment.

Method: about 30 web searches and 16 page fetches on 2026-10-11. No accounts created. Paywalled pages (Bloomberg, Data Center Watch in-depth reports, Heatmap Pro) were not read beyond what is public.

## Q1. Where have local data-center moratoria, rezonings and denials happened in 2025–2026, county by county?

### Takeaway
Local data-center actions have moved from a Northern Virginia story to a national wave. By October 2026 the trackers count anywhere from 47 (dcmap, formal local actions only) to 530+ severe restrictions (Heatmap Pro) to 1,233 local measures (Savrn, a floor). The densest 2026 activity is in Michigan townships, Georgia counties, North Carolina, Indiana, Ohio townships, Maryland, Kentucky and Pennsylvania townships. Virginia still holds the largest single dollar stakes, but it is no longer where most decisions happen.

### Cited Findings

**Scale of local action (tracker counts; definitions differ)**
- dcmap.us lists 70 documented data-center policy actions in 27 states: 23 statewide and 47 local. The 47 local actions are 30 moratoriums, 11 ordinances and 6 rejections; 27 are active pauses. dcmap says "coverage favors documented state and larger local actions; smaller local pauses can go unreported" [V] — [dcmap policy tracker](https://dcmap.us/insights/policy/)
- Savrn tracks 1,233 local (city, county, township, village) measures with 1,397 primary sources, re-verified weekly. It calls the local count "a floor, not a census." Last change 2026-10-11, with 19 entries added in the prior 7 days [V] — [Savrn tracker](https://savrn.com/data-center-moratorium-tracker)
- Savrn state counts (state and local combined): NJ 119, OH 118, MI 104, GA 71, NC 68, CA 65, PA 61, WI 58, FL 50, KY 47, VA 44, IN 43, MD 43, TN 42, NY 41, MN 40, TX 37, IA 36, IL 32, KS 30, NE 30, WA 30, ME 29, SC 26, CO 25, MO 24, MA 22 [V] — [Savrn tracker](https://savrn.com/data-center-moratorium-tracker). ## Q2. How much data-center investment has been blocked or delayed by local action?

### Inferences
- [E] **LocalGovIndex changes the competitive picture in `where_we_win.md`.** It is agenda- and minutes-sourced, prices self-serve ($399/mo Professional, close to the proposed $349 "Siting Desk") and already sends pre-meeting agenda summaries. Its national 2,823-government footprint against about 38,700 general-purpose governments (Census, prior note) leaves townships and small towns thin. 351 Watch's differentiation must be completeness within a state, topic classification, lead time and the decision lifecycle, not "we read agendas."
- [E] Trackers sort into three groups:
  1. free, news-compiled lists (dcmap, Savrn, Brockovich, Electric Choice, datacenterbans);
  2. paid project or risk platforms (Heatmap Pro, Cleanview, DCW private lists);
  3. agenda-grain monitors (LocalGovIndex, Citizen Portal, plus the enterprise MultiState / Quorum / FiscalNote from the prior note).
  
  Only group 3 sees items *before* the vote, and none publishes township-complete state coverage.
- [E] Price anchors for a data-center local-action feed now run from $49–399/mo (LocalGovIndex) to $9k/yr (Cleanview) to unpublished enterprise (Heatmap Pro). That supports the $499/mo feed proposed in `where_we_win.md`, but only with visible completeness proof.

## Q5. For the other subjects, which ones have the biggest stakes per local decision and the least existing tracking?

### Takeaway
After data centers, the highest stakes per decision with the least local tracking are:
1. **on-site and dedicated gas plants for data centers** (1–4.5 GW campuses, decided partly through township rezonings);
2. **transmission lines and substations** ($1B+ lines, where counties mostly intervene rather than decide);
3. **warehouses and industrial logistics**, now including data-center supply-chain buildings and ICE detention conversions. Volume is high and nobody tracks it locally.

Quarries and CAFOs are frequent, intensely contested and untracked, but carry smaller dollar stakes and weaker buyers. Housing and zoning reform has the most decisions and is the best tracked. CO2 pipelines are fading after South Dakota's 2025 eminent-domain ban. Crypto mining now mostly rides inside data-center moratoria.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- [E] **Biggest stakes per decision with the least tracking:**
  1. gas or on-site generation for data centers;
  2. transmission and substations;
  3. data-center tax deals, annexations and referendums (Port Washington TIF, Augusta Twp, Monterey Park, Sabey $240M+, El Paso $800M). These are data-center decisions that the moratorium trackers do not count.
- [E] **Best "volume × untracked" add-ons** for an agenda reader are warehouses (hundreds of projects a year, often in the same industrial-zone amendments as data centers, for example Falls Twp) and quarries (rising with data-center construction demand). Both appear on the same planning-board agendas 351 Watch already reads.
- [E] **Weak commercial fits:** CAFOs and CO2 pipelines are fought hard locally, but decided mostly at state level with low-WTP buyers. Crypto is best handled as a tag inside data centers. Housing is high-volume but well tracked; it suits 351 Watch only where a 40B-style appeal regime exists (prior note).
- [E] **ICE detention warehouses** are high-salience council agenda items in 2026 but locally preempted. They are a journalist and advocacy distribution hook, not a paid product line.

## Q6. Ranking: (a) subject matters and (b) sub-state areas for 2025–2027

### Takeaway
(a) Data centers (including their tax, annexation and power decisions) rank first by a wide margin, then on-site gas generation and transmission tied to them, then warehouses and quarries.

(b) The best sub-state targets combine heavy 2026 data-center activity with **township or town decision-making** and thin tracking:
1. **Southeast Michigan townships**;
2. **Pennsylvania township belts**;
3. **Ohio's SW townships**;
4. **SE Wisconsin and Dane County**;
5. **Twin Cities south metro**.

County-grain hot zones with bigger dollars but more competition follow: **Georgia's metro ring**, **NC Triangle and Piedmont**, **Kentucky Bluegrass**, **central Indiana**, **Maryland**, **Northern Virginia** and **Texas exurbs**. Together they span five regions: Great Lakes/Midwest, Mid-Atlantic, Southeast, Texas/Southwest and Northeast.
