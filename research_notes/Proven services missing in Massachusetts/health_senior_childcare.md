# Health, Senior Care, Childcare and Family-Support Services Missing in Massachusetts (Owner-Operator, Non-Clinician Model)

Research date: 2026-10-09. Method: web search plus page fetches. Several mass.gov PDFs returned HTTP 403 and could not be read in full. Where a figure comes only from a search-result summary or a secondary or aggregator site, it is labeled that way. "Inferences" are my reasoning and are not verified facts.

## Childcare: How bad are MA child care deserts, what is the status of C3, how does licensing work, and which models are proven?

### Takeaway
Massachusetts is a national leader on overall child care supply but has a severe shortage of **infant and toddler** care. State data show 70% of infants and 43% of toddlers live in "access deserts." C3 operating grants have been flat at $475M for three straight budgets (FY25–FY27), and about 600 newer programs have not been able to get C3. So a new entrant should not count on C3 revenue. The model that best fits an owner who does not deliver care is a center (large group license) or a network of family child care (FCC) homes. Corporate backup care (Bright Horizons, headquartered in MA) is growing about 19% a year.

### Cited Findings
**Shortage and desert data**
- At the second meeting of the state's Data Advisory Commission on Early Education and Care (reported Aug 6, 2025), state data showed about 59,000 infants (70%), 43,000 toddlers (43%) and 10,000 preschoolers (5%) living in a child care "access desert." The state defines that as an area with 3 or more children per licensed slot. Some regions, especially central MA, have more than 10 children per slot. Enrollment is most available to the highest-income families. — [CommonWealth Beacon, Aug 6 2025](https://commonwealthbeacon.org/newsletter/dl-08-06-25/)
- Coverage of the same data said the system added about 17,000 seats over the prior two years, but infant and toddler care still has the fewest seats (from a search-result summary). — [CommonWealth Beacon](https://commonwealthbeacon.org/newsletter/dl-08-06-25/); [Yahoo syndication](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/70-mass-infants-live-child-135935054.html)
- CAP's national analysis (2025) estimated that about 21% of MA children under 6 live in a *licensed* child care desert. That makes MA one of the four lowest jurisdictions, alongside DC, NJ and NE. Nationally the figure is 46%. CAP measures all children under 6, so its number is not comparable to the state's infant-specific figure. — [Center for American Progress](https://www.americanprogress.org/article/americas-licensed-child-care-deserts/); [CommonWealth Beacon coverage](https://commonwealthbeacon.org/education/massachusetts-emerges-as-a-national-leader-in-child-care-access-but-accessibility-issues-remain/)

**Prices (the demand signal for private pay)**
- Median annual price of center-based infant care is over $26,000 in Middlesex and Norfolk counties. Only Arlington County, VA and San Francisco ranked higher nationally, and all 14 MA counties were in the national top 100 (DOL data analyzed by the Boston Globe). — [NBC Boston](https://nbcboston.com/news/local/2-mass-counties-rank-among-the-most-expensive-for-infant-care/2959511)
- Infant center care costs 14.9% of median income for married couples in MA, ranking 44th (2025 data). — [America's Health Rankings](https://www.americashealthrankings.org/explore/measures/Infant_Child_Care/MA)
- A secondary site citing 2026 federal ACF data puts MA second among states for infant center care at about $2,200/month. It links this to the mandatory 1:3 infant ratio. This is unverified secondary data. — [DaycareCalc](https://daycarecalc.com/most-expensive-states)

**Center economics and the cost-versus-rate gap**
- EEC's cost study (Northern Illinois University CELFE) estimated Metro Boston infant care at about $136.55/day in cost versus a $97.00 FY2023 subsidy rate. In all but 3 scenarios, cost exceeded reimbursement. — [Harvard Zaentz Navigator, MA Cost Estimation Model](https://zaentznavigator.gse.harvard.edu/policy-strategies/massachusetts-cost-estimation-model/)
- The FY2024 rate reform cut the reimbursement regions from 6 to 3 and raised center rates to at least 81% of the modeled cost of care (from a search-result summary). — [Harvard Zaentz Navigator](https://zaentznavigator.gse.harvard.edu/policy-strategies/massachusetts-cost-estimation-model/); [mass.gov press release](https://www.mass.gov/news/healey-driscoll-administration-proposes-transformative-new-rates-for-child-care-providers)
- AIR's updated Phase 1 cost model (March 2025) found Metro Boston about 18% more expensive than the Western region for children under 5 in centers. Yet Metro Boston reimbursement rates were up to 52% higher, so the West is relatively underpaid (from a search-result summary). — [AIR, MA EEC Phase 1 Cost Model, March 2025](https://www.air.org/sites/default/files/2025-05/MA-EEC-Phase1-Cost-Model-March-2025.pdf)

**C3 (Commonwealth Cares for Children) status, 2025–2026**
- FY26: C3 funded at $475M, from general appropriations plus Fair Share surtax revenue. Center-based providers must spend 50% of C3 on workforce (this does not apply to FCC). Recipients must accept subsidy vouchers or offer equivalent care. Nearly 8,000 licensed programs receive C3. — [mass.gov C3 page](https://www.mass.gov/service-details/commonwealth-cares-for-children-child-care-stabilization-grants); [Early Success / A Lever for Scale](https://earlysuccess.org/massachusetts)
- FY27: The General Appropriations Act, signed July 9, 2026, level-funds C3 at $475M (0.0% change). This is the third straight year at $475M. MassBudget notes that about 600 new programs have not had access to C3 because funding is limited. — [MassBudget FY2027 GAA analysis, Jul 21 2026](https://massbudget.org/2026/07/21/massbudget-in-depth-analysis-of-fy-2027-gaa/); [Eye on Early Childhood FY27 wrap-up](https://eyeonearlychildhood.org/2026/07/14/fy27-massachusetts-state-budget-wrap-up/) (this page would not render on fetch, so the figures come from a search summary)
- Total FY27 early education and care funding was about $1.87B (from a search summary of the wrap-up). — [Eye on Early Childhood](https://eyeonearlychildhood.org/2026/07/14/fy27-massachusetts-state-budget-wrap-up/)
- EEC published a C3 data brief in August 2026 on supply trends, using surveys from May and August 2025. It returned HTTP 403 and could not be read. — [mass.gov C3 Aug 2026 data brief](https://www.mass.gov/doc/commonwealth-cares-for-children-c3-august-2026-data-brief/download)

**Family child care (FCC) supply**
- As of May 2024, MA had over 5,400 licensed FCCs with 43,077 licensed slots, about 18% of licensed capacity. In FY2023, 42% of FCC providers employed an assistant, which raises a home's licensed capacity. — [MassBudget testimony to EEC Task Force, Aug 2024](https://massbudget.org/2024/08/05/testimony-eec-task-force/)

**Licensing (EEC, 606 CMR 7.00)**
- Centers apply for a large group and school-age license through EEC's LEAD system, starting by contacting the regional EEC office. A licensor then schedules a licensing visit. — [mass.gov, Apply for a Large Group and School Age license](https://www.mass.gov/how-to/apply-for-a-large-group-and-school-age-child-care-license)
- Every prospective licensee must attend a Potential Provider meeting and needs a Professional Qualifications number. Fingerprint background checks are renewed every 3 years. FCC applicants planning to care for 7 or more children need extra pre-service training under 606 CMR 7.09(15)(b). Source is a third-party guide. — [Playground guide](https://www.tryplayground.com/blog/child-care-licensing-requirements-massachusetts); [606 CMR 7.00 on Justia](https://regulations.justia.com/states/massachusetts/606-cmr/title-606-cmr-7-00/section-7-03)
- Large FCC capacity: a third-party page lists 9–12 children with a provider plus an assistant, and "up to 12–14 with assistant (verify with MA EEC)." This is unconfirmed and conflicts with other summaries. — [Brightwheel](https://mybrightwheel.com/licensing-requirements/massachusetts/massachusetts-large-family-child-care-home-licensing-requirements/)
- Fraud and oversight risk in the FCC sector: Boston 25 found some FCC providers operating despite red flags in their records. — [Boston 25 News](https://www.boston25news.com/news/local/25-investigates-some-family-childcare-providers-operating-despite-red-flags-records-show/KRNRXXGY45BBDF7OQNNWDL3LLU)

**Proven models elsewhere and in MA**
- Bright Horizons (headquartered in MA) grew backup care revenue by $117.9M (19%) in 2025, to more than $725M. Growth came from higher use of center-based, in-home and school-age backup care. — [Bright Horizons FY2025 annual report (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001437578/000119312526162618/d98247dars.pdf); [Q4/FY2025 earnings release, Feb 12 2026](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260212007443/en/Bright-Horizons-Family-Solutions-Reports-Financial-Results-for-the-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-of-2025)

### Inferences
- The gap is in **infant and toddler seats**, not preschool. With preschool deserts at only 5%, a preschool-heavy center would compete with existing supply and public pre-K. An infant/toddler-heavy center or FCC network targets the 70%/43% gap but runs at the costliest ratios (the 1:3 infant ratio is from secondary data).
- Because C3 is flat and about 600 programs are already shut out, a 2027 start-up should model **zero C3 revenue** and treat any grant as upside. Subsidy rates in Metro Boston historically ran below cost, so a private-pay or employer-contract mix is safer than a voucher-heavy one.
- Ways an owner can stay out of case work: (a) a licensed center with a qualified director (EEC requires director qualifications; exact rules not verified here); (b) a staffed FCC network or "hub," modeled on Wonderschool, where the owner supplies business services, enrollment and marketing to licensed FCC educators; (c) employer-sponsored or backup care contracts with MA employers, the Bright Horizons model at local scale.
- Central MA (more than 10 children per slot in some areas) and Western MA (underpaid relative to cost) are the most documented target geographies.

### Gaps
- No verified MA center startup cost or operating margin was found. The AIR Phase 1 report has per-child daily cost exhibits, but the PDF was read only through a summary.
- Official FCC capacity rules (small vs. large FCC, assistant ratios) were not confirmed from the 606 CMR 7.00 text.
- EEC waitlist counts for the income-eligible subsidy were not retrieved.
- No independent MA data found on Wonderschool-type FCC networks or employer-sponsored care uptake. The Aug 2026 C3 data brief (supply trends, possible FCC closure data) could not be accessed.
- No current EEC director-qualification rules confirmed. The owner can be a non-educator only if a qualified director is employed; this needs verification.

## Senior care: What is the status of MA home care agency licensure, how fast is demand growing, and what are the economics of home care, adult day health, assisted living, move management and care management?

### Takeaway
**This is the biggest regulatory change in scope.** Massachusetts enacted its first licensure system for private-pay, non-medical home care agencies: H.5627, signed Aug 6, 2026 as Chapter 180 of the Acts of 2026. EOHHS will license agencies and review the suitability of every owner with at least 5%. Temporary licenses are due within 180 days of the effective date and final regulations within about a year, so mid-to-late 2027. The law raises the bar and should push out fly-by-night operators. Owners are explicitly allowed to be non-clinicians as long as they pass suitability and financial-capacity review. Demand is rising: there are 172k residents aged 85+ now, projected to reach 275k by 2040, while 34 nursing homes have closed since 2020. The proven model is an owner-run, private-pay agency, often a franchise. Aggregator data put average franchise revenue at about $1.3M–$2.75M per unit.

### Cited Findings
**New home care licensure law (changed in 2026)**
- The Legislature passed H.5627, "An Act to improve Massachusetts home care," on July 31, 2026. EOHHS runs licensure and can approve licenses, survey, investigate and fine, working with the Executive Office of Aging & Independence and DPH. — [MA Legislature press release](https://malegislature.gov/PressRoom/Detail?pressReleaseId=1473)
- Requirements:
  - Workers' compensation and liability insurance
  - Equipment and supplies for workers
  - Annual training and competency on confidentiality, infection control, emergencies and fall prevention, abuse/neglect/theft reporting, and Alzheimer's and dementia care
  - Background checks, plus driving-record checks for workers who transport clients
  - **Suitability review of all applicants, including anyone with at least 5% ownership**, and proof of **sufficient financial capacity**
  - Service plans and contracts that state the total cost of care
  - A public list of licensed agencies; unlicensed entities may not advertise as home care agencies
  — [MA Legislature press release](https://malegislature.gov/PressRoom/Detail?pressReleaseId=1473)
- Governor Healey signed it on Aug 6, 2026 as Chapter 180 of the Acts of 2026, after 157-0 House and 40-0 Senate votes. As of late August 2026, no regulations had been written and no licenses issued. About 400 home care companies operate in MA, and only about one-third (those billing Medicare or MassHealth) answer to a regulator. Source is a private-pay agency's blog, so it is partly promotional. — [Care Remedy, Aug 2026](https://www.careremedy.com/massachusetts-to-start-licensing-home-care-agencies-just-not-yet-and-that-gap-is-worth-understanding-before-you-hire)
- EOHHS must issue temporary licensure regulations and temporary licenses within 180 days of the effective date, and final regulations within one year (from a search summary). — [CarePolicy.us](https://carepolicy.us/blogs/blog/massachusetts-enacts-first-statewide-licensing-system-for-home-care-agencies)
- The Home Care Alliance of MA (CEO Jake Krilovich) praised the law. Illinois passed a similar licensure update, and Illinois has long licensed non-medical home care, which makes it a proven comparison state. — [McKnight's Home Care](https://www.mcknightshomecare.com/news/providers-praise-new-home-care-licensure-laws-in-massachusetts-illinois/)
- Background: a separate 2017 law (FY2018 budget) already required DPH licensure of *home health* (medical) agencies, with fines up to $10,000/day for operating unlicensed. — [Hinckley Allen](https://www.hinckleyallen.com/publications/massachusetts-fy2018-budget-ushers-in-new-requirement-of-licensure-for-home-health-agencies-in-the-state/)
- Source conflict: a Vitable Health page from mid-2026 said MA still had no licensure. It predates the signing. — [Vitable Health](https://www.vitablehealth.com/home-care-compliance/massachusetts)

**Demand**
- 85+ population: 172,034 now, projected 274,527 in 2040 and 325,148 in 2050 (UMass Donahue estimates). — [Dorchester Reporter / State House News, Aug 20 2025](https://www.dotnews.com/2025/08/20/care-infrastructure-under-pressure-state-s-oldest-population-explodes/)
- Long-term care facilities:
  - 34 nursing facilities closed since 2020, a loss of 3,515 beds
  - Current inventory: 348 nursing facilities (39,899 beds), 273 assisted living residences (19,093 beds), 58 rest homes (2,002 beds)
  - Nursing homes reported a median total margin of -1.6% in 2023
  - Nursing homes need more than 5,000 direct care workers
  - A skilled nursing bed deficit is projected by 2035
  - Alzheimer's prevalence is expected to rise 25%
  — [Dorchester Reporter / LTC Task Force](https://www.dotnews.com/2025/08/20/care-infrastructure-under-pressure-state-s-oldest-population-explodes/)
- AARP cites about 1.4M family caregivers and nearly 1.8M older adults in MA who could benefit from long-term care services. — [MA Legislature press release](https://malegislature.gov/PressRoom/Detail?pressReleaseId=1473)
- 65+ share projected to rise from 14% (2010) to 21% (2030), an increase of 548,699 people. This is an older projection. — [UMass Donahue Institute](https://donahue.umass.edu/news-events/institute-news/aging-population-expected-to-slow-growth-in-massachusetts-over-long-term)

**Prices and reimbursement**
- 2024 CareScout/Genworth medians for MA:
  - Home health aide: $86,944/yr, about $38/hr at 44 hrs/week (national $77,792)
  - Adult day care: $28,860/yr (national $26,000)
  - Assisted living: $108,696/yr, about $9,060/mo, up 27% from 2023 (national $70,800)
  The MA homemaker figure matches the home health aide figure exactly, which may be an error. — [Genworth/CareScout via Business Wire, Mar 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250301706133/en/); [CareYaya MA](https://www.careyaya.org/home-care-costs/by-state/massachusetts)
- Assisted living: 273 certified ALRs operating as of January 2025, per the state census report. A broker's guide claims 86.1% stabilized occupancy, which is unverified and undated. — [mass.gov ALR Census Report 2025](https://www.mass.gov/doc/assisted-living-residences-census-report-2025/download); [Sovdoc (broker)](https://sovdoc.com/guides/sell-assisted-living-facilities-practice-in-massachusetts)
- Adult day health (ADH) MassHealth rates, 101 CMR 310.00, effective July 1, 2025:
  - Basic per diem (over 3 hrs): $99.49
  - Complex per diem: $136.72
  - Partial basic (3 hrs or less): $49.75; partial complex: $68.36
  - Transport: $28.56 one-way ambulatory, $34.98 wheelchair
  The 2023 basic per diem was $106.32, so the **basic rate fell** while the complex rate stayed flat. — [mass.gov 101 CMR 310.00 rates, eff. Jul 1 2025](https://www.mass.gov/doc/rates-for-adult-day-health-services-effective-july-1-2025/download); [2023 rates](https://www.mass.gov/doc/rates-for-adult-day-health-services-effective-july-5-2023/download)

**Franchise economics (proven model elsewhere): aggregator data, low reliability, verify against the FDDs**
- Home Instead: FY2025 Item 19 average gross sales of about $2.75M and median of about $2.35M across 611 units (one aggregator). Another aggregator, using the 2024 FDD, reports average about $2.39M and initial investment of about $112.5K–$156.5K. — [FranchiseInvestorData](https://franchiseinvestordata.com/franchise/home-instead); [Franzy](https://franzy.com/franchises/home-instead)
- Right at Home: 2024 FDD figures conflict across aggregators, from about $1.34M AUV across 361 offices to about $2.5M average. Investment about $89K–$165K. — [Franzy](https://franzy.com/franchises/right-at-home); [FranchiseInvestorData](https://franchiseinvestordata.com/franchise/right-at-home)
- Visiting Angels: a consultancy blog reports a 2025 FDD median of about $1.3M and average of about $1.68M, investment about $125K–$172K, and franchise fee about $52K–$90K. Low reliability. — [PulseRevOps](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/q14735)

**Senior move management and geriatric care management**
- National pricing for move managers is $55–$100/hr. A house-to-two-bedroom independent-living move runs about $2,500–$5,000 excluding movers and takes 3–5 weeks (Kiplinger, quoting a NJ firm). NASMM is the trade association and accreditation body. — [Kiplinger](https://kiplinger.com/article/retirement/t013-c000-s004-a-move-manager-helps-you-downsize-in-retirement.html); [NASMM estimating seminar](https://www.nasmm.org/seminars/Estimating_Essentials_for_Senior_Move_Managers_FINAL_1_slide_per_page.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Non-medical home care is the strongest senior-care fit for an owner who doesn't deliver care.** The new law expressly contemplates owners being vetted by suitability and financial review, not clinical licensure. It is private pay at about $38/hr or more. The 2026–2027 transition favors well-capitalized, compliant new entrants and should squeeze the roughly two-thirds of unregulated operators. Timing risk: rules will not be final until about mid/late 2027, so a new agency would launch under temporary licensure.
- Adult day health is MassHealth-dependent. The basic rate *fell* from $106.32 to $99.49 between 2023 and 2025, a meaningful reimbursement risk. Private-pay "social" adult day (about $28.9K/yr median) may be better positioned, though ADH licensure and certification rules were not verified.
- Senior move management and geriatric care management (Aging Life Care) are low-capex, private-pay services that pair with home care. The owner can run sales and operations while staff do the work. No MA-specific shortage data were found, so the demand case rests on the 85+ growth and nursing-home closure data.
- Assisted living: 273 ALRs at a $108.7K/yr median, up 27%, signals tight supply. But ALR development is real-estate heavy, well beyond a small-owner budget.

### Gaps
- Effective date of the Chapter 180 licensing provisions, license fees, and any minimum training hours were not confirmed from statute text. The Care Remedy page contradicts itself on "took effect" versus signing.
- Primary FDD Item 19 and Item 7 figures were not retrieved. All franchise numbers are aggregator-sourced and conflict by up to about 2x.
- No verified MA home care operating margin was found.
- No MA wait-time data for home care hours, such as unfilled ASAP/Frail Elder Waiver hours, was found.
- Adult day health licensure and certification requirements (EOEA/MassHealth) and current ADH program counts were not verified.
- No Mass-ALA occupancy survey was found. The 86.1% figure is unverified.
- No MA geriatric care manager hourly rate was found.

## Behavioral and developmental: How long are the waits for pediatric speech, OT, ABA and mental health therapy, and can a non-clinician own the practice under MA law (CPOM, the 2025 PE oversight law)?

### Takeaway
Waits are long and documented. In-home youth therapy averaged 20.5 weeks for MassHealth and 26.5 weeks for privately insured families in 2023. New ABA centers have long waitlists (Autism Commission, Sept 2025). The ownership picture differs by service:
- **Medicine:** MA is a strict corporate practice of medicine (CPOM) state, so a non-physician cannot own a medical practice.
- **Mental health:** a group not wholly owned by its practitioners must hold a **DPH clinic license** (105 CMR 140), which is the legitimate route for a lay owner.
- **ABA:** widely provided by non-clinician-owned companies, with MassHealth accreditation required by Jan 1, 2027 (center-based) and Jan 1, 2028 (others). MassHealth ABA rates are **frozen at October 2024 levels**, with a re-adoption proposed for Dec 1, 2026 with no increase.
- **H.5159** (signed Jan 8, 2025, effective Apr 8, 2025) mainly targets PE, REITs and MSOs, and its notice triggers apply to larger organizations.

### Cited Findings
**Wait times and shortages**
- A state provider association survey (2023) found that families waited an average of 20.5 weeks for in-home therapy on MassHealth and 26.5 weeks with private insurance. It linked the delays to workforce shortages and low reimbursement. — [Boston Globe, Dec 14 2023](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2023/12/14/metro/teen-mental-health-crisis/)
- An Association for Behavioral Healthcare report (2022) found the average wait for an initial outpatient mental health assessment exceeded 2 months, and waits for children and youth were 3 weeks longer than for adults. — [WBUR, Feb 15 2022](https://www.wbur.org/news/2022/02/15/boston-behavioral-health-hospitals-clinics)
- A BCBSMA Foundation/Abt study noted the state lacks public wait-time data. — [Abt Global](https://www.abtglobal.com/insights/publications/report/what-are-the-barriers-to-accessing-mental-health-care-in-massachusetts)
- Autism Commission health subcommittee minutes (Sept 16, 2025): new ABA centers are opening but still have long waitlists. MassHealth ABA use rose 25% in 2023/24, driven by more hours per person. — [mass.gov Autism Commission minutes 9/16/2025](https://www.mass.gov/doc/meeting-minutes-9-16-2025-0/download)
- Commercial insurers must cover ABA for Down syndrome starting Jan 1, 2026, in addition to the existing autism mandate (ARICA). This comes from a provider press release and should be verified against the statute. — [First Steps Developmental Services press release, Apr 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/17/3276122/0/en/First-Steps-Developmental-Services-Expands-Services-in-Woburn.html)
- Early Intervention: MA was rated "needs assistance" by the federal government on the 45-day initial-meeting timeline. — [PBS, "The Long Wait"](https://www.pbs.org/video/the-long-wait-c70e4x)
- Boston 25 reported hundreds of MA children on waitlists for special education programs (2025). — [Boston 25 via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/50-years-special-education-established-230311029.html)

**ABA reimbursement and regulation (changed 2024–2026)**
- MassHealth ABA rates were last set effective Oct 1, 2024. — [mass.gov 101 CMR 358.00 rates eff. Oct 1 2024](https://www.mass.gov/doc/rates-for-applied-behavior-analysis-effective-october-1-2024/download)
- On May 22, 2026, EOHHS proposed re-adopting 101 CMR 358.00 effective Dec 1, 2026 with no rate change. At the June 17, 2026 hearing, staff testified that rates stay at existing levels, citing rising utilization and high provider participation in managed care networks. — [mass.gov hearing notice](https://www.mass.gov/doc/notice-of-public-hearing-716/download); [mass.gov staff testimony, Jun 17 2026](https://www.mass.gov/doc/staff-testimony-for-rates-for-applied-behavior-analysis-presented-at-the-remote-public-hearing-on-june-17-2026-0/download)
- MassHealth requires center-based ABA providers to be accredited by a national ABA accreditation body by Jan 1, 2027, and all other providers by Jan 1, 2028. — [Behavioral Health Business, Oct 2024](https://bhbusiness.com/2024/10/04/massachusetts-mandates-accreditation-for-medicaid-aba-providers/)
- MassHealth does not pay for ABA delivered by assistant-level (AABA) licensees. A 2025 bill would add a rate for them. A provider group (MPAAQ) backs bills requiring biannual ABA rate reviews. — [Sen. Mike Moore bill page](https://www.senatormikemoore.com/moore-legislation-2026/2025/2/1/an-act-increasing-access-to-aba-services-by-recognizing-assistant-level-providers); [MPAAQ press release, Jul 2025](https://www.biermanautism.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/MPAAQ_Press_Release_Rate_Review_Bills.pdf)

**Ownership rules**
- CPOM: MA follows the doctrine from *McMurdo v. Getter* (1937). Secondary legal sources call it a strict CPOM state where only licensed physicians (and certain NPs) may own and operate a medical practice. Non-physicians may be employed but cannot hold ownership. — [McLane Middleton](https://www.mclane.com/insights/know-the-law-in-nh-and-mass-can-corporations-be-doctors-too/); [MedPath 50-state CPOM guide](https://www.medpathcompliance.com/post/corporate-practice-of-medicine-cpom-50-state-guide); [Boston Globe opinion, Jul 10 2025](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/07/10/opinion/private-equity-hospitals-steward-health-care)
- Clinic licensure: under M.G.L. c.111 §51, any entity, for-profit or not, that provides ambulatory medical, dental, physical rehabilitation or **mental health** services must be licensed by DPH as a clinic. The exception applies only to entities **wholly owned and controlled by their practitioners** that do not use "clinic," "institute" or "dispensary" in their names. — [DPH reminder to licensees](https://www.mass.gov/doc/reminder-to-licensees-regarding-licensure-obligations-and-providing-standard-of-care/download); [105 CMR 140.000](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/105-CMR-14000-licensure-of-clinics)
- Licensed mental health clinics must run a program covering intake, evaluation, treatment planning, treatment, emergency services and referrals (105 CMR 140.510–140.560), with staffing rules in 140.310–140.334. — [105 CMR 140.510 (Justia)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/massachusetts/105-cmr/title-105-cmr-140-000/subpart-e/section-140-510)
- H.5159 (Chapter 343 of the Acts of 2024): signed Jan 8, 2025, effective Apr 8, 2025. It targets PE, REITs and MSOs, adding material-change notices for equity-investor transactions, annual financial reporting in some cases, and False Claims Act exposure for owners who learn of violations and fail to act. Material Change Notices already applied to providers above $25M in net patient service revenue. Earlier versions with MSO restrictions and PE bans did **not** survive into the enacted law. — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/massachusetts-expands-oversight-private-equity-investment-healthcare-key-takeaways); [Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/massachusetts-expands-oversight-of-private-equity-investment-in-healthcare-key-takeaways-from-house-bill-5159-signed-into-law-by-governor-healey); [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/?p=298964)

### Inferences
- **Mental health group practice owned by a non-clinician** is possible but requires a DPH clinic license (105 CMR 140) with its program and staffing standards. That is a bigger lift than a practitioner-owned practice, and also a moat. An alternative is an MSO serving a clinician-owned practice. H.5159 adds scrutiny of MSOs mainly when PE is involved or revenue passes $25M, so a small, self-funded owner is unlikely to trigger notice duties. This is an inference; get MA health counsel.
- **ABA** has the most documented demand (25% utilization growth, waitlists, the new Down syndrome mandate) and is commonly owned by non-clinicians with BCBA clinical directors. But MassHealth rates are frozen, accreditation (about a year of prep) is required by 2027–2028, and the AABA rate gap limits staffing models. Private-insurance-heavy payer mixes under ARICA are less exposed than MassHealth-heavy ones.
- **Pediatric speech/OT clinics**: the shortage is plausible (EI federal finding, special-ed waitlists), but no hard MA wait-time data were found. A lay-owned rehab practice would likely fall under DPH clinic licensure as "physical rehabilitation" services. This is an inference, not verified.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 MA-specific waits (in weeks) for outpatient therapy, pediatric speech or pediatric OT were found.
- The MassHealth 97153 ABA unit rate was not retrieved; the rate PDF was not fetched in full.
- Whether a stand-alone ABA agency (not a medical or mental health clinic) needs DPH clinic licensure in MA was not confirmed.
- No MA source directly addressing non-clinician ownership of speech/OT practices was found.
- The current text of the Down syndrome ABA mandate (bill/chapter number) was not verified.

## Other services (mobile phlebotomy, mobile vet, dental, PT, hearing, NEMT): Where is the shortage documented, what are the ownership rules, and what is the reimbursement risk?

### Takeaway
The most owner-friendly of these are **mobile and in-clinic veterinary care**, because MA allows non-vets to own practices outside a professional corporation if they appoint a veterinarian medical director, and **non-emergency medical transport (NEMT)**, which is a fleet business but tied to MassHealth broker rates and the HST system that changed on Jan 1, 2026. **Dental** has the clearest access gap: only 43% of offices accept MassHealth, and the Berkshires are acute. But a non-dentist-owned practice must be licensed as a dental clinic, and MassHealth rates are the core problem. **Physical therapy** requires a Board-issued PT facility license that corporations and LLCs can hold, with a PT compliance officer, so a lay owner appears possible. Mobile phlebotomy needs no state phlebotomist license, but lab licensing is unverified. Hearing care was not researched successfully.

### Cited Findings
**Veterinary**
- The MA Veterinary Medical Association says non-veterinarians may own practices if the business is not a professional corporation under MGL 156A (sole proprietorships, partnerships and similar are allowed). Under 256 CMR 5.06, a non-vet owner must appoint a licensed veterinarian as veterinary medical director. — [MassVet FAQ](https://massvet.org/faqs)
- Shortage evidence (mostly 2021–2024):
  - Angell Animal Medical Center had a vet tech shortage above 20% and was at times "one nurse away" from diversion (NBC Boston).
  - ER waits that averaged under 1 hour in 2019 reached up to 5 hours in 2021 (Boston Globe).
  - Western MA shelters rely on relief vets (WAMC, 2024).
  — [NBC Boston](https://nbcboston.com/news/local/veterinarian-shortage-at-crisis-levels-in-massachusetts/3005671); [Boston Globe, Jul 2021](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2021/07/09/metro/overwhelmed-veterinarians-struggle-with-staggering-surge-pet-visits); [WAMC, Aug 2024](https://wamc.org/news/2024-08-12/national-veterinarian-shortage-felt-in-pioneer-valley)
- Tufts Cummings is New England's only vet school. Nationally, Mars Veterinary Health estimates about 41,000 more veterinarians are needed by 2030. An Oct 2025 MA bill cites a "shortage of veterinarians in the commonwealth." — [UMass Donahue / Tufts study](https://donahue.umass.edu/news-events/media-coverage/umdi-report-on-veterinary-medicine-in-new-england); [Scripps News](https://www.scrippsnews.com/science-and-tech/veterinarian-shortage-could-leave-pets-without-medical-care); [H.4737](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H4737.Html)

**Dental (Western/Central MA)**
- Only 43% of MA dental offices are in-network with MassHealth, mainly because of low rates (Mar 2026). — [BINJ, Mar 19 2026](https://binj.news/2026/03/19/more-than-half-of-mass-dental-offices-no-longer-accept-masshealth-due-to-low-reimbursement-rates)
- Berkshires: nearly half of dentists are near retirement, and only 3 general dentists are accepting new MassHealth patients. This is an advocate's op-ed, Mar 2026. — [Berkshire Edge](https://theberkshireedge.com/berkshires-state-need-accessible-dental-care-providers/)
- 56 federally designated dental shortage areas and 69 cities/towns with no dentist (KFF data cited in a 2021 advocacy fact sheet, dated). — [HCFA fact sheet 2021](https://hcfama.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Oral-Health-Commision-and-Needs-Assessment-Act-Fact-Sheet-5.6.21.pdf)
- The "Take 10" bill (H.1401/S.888) would pilot supplemental MassHealth dental payments in 10 municipalities. It was in committee after a July 2025 hearing (search summary). — [Special Commission on Oral Health minutes](https://www.mass.gov/doc/special-commission-on-oral-health-access-coverage-subcommittee-meeting-minutes-11725-docx/download)
- Ownership: 234 CMR 5.02 requires practices where non-owner dentists work to designate a dentist as Dental Director. A dated legal summary says practices not wholly owned by dentists must be licensed as dental clinics, and ads must name a dentist-owner. This is consistent with DPH clinic licensure covering "dental" services (above). — [234 CMR 5.02 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/234-CMR-5-02); [2012 survey of state corporate-practice-of-dentistry laws](https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/4-25-12-Survey-of-State-Laws-Governing-the-Corporate-Practice-of-Dentistry.pdf); [DPH clinic licensure reminder](https://www.mass.gov/doc/reminder-to-licensees-regarding-licensure-obligations-and-providing-standard-of-care/download)

**Physical therapy**
- PT may only be delivered at a site holding a Board-issued Physical Therapy Facility license (259 CMR 6.00). Sole proprietorships, partnerships, corporations, LLCs and LLPs can apply. Each facility needs a PT Compliance Officer who is a physical therapist. Owners with more than 10% are subject to good-moral-character discipline. A transfer of controlling interest voids the license, and the new owner must reapply within 30 days. — [259 CMR 6.00 (mass.gov PDF)](https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2017/10/30/259cmr6.pdf); [259 CMR 6.02 (Justia)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/massachusetts/259-cmr/title-259-cmr-6-00/section-6-02)
- Private-practice therapists must file a Notice of Ownership Interest and disclose when a referral source earns income from the PT service. — [259 CMR 5.05 (Justia)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/massachusetts/259-cmr/title-259-cmr-5-00/section-5-05)

**Mobile phlebotomy**
- MA does not require a state phlebotomist license, but employers expect ASCP, NHA or AMT certification (secondary licensing aggregator). Existing MA mobile draw services, such as Speedy Sticks, route specimens to partner labs. — [GetLicenseMap](https://getlicensemap.com/states/phlebotomist/massachusetts); [Speedy Sticks MA](https://www.speedysticks.com/locations/massachusetts/mobile-phlebotomy)

**Non-emergency medical transportation (NEMT)**
- Starting Jan 1, 2026, MassHealth NEMT moved to fee-for-service with members assigned to a regional broker by address. The prescriber submits a PT-1 form, which can take up to 3 business days. — [mass.gov MassHealth transportation notice, Dec 2025](https://www.mass.gov/doc/accessing-masshealth-transportation-services-december-2025/download)
- Wheelchair van trips have gone through brokers since April 2022. Sedan service still requires a PT-1. — [All Provider Bulletin 339](https://www.mass.gov/doc/all-provider-bulletin-339-upcoming-changes-to-masshealth-nonemergency-wheelchair-van-transportation-services/download)
- The enhanced wheelchair van rate (selective contracts) is $92.88 per trip for the first 5 miles, effective July 1, 2025, with extra mileage per the broker contract. — [Administrative Bulletin 25-14](https://www.mass.gov/doc/administrative-bulletin-25-14-101-cmr-32700-rates-for-ambulance-and-wheelchair-van-services-payment-rate-for-enhanced-wheelchair-van-trips-offered-through-selective-contracts-effective-july-1-2025/download)
- Broker MART reportedly requires at least 1 year of owning a passenger transport business and closes applications May 1–July 31. This is an undated third-party guide. — [RouteGenie MART guide](https://routegenie.com/blog/mart-nemt-broker-guide/)
- Adult day health programs are paid $28.56 ambulatory and $34.98 wheelchair per one-way trip, a possible B2B transport channel. — [101 CMR 310.00](https://www.mass.gov/doc/rates-for-adult-day-health-services-effective-july-1-2025/download)

### Inferences
- **Mobile or general-practice vet clinic with a hired DVM medical director** is a clean fit for an owner who doesn't treat patients. The ownership rules are explicit, demand is private-pay, and there is no MassHealth exposure. The weak point is the evidence: MA-specific shortage data are 2021–2024 and anecdotal, and hiring the DVM is itself the bottleneck.
- **NEMT** fits an owner-operator (fleet, drivers, dispatch), but revenue depends on broker volume and fixed MassHealth rates. The 1-year operating requirement (if accurate) means starting with private-pay or ADH/facility contracts.
- **Dental** has a clear MassHealth access gap, but the gap exists *because* rates are low. A lay owner needs a DPH dental clinic license and a dentist director. Viable mainly as a mostly private-pay or community-health-partnership model in an underserved county.
- **PT** facility licensing appears to allow lay ownership with a PT compliance officer, but no MA PT shortage data were found.

### Gaps
- No MA source on whether a mobile phlebotomy business needs a DPH clinical lab license or registration, or a specimen-collection station permit.
- No MA hearing-care research completed: audiologist and hearing-aid dispenser licensing and ownership, OTC hearing aid market impact, shortage data.
- No 2025–2026 MA vet wait-time data, and no MA mobile-vet registration rules.
- No current HRSA dental HPSA count for MA, and no Western/Central MA dentist-per-capita data.
- MassHealth sedan (PT-1) per-trip rate and the 2026 FFS provider enrollment process were not found. The state auditor's MassHealth transportation audit was not reviewed.
- No MA PT access or wait-time data.

## Synthesis: Which 6–10 opportunities best fit an owner who doesn't deliver the service?

### Takeaway
Ranked by documented MA shortage, a proven model elsewhere, a clear legal path for a non-clinician owner, and reimbursement risk:
1. Private-pay non-medical home care (new 2026 licensure)
2. Infant/toddler child care center, or a staffed FCC network
3. Employer-sponsored and backup child care
4. Veterinary clinic (mobile or fixed) with a hired DVM director
5. ABA center (accreditation and rate-freeze caveats)
6. DPH-licensed outpatient mental health clinic for youth and families
7. Senior move management plus geriatric care management (add-on to home care)
8. NEMT / wheelchair van
9. Private-pay social adult day
10. Lay-owned PT clinic (licensing confirmed, demand unverified)

Dental is a documented gap but a weak fit, because of MassHealth economics and clinic-licensure overhead.

### Cited Findings
- Home care: new EOHHS licensure with 5% owner suitability and financial-capacity review ([MA Legislature](https://malegislature.gov/PressRoom/Detail?pressReleaseId=1473)); about 400 agencies, only 1/3 regulated ([Care Remedy](https://www.careremedy.com/massachusetts-to-start-licensing-home-care-agencies-just-not-yet-and-that-gap-is-worth-understanding-before-you-hire)); about $38/hr median aide price ([Business Wire/CareScout](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250301706133/en/)); 85+ population up about 60% by 2040 ([Dorchester Reporter](https://www.dotnews.com/2025/08/20/care-infrastructure-under-pressure-state-s-oldest-population-explodes/)); franchise investment about $90K–$172K (aggregators, unverified) ([Franzy](https://franzy.com/franchises/home-instead)).
- Infant/toddler care: 70% and 43% desert rates ([CommonWealth Beacon](https://commonwealthbeacon.org/newsletter/dl-08-06-25/)); over $26K median infant price in Middlesex and Norfolk ([NBC Boston](https://nbcboston.com/news/local/2-mass-counties-rank-among-the-most-expensive-for-infant-care/2959511)); C3 flat and closed to about 600 new programs ([MassBudget](https://massbudget.org/2026/07/21/massbudget-in-depth-analysis-of-fy-2027-gaa/)).
- Backup care: Bright Horizons backup revenue above $725M, +19% in 2025 ([SEC ARS](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001437578/000119312526162618/d98247dars.pdf)).
- Vet: lay ownership allowed with a DVM medical director ([MassVet](https://massvet.org/faqs)).
- ABA: utilization +25%, waitlists ([Autism Commission](https://www.mass.gov/doc/meeting-minutes-9-16-2025-0/download)); rates frozen ([mass.gov testimony](https://www.mass.gov/doc/staff-testimony-for-rates-for-applied-behavior-analysis-presented-at-the-remote-public-hearing-on-june-17-2026-0/download)); accreditation by 2027/2028 ([BH Business](https://bhbusiness.com/2024/10/04/massachusetts-mandates-accreditation-for-medicaid-aba-providers/)).
- Mental health: 20.5–26.5 week waits for in-home therapy ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2023/12/14/metro/teen-mental-health-crisis/)); lay ownership requires a DPH clinic license ([DPH](https://www.mass.gov/doc/reminder-to-licensees-regarding-licensure-obligations-and-providing-standard-of-care/download)).
- NEMT: Jan 2026 FFS/broker change ([mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/doc/accessing-masshealth-transportation-services-december-2025/download)); $92.88 enhanced wheelchair van trip rate ([AB 25-14](https://www.mass.gov/doc/administrative-bulletin-25-14-101-cmr-32700-rates-for-ambulance-and-wheelchair-van-services-payment-rate-for-enhanced-wheelchair-van-trips-offered-through-selective-contracts-effective-july-1-2025/download)).
- Adult day: MassHealth basic ADH rate fell to $99.49 ([101 CMR 310.00](https://www.mass.gov/doc/rates-for-adult-day-health-services-effective-july-1-2025/download)); private-pay adult day median $28,860/yr ([Business Wire/CareScout](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250301706133/en/)).
- PT: facility license open to LLCs and corporations, with a PT compliance officer ([259 CMR 6.00](https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2017/10/30/259cmr6.pdf)).

### Inferences
- **Reimbursement-risk ranking, lowest to highest:**
  - Private-pay home care, vet, backup care and move management: low
  - Child care: medium, given its subsidy/C3 dependence
  - ABA: medium-high, from the MassHealth freeze, offset by commercial ARICA coverage
  - Mental health clinic: medium-high
  - NEMT, adult day health and dental: high, being MassHealth-rate-bound
- **Licensing barriers to the owner-not-provider structure, lowest to highest:**
  - Senior move management: no license found (verify)
  - Home care: new EOHHS license, owner suitability review
  - Vet: DVM medical director
  - PT: facility license plus PT compliance officer
  - Child care: EEC license plus qualified director
  - ABA: payer credentialing plus accreditation
  - Mental health and dental: DPH clinic license
  - Physician-led services: blocked by CPOM; an MSO is the only path
- **Laws that changed 2024–2026:**
  - H.5159 PE/MSO oversight (eff. Apr 8, 2025)
  - MassHealth ABA accreditation mandate (announced 2024; deadlines 2027/2028)
  - MassHealth ABA rate freeze (proposed re-adoption Dec 1, 2026)
  - Down syndrome ABA coverage (Jan 1, 2026; verify)
  - MassHealth NEMT move to FFS/broker (Jan 1, 2026)
  - ADH rate reset (Jul 1, 2025)
  - C3 level funding through FY27
  - **Home care agency licensure, Chapter 180 of the Acts of 2026 (signed Aug 6, 2026)**
- Startup cost signals: only home care franchise Item 7 ranges were found (about $90K–$172K, aggregator data). Child care center, vet clinic, ABA center, NEMT fleet and PT clinic startup costs were not found and should be treated as unknown.

### Gaps
- Verified startup costs and margins for every opportunity except the aggregator-sourced home care franchise ranges.
- Primary franchise FDDs for home care, senior move management (e.g., Caring Transitions), child care (e.g., Goddard, Primrose) and mobile vet concepts were not retrieved.
- No MA-specific market-sizing for backup care or employer-sponsored care contracts.
- Hearing care and mobile phlebotomy lab licensing remain unresearched.
