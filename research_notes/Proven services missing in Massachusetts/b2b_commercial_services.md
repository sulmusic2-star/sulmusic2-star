# B2B and Commercial Services Proven Elsewhere but Possibly Undersupplied in Massachusetts (owner-not-operator models)

Research date: 2026-10-09. All dollar figures are as reported by the cited source, with its date where known. "Verified" means read on a primary or official source in this session. "Secondary" means it came from an aggregator, vendor, or law-firm summary. "Estimate/inference" means my own reasoning. Several mass.gov pages returned HTTP 403 to direct fetches, so some mass.gov facts come from search-result extracts of those official documents. Those are flagged.

## Q1. Fire and life safety inspection, testing and maintenance (extinguishers, kitchen suppression, sprinkler, fire alarm): MA regulatory trigger, licensing, proven model, economics

### Takeaway
This is the most proven owner-not-operator model on the list. Code requires the inspections, so demand recurs and cannot be deferred, and PE roll-ups (Pye-Barker, Altus/Apax, Riverside, APi, Summit) are actively buying Massachusetts firms. That proves the model's value and gives an exit path. It also means MA is not "missing" this service. The opening is a dense, well-run, inspection-led regional operator built to sell to a roll-up, not a gap nobody serves.

### Cited Findings
- MA Department of Fire Services (DFS) licensing: a company that services fire suppression systems or portable extinguishers needs a DFS **Certificate of Registration**, at about $60 per restriction. Each technician needs a **Certificate of Competency** for each system type, at about $50 per restriction. The portable extinguisher restriction is "R46". A company's registration application must list the Certificate of Competency number of every person it employs. Sources: [Mass.gov – Apply for licenses to service fire extinguishers and extinguishing systems](https://www.mass.gov/how-to/apply-for-licenses-to-service-fire-extinguishers-and-fire-extinguishing-systems); [FP-085 company registration form](https://www.mass.gov/doc/fp-085-application-for-certificate-of-registration-for-servicing-portable-fire-extinguishers/download). Fees conflict slightly between forms, so confirm with DFS.
- The current MA fire code, 527 CMR 1.00, took effect December 9, 2022 and largely adopts NFPA 1 (2021) with MA amendments. [Mass.gov DFS study materials](https://www.mass.gov/doc/commercial-hood-cleaning-study-materials/download)
- Investor thesis, from Lincoln International (Aug 2022): codes mandate regular inspection, which makes inspections and repairs "critical and non-discretionary." The market is "highly fragmented," and licensing creates "high barriers to entry." There were 86 fire and life safety deals in 2021 and 53 in H1 2022. Pye-Barker had done 30+ deals since 2019, Summit 12 and AI Fire 11. [Lincoln International](https://www.lincolninternational.com/perspectives/articles/ma-activity-in-fire-and-life-safety-services-continues-to-heat-up/). A later Lincoln update reported 129 deals in 2023 and 109 through September 2024; I saw that only as a search snippet, not the full page. [Lincoln International update](https://lincolninternational.com/?p=129054)
- Pye-Barker completed **57 acquisitions in 2025** and reports 9,000 team members across 47 states. [Security Systems News](https://www.securitysystemsnews.com/article/pye-barker-fire-safety-accelerates-growth-strategy-with-57-acquisitions-in-2025)
- MA-specific roll-up activity (secondary, aggregator-reported):
  - Altus Fire & Life Safety bought Fire Systems, Inc. (MA/RI) in 2022 and Priority Fire & Security (Clinton, MA) in 2023.
  - Apax bought Altus from AE Industrial in September 2024. Altus then added Star Fire Protection in August 2025 to grow in the Northeast.
  - [Security Systems News – Altus tag](https://www.securitysystemsnews.com/tag/altus-fire-and-life-safety); [PrivSource deal](https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/deal/vXSazo)
  - Riverside formed a fire and life safety platform from American Service Company (eastern MA) and Advanced Fire Protection Systems. The source is undated. [Capital-riesgo](https://capital-riesgo.es/en/articles/the-riverside-company-has-formed-its-third-fire-and-life-safety-platform-through-the-acquisitions-of-american-service-company-and-advanced-fire-protection-systems/)
  - Massachusetts Capital Resource Co. invested in Boston Fire Sprinkler Protection, Inc. in April 2024. [MCRC](https://masscapital.com/april-2024-mcrc-announces-a-new-investment-in-boston-fire-sprinkler-protection-inc/)
  - Endeavor Fire Protection (Boston HQ) was reported sold to APi Group in November 2024. This comes only from an aggregator and is unverified. [PrivateEquityInfo](https://privateequityinfo.com/directory/private-equity-portfolio-company/3/endeavor-fire-protection)
- Economics (secondary):
  - APi management has said every $1 of inspection revenue generates $3–$4 of follow-on service revenue at roughly 10 points higher gross margin. This is an analyst blog citing management. [Kairos Research](https://kairosresearch.substack.com/p/apg-a-unique-fire-safety-company)
  - A sell-side M&A guide puts the "implied EBITDA margin" for fire protection companies at 18.8%. It says PE buyers target firms with at least $1M EBITDA and contract renewal above 90%. [Ad Astra Equity](https://www.adastraequity.com/sell-a-business/fire-protection-companies)
  - A software vendor's benchmark for top performers is that 20% of work orders are high-margin deficiency-repair "pull-through." This is a vendor claim. [ServiceTrade](https://servicetrade.com/lp/fire-service-solution/)
- Labor: an industry site cites an EHS Today 2025 survey showing a 14–20% skilled-labor shortfall in commercial fire and mechanical work and 29% of the workforce retiring by 2026. These are secondhand and advocacy-framed. [FireProtectionFinder](https://fireprotectionfinder.com/news/fire-protection-workforce-shortage-nicet-2026). Non-MA job postings show NICET II/III sprinkler inspectors paid about $40–$52/hr. [GPAC posting](https://jobs.gogpac.com/gpac/search/vacancy/all/1/504054410); [Insight Global posting](https://jobs.insightglobal.com/find_a_job/colorado/job-531685/)
- Public-sector work triggers MA prevailing wage:
  - The Town of Reading's fire alarm testing, inspection, maintenance and repair contract states that prevailing wage (c.149 §§26–27D) applies. [Reading Post, 2022](https://thereadingpost.com/2022/03/02/fire-alarm-testing-inspection-maintenance-and-repair-services-contract-contract-no-22-31)
  - DLS issued a December 2024 opinion letter on job classifications for fire alarm repair, maintenance and operation. [Mass.gov DLS opinion](https://www.mass.gov/doc/opinion-letter-regarding-fire-alarm-system-repair-maintenance-and-operation/download)
  - A 1999 letter had found pure system testing and shutdown not covered. Its current status is unclear. [Mass.gov 1999 letter](https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2016/10/ud/1999-05-27-pw-moschos.pdf)

### Inferences
- Owner-not-operator feasibility is **high**. Licenses attach to individual technicians and are listed on the company registration, so the owner can hire licensed technicians instead of holding the license personally. Confirm whether DFS requires a designated licensed principal; I did not verify this.
- The best entry is the low-capital, inspection-led segment: portable extinguishers, kitchen suppression systems, and emergency and exit lighting. Sprinkler and alarm work can be added later via licensed hires or subcontracted sprinkler contractors. Sprinkler and fire alarm contracting carry heavier licensing that I did not verify (DPL sprinkler licenses; c.141 systems licenses).
- "Undersupply" in MA is mainly a **labor and service-quality gap**, not a provider-count gap. Roll-up consolidation can leave small customers such as restaurants and small landlords underserved, which is an opening for a responsive local operator.
- Exit: at the 18.8% implied margin (secondary), a $3M-revenue operator would earn about $560K EBITDA. That is below the $1M PE threshold but a typical add-on size. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- No count of MA DFS-registered extinguisher or suppression companies, or of licensed individuals. DFS licensing lookups were not accessible in this session.
- No MA-specific technician wage data, revenue per technician, or inspection price list. The national per-extinguisher inspection price was not found.
- Exact licensing for fire sprinkler inspection (DPL sprinkler fitter/contractor) and fire alarm (c.141 systems contractor/technician) was not verified in this session.

## Q2. Commercial kitchen exhaust (hood) cleaning plus grease trap/FOG compliance for restaurants

### Takeaway
MA has about 15,300 restaurant locations (NRA, 2025). Each has fire-code-mandated hood cleaning on a monthly-to-annual schedule set by cooking volume, and in Boston, mandatory grease-trap maintenance logs. Certification is via a State Fire Marshal Certificate of Competency held by the technician. The HOODZ franchise shows the model is proven nationally (about $577K average unit revenue). This is a strong route-based, recurring, owner-not-operator candidate. Direct evidence of MA provider scarcity was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Anyone who cleans or inspects commercial cooking exhaust systems in MA must hold a **State Fire Marshal Certificate of Competency**. A **Type 1** certificate is for work performed for the public. A **Type 2** certificate is restricted to systems the holder or employer owns or operates. Only Type 1 holders may perform inspections. [Mass.gov – Commercial Cooking Exhaust Systems](https://www.mass.gov/service-details/commercial-cooking-exhaust-systems) (content via search extract; the page returned 403 to direct fetch)
- The DFS exam study list cites 527 CMR 1.00 Chapter 50 and NFPA 96 (2021). [DFS hood cleaning study materials](https://www.mass.gov/doc/commercial-hood-cleaning-study-materials/download)
- Inspection and cleaning frequency follows cooking volume: **monthly** for solid-fuel cooking, **quarterly** for high-volume, **semiannual** for moderate-volume, **annual** for low-volume. This comes from an older DFS document; verify against current code. [DFS – Cleaning or inspection of commercial cooking exhaust systems](https://www.mass.gov/doc/cleaning-or-inspection-of-commercial-cooking-exhaust-systems/download)
- A cleaning company must leave a certificate showing the company, technician and date, and must affix a service label. Records are kept for 3 years. [DFS hood cleaning label requirements](https://www.mass.gov/doc/hood-cleaning-label-requirements/download)
- Boston adds city registration for hood cleaners and inspectors and a color-coded sticker system. [Boston sticker requirements](https://d8-dev2.boston.gov/sites/default/files/embed/s/sticker-requirements.pdf)
- Demand base: the NRA Massachusetts fact sheet reports **15,294 restaurant locations** and $36.9B in restaurant and foodservice sales (2025 data). [National Restaurant Association](https://www.restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/industry-statistics/massachusetts-state-fact-sheet). A payments-company blog cites 18,124 establishments, likely using a different definition. [Ramp, June 2025](https://ramp.com/blog/top-restaurants-massachusetts)
- Boston Water and Sewer Commission requires properly installed and maintained grease traps in **all** restaurants and food establishments in Boston. The FOG discharge limit is 100 mg/L. Owners must keep a Grease Control Log of date, quantity removed and signature. [BWSC Commercial Kitchen Grease Trap Guidelines](https://www.bwsc.org/business-customers/programs-guidelines-assistance/commercial-kitchen-grease-trap-guidelines). Somerville runs its own FOG program. [Somerville FOG](https://SomervilleMA.gov/fog)
- Proven model: HOODZ (a BELFOR franchise) had average unit gross revenue of about **$577,400** in its 2024 FDD, against a subsector average of about $798K. Royalties are 7–10% and total investment is about **$184K–$232K**. [Franzy – HOODZ](https://franzy.com/franchises/hoodz). The Item 19 revenue range is about $422K–$2.9M. [FranchiseOverview – HOODZ](https://franchiseoverview.com/company/hoodz). Sources conflict on the franchise fee. Secondary.
- Grease trap pumping price (generic US, low-quality source): about $240–$330 per pumping for a 1,000–1,500 gallon trap under contract. [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/?p=19032). No MA-specific pricing was found.

### Inferences
- **Owner-not-operator feasibility is high.** Certification sits with Type 1 technicians, and work is done at night by 2-person crews. The owner handles sales, routing and compliance documentation.
- Bundling hood cleaning with grease-trap service (pumping by a licensed hauler subcontractor, or in-house with a vac truck), used-cooking-oil collection and kitchen-suppression inspection (an R-restriction DFS license) creates a single "kitchen compliance" account with three to four recurring revenue lines. This is an inference.
- Illustrative demand math (estimate): if even 50% of 15,294 locations run hoods needing semiannual-or-better cleaning at an assumed $400–$800 per clean, the statewide hood-cleaning pool would be roughly $6M–$25M+ a year. This is a rough order of magnitude only, and the price is an unsourced assumption.

### Gaps
- No count of Type 1 hood-cleaning certificate holders or companies in MA, so direct supply-side scarcity is unverified.
- No MA hood-cleaning price data, no Boston registration count, and no MA-specific grease-trap pumping frequency rule outside Boston's log requirement.
- MA grease-hauler licensing (local Board of Health and MassDEP septage/grease hauler permits) was not verified.

## Q3. Backflow / cross-connection testing (310 CMR 22.22)

### Takeaway
MassDEP rules make backflow testing a mandatory, calendar-driven recurring service. Reduced-pressure backflow preventers are tested **semiannually** by MassDEP-certified testers. Failures force 14-day repairs by licensed plumbers or sprinkler contractors. Low capital and per-test pricing of about $50–$150 make it a good route business or a lead-generator add-on to fire sprinkler and plumbing work. Evidence of a tester shortage in MA was **not found**.

### Cited Findings
- RPBP installations registered with a public water system require **semiannual testing** by a MassDEP Certified Backflow Tester. An RPBP in service fewer than 6 months a year needs at least one annual test. Pressure vacuum breakers may be tested by the supplier or MassDEP at any time, and MassDEP recommends annual owner testing for irrigation units. [MassDEP Cross-Connection Control Program Manual](https://www.mass.gov/doc/cross-connection-control-program-manual/download) (via search extract)
- Tester certification falls under 310 CMR 22.22(13). Renewal requires at least 24 device tests and 3 training contact hours in the prior 3 years. [MassDEP certification fact sheet](https://www.mass.gov/doc/instructions-fact-sheet/download). Certification follows passing a MassDEP-approved written and practical exam, and anyone who passes may apply. [MassDEP fact sheet](https://www.mass.gov/doc/instructions-fact-sheet/download)
- A failed device must be repaired by a **licensed plumber or fire sprinkler contractor within 14 calendar days** under 310 CMR 22.22(13)(b), then retested by a certified tester. [MassDEP device test report form](https://www.mass.gov/doc/backflow-prevention-device-inspection-maintenance-report-form-0/download)
- Pricing signals (secondary): MA municipal bid listings budget about **$50/test** (Framingham IFB), **$50–$100/test** (a Framingham RFP) and about **$100/test** (Westborough). These are municipal planning figures, not contractor quotes. [BidBanana listing 1](https://bidbanana.thebidlab.com/bid/4rUWgtNUFDVv2ROWKK6u); [BidBanana listing 2](https://bidbanana.thebidlab.com/bid/7WPgTSRKiCsiYdvHqWAN); [BidBanana listing 3](https://bidbanana.thebidlab.com/bid/4g3T6NsT8QsCKn2jscYm). Which listing maps to which town was not confirmed in this session.
- Towns run active programs (example: [Salisbury MA Backflow Testing](https://www.salisburyma.gov/print/71331)).

### Inferences
- **Owner-not-operator feasibility is high.** Certification is individual, and an owner can employ certified testers. Repairs require a licensed plumber or sprinkler contractor, so pairing with one, or employing one, captures the repair pull-through. This mirrors the fire-inspection pull-through model.
- Illustrative unit economics (estimate, not sourced): one tester doing about 8–12 commercial tests a day at $75–$125 would bill roughly $600–$1,500 a day before repairs. Because semiannual RPBP testing guarantees two visits a year per device, a 1,000-device route is about 2,000 tests a year.
- Best fit is as a **bolt-on to fire sprinkler inspection**, since fire-line backflows sit in sprinkler rooms, or to hood and grease-trap kitchen routes. It is weaker as a stand-alone business, because municipal bids push prices toward about $50.

### Gaps
- No MassDEP count of certified testers or registered devices in MA, and no news of tester shortages or long lead times.
- No MA private-market per-test price survey.
- I did not verify whether municipal backflow testing contracts trigger prevailing wage.

## Q4. Building-performance compliance services: Boston BERDO, Cambridge BEUDO, statewide LBER (new question, not in the original brief)

### Takeaway
Boston's BERDO requires a **credentialed third-party verifier** (PE, CEM, licensed architect, etc.) who is independent of the owner. Verification is due in the first emissions-compliance year (**2026** or **2031**) and every five years after. Fines run $150–$1,000 a day, and the alternative compliance payment is $234 per ton of CO2e. Statewide LBER adds annual reporting for all buildings of 20,000 sq ft or more. Annual reporting, periodic verification and emissions-compliance planning can be a recurring, staff-delivered B2B compliance service. The main competition is engineering firms and the city's own free help for some owners.

### Cited Findings
- BERDO covers residential buildings with 15 or more units, non-residential buildings of 20,000 sq ft or more, and parcels whose buildings sum to 20,000 sq ft or 15 units. Owners must hire a **third-party qualified energy professional** to verify reported data in the first reporting year, the first emissions-compliance year (2026 or 2031), and every five years after. The 2026 reporting deadline was extended from May 15 to **August 15, 2026**. [Boston.gov – BERDO](https://www.boston.gov/departments/environment/building-emissions-reduction-and-disclosure)
- From the city's BERDO Data Verification Webinar (March 9, 2026) [Boston.gov PDF](https://www.boston.gov/sites/default/files/file/2026/03/BERDO%20Data%20Verification%20Webinar%20-%2003.09.26_2.pdf):
  - The verifier must not be employed by the owner or property manager, must hold an approved credential (e.g., PE, CEM, licensed architect), and must register in the BERDO portal.
  - Fines: failure to report is $150/day (20,000–35,000 sq ft) or $300/day (over 35,000 sq ft or multi-building). Failure to meet emissions standards is $300/day or $1,000/day. Inaccurate reporting is $1,000–$5,000.
  - The city offers "Free Reporting & Verification Services" to some owners and publishes an RFI list of data verifiers.
  - BERDO covers "only 5%" of Boston's building stock, but those buildings could reduce city emissions by nearly 40%.
- Alternative compliance payment: **$234 per metric ton CO2e** over the limit in Boston [Boston BERDO emissions webinar, Jan 2024](https://search.boston.gov/sites/default/files/file/2024/01/BERDO%20Emissions%20Compliance%20Webinar%201_3_24%20%281%29.pdf). Cambridge BEUDO also uses $234/tCO2e for its first cycle [Cambridge Day](https://www.cambridgeday.com/?p=106192). Cambridge enacted BEUDO emissions standards in July 2023. [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2023/07/08/metro/cambridge-enacts-ambitious-building-emissions-reduction-standards)
- Statewide LBER (M.G.L. c.25A §20) covers buildings of 20,000 sq ft or more. Calendar-year 2025 data was due **June 30, 2026**. Utilities report electric, gas and steam usage, while owners or their agents must report oil, propane, wood and on-site renewables. Boston and Cambridge share BERDO/BEUDO data with DOER. [Mass.gov LBER policy](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/large-building-energy-reporting-policy); [DOER 2026 owner notification](https://www.mass.gov/doc/lber-building-owner-notification-redacted-sample-2026/download)
- Secondary: consultant blogs confirm BERDO and BEUDO compliance activity started in 2025. [Veolia](https://blog.veolianorthamerica.com/massachusetts-beudo-and-berdo-compliance-kickstart-in-2025-are-you-prepared); [SOCOTEC](https://www.socotec.us/blog/boston-berdo-regulations-compliance)

### Inferences
- **Owner-not-operator feasibility is high.** Verification must be done by credentialed staff (PE or CEM), whom an owner can hire. Annual benchmarking and reporting can be done by non-credentialed analysts under the verifier.
- The recurring revenue stack would be: annual BERDO/BEUDO/LBER reporting retainers, plus verification in 2026, 2031 and every 5 years, plus emissions-compliance planning (ACP versus RECs versus retrofits). Retrofit project management and referral fees could be added. This is an inference.
- Similar building-performance laws elsewhere (e.g., NYC Local Law 97/84) have supported dedicated compliance firms, but I did not source this in the session; treat it as an unverified analog.
- Risks: the city's free services for some owners, engineering-firm competition, and policy risk (deadline extensions, flexibility such as RECs).

### Gaps
- No count of BERDO, BEUDO or LBER covered buildings, and no count of registered BERDO verifiers. The RFI list exists but was not retrieved.
- No market price for BERDO verification or annual reporting.
- Whether Cambridge BEUDO requires third-party verification was not confirmed.

## Q5. Stormwater BMP inspection and maintenance for private commercial sites (MS4 / MassDEP Stormwater Standard 9)

### Takeaway
Private developments in MA operate under MassDEP Stormwater Standard 9 operation and maintenance (O&M) plans. Several towns, including Dracut, Newton and Haverhill, require annual inspection logs or reports to support their own MS4 permit compliance. This creates recurring inspection, reporting and cleaning demand. Towns themselves report staffing limits in inspecting BMPs. MA evidence of demand is good. Evidence about MA providers and pricing is thin.

### Cited Findings
- Private O&M plans cite 310 CMR 10.05(6)(k) and state they satisfy MA Stormwater Standard 9. [Newton O&M plan](https://www.newtonma.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/128321/638799669114870000); [Sutton O&M plan](https://www.suttonma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/5808/Updated-O-M-Plan)
- Dracut plans require inspection and cleaning logs to be maintained and **submitted to the Town Stormwater Authority in an annual report**. [Dracut O&M Plan, Sept 2025](https://dracutma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/5091/2025-09-23---Operation-and-Maintenance-Plan--PDF)
- A Newton plan requires annual inspection logs to be submitted to the DPW Engineering Division to maintain compliance under Newton's NPDES MS4 permit. [Newton document](https://www.newtonma.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/134233/639001762451430000). I could not confirm which Newton PDF holds that exact clause.
- Haverhill plans require annual reports on the stormwater system and maintenance performed. [Haverhill O&M plan](https://www.haverhillma.gov/media/kgebchai/20250130_33-1540_operationsmaintenanceplan_09-10-24.pdf)
- Capacity strain: Bellingham reported it was unable to inspect all inline proprietary BMPs in Permit Year 3, citing staffing and COVID-19. [EPA Region 1 – Bellingham MS4 annual report 2021](https://www3.epa.gov/region1/npdes/stormwater/ma/reports/2021/BELLINGHAM_MA_AR21.pdf)
- DCR's own O&M plan was written to meet the 2016 Small MS4 General Permit. [Mass.gov DCR O&M plan](https://www.mass.gov/doc/operations-and-maintenance-om-plan/download)

### Inferences
- The model would be annual or semiannual inspection contracts with property managers of retail centers, warehouses, office parks and HOAs, and filing the town reports. Sediment and catch-basin cleaning would be subcontracted to a vac-truck operator, or done in-house later. **Owner-not-operator feasibility is high.** Inspections can be done by trained technicians, and some towns may require a PE to sign certain reports, which is unverified.
- Proprietary manufactured-treatment devices (e.g., hydrodynamic separators) typically need manufacturer-specified maintenance, which creates recurring cleanouts. This is an inference and was not sourced here.
- Overlap with the parking-lot sweeping, catch-basin cleaning and line-striping customer base supports bundling.

### Gaps
- No named MA private-BMP maintenance firms, pricing, or national roll-up examples were sourced.
- No count of private BMPs or O&M plans on file statewide.
- The specific bylaw language that compels private owners to submit annual reports (versus plan-conditioned requirements) varies by town and was not compiled.

## Q6. Commercial EV charger operations and maintenance (uptime-as-a-service)

### Takeaway
MA had about 10,900 public charging ports as of April 2026 and needs about 46,300 by 2030. Its own fleet has visible reliability problems: Turnpike chargers were removed, and the MBTA signed a 2026 legacy-charger maintenance contract. ChargerHelp's subscription "Reliability as a Service" proves the national model. Demand is growing and recurring, but electrical licensing is needed and per-port revenue is small.

### Cited Findings
- MA had **10,867 public EV charging ports as of April 1, 2026**, and ports grew about 50% between August 2023 and May 2025. I saw this figure only as a search extract of a mass.gov slide deck and could not confirm which deck. [Mass.gov Drive Electric symposium slides](https://www.mass.gov/doc/drive-electric-massachusetts-symposium-slides/download); [EVICC Sept 9, 2026 deck](https://www.mass.gov/doc/evicc-meeting-deck-september-9-2026/download)
- The Second EVICC Assessment counted more than 9,400 public ports (May 2025). It estimates about **46,300 ports needed by 2030 and 105,000 by 2035**, and says the deployment pace must roughly triple. [EVICC Assessment Executive Summary](https://www.mass.gov/doc/1-executive-summary/download)
- The EVICC September 9, 2026 deck notes nearly 350 Level 2 chargers across about 25 MBTA lots (a 2023 USDOT $14.4M grant). It also lists a plan to upgrade 30 legacy chargers by the end of FY27 and **a 2026 maintenance contract for legacy chargers**. [EVICC deck Sept 2026](https://www.mass.gov/doc/evicc-meeting-deck-september-9-2026/download)
- MassDOT permanently removed six unreliable Turnpike rest-stop chargers [Acadia Center](https://acadiacenter.org/massdot-removing-six-unreliable-turnpike-ev-chargers/). In 2022, senators complained that Pike chargers had been broken for over a year. [State House News via AgencyChecklists, June 2022](https://agencychecklists.com/2022/06/10/senators-want-turnpike-ev-chargers-fixed-59657/)
- National: J.D. Power found about 1 in 5 public charging attempts failed, with equipment failures causing 62% of failures due to out-of-service stations. This is a secondary newsletter citing J.D. Power. [EVPowerPulse](https://evpowerpulse.beehiiv.com/p/43-charging-reliability-declines)
- Proven model: ChargerHelp! (Los Angeles) raised a $17.5M Series A in February 2023, led by Blue Bear with Exelon participating. It sells annual-fee "Reliability as a Service" with certified EVSE technicians, pays technicians at least $30/hr, and targets the NEVI 97% uptime requirement. [ChargerHelp news](https://www.chargerhelp.com/posts/17-5m-series-a-will-support-growth-essential-to-ev-infrastructure.html); [ImpactAlpha](https://impactalpha.com/chargerhelp-scores-17-5-million-to-repair-ev-chargers-and-create-inclusive-green-jobs/)

### Inferences
- Target customers are site hosts (property managers, municipalities, dealerships, fleet depots, workplaces) that bought chargers with Mass Save or utility incentives and lack O&M contracts. The model is preventive maintenance and first-line repair, plus software monitoring for uptime. **Owner-not-operator feasibility is medium-high.** Electrical repair work in MA requires licensed electricians under c.141 (not verified in this session), so the business must employ or subcontract licensed journeymen or masters.
- Revenue per port is low, so density (multi-port sites, fleet depots, municipal portfolios) and OEM/CPO warranty-service contracts matter. Public contracts (MBTA, municipalities) may trigger prevailing wage.

### Gaps
- No MA-specific uptime data, and no list of MA charger O&M providers.
- No pricing for per-port O&M contracts.
- Whether MA or utility incentive programs require uptime or maintenance contracts was not verified.

## Q7. Facilities and operations services: snow and ice, micro-markets, mobile fleet washing, document destruction, lab and commercial cleaning

### Takeaway
Of these, **commercial snow and ice management** (certain demand, but MA liability and insurance stress) and **micro-markets** (route-based, owner-friendly) are the most viable owner-not-operator plays. **Mobile fleet washing** is proven (Fleet Clean, about $1.09M average unit revenue) but lacks MA evidence. **Document destruction** has a clear MA legal trigger (c.93I) but no evidence of undersupply. **Lab and cleanroom cleaning** is weakened by Greater Boston lab vacancy near 29%. Generic commercial cleaning is commodity and carries MA independent-contractor (Coverall) risk.

### Cited Findings
**Snow and ice management**
- State House News (May 2025) reported:
  - MA snow contractors are pushing for Sen. Tarr's **S 1370** to regulate snow and ice contracts. It was reported favorably in 2022 and 2024 but stalled in Senate Ways and Means both times.

## Q9. MA labor and legal considerations affecting owner-not-operator and subcontractor models

### Takeaway
MA's labor costs are high, but the rules are stable for 2026: a $15 minimum wage and a 0.88% PFML contribution. The binding constraint for "subcontracted delivery" is the **ABC test (c.149 §148B)**. Prong B, which requires the work to be outside the hiring firm's usual course of business, makes 1099 technicians performing the company's core service presumptively employees, with treble damages exposure. Plan for W-2 technicians, or subcontract only to genuinely independent licensed firms. Public-sector work brings prevailing wage.

### Cited Findings
- The MA minimum wage is **$15.00/hr in 2026**, unchanged since 2023, and the tipped cash minimum is $6.75. These are secondary sources; confirm with mass.gov. [AllVoices](https://www.allvoices.co/blog/massachusetts-labor-laws); [Slasify](https://slasify.com/en/blog/how-to-pay-employees-in-massachusetts-salary-bonuses-benefits)
- PFML 2026 [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/news-insights/massachusetts-employers-should-prepare-for-2026-paid-family-and-medical-leave-updates.html):
  - The total contribution is **0.88%** of eligible wages for the third straight year.
  - Employers with 25 or more covered individuals must pay at least 0.42%, which is 60% of the medical portion.
  - Employers with fewer than 25 have only the 0.46% employee share and no required employer share.
  - The maximum weekly benefit is $1,230.39.
- Independent-contractor rules:
  - c.149 §148B presumes employment unless all three prongs are met. Prong B, "outside the usual course," is the hardest. Misclassification that also violates wage laws carries treble damages plus fees. [Seyfarth "Mass-Peculiarities" 2022](https://content.seyfarth.com/publications/Mass-Peculiarities-2022/155)
  - In *Somers v. Converged Access* (SJC 2009), the court held that the parties' intent or contract labels do not matter. [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2009/10/massachusetts-high-court-decision-highlights-perils-of-misclassifying-workers)
  - Courts applied the test to janitorial franchising in *Awuah v. Coverall* (2010). [Lathrop GPM](https://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/massachusetts-federal-court-compares-franchising-to-a-ponzi-scheme-and-labels-franchisees-as-employees)
- Prevailing wage (c.149 §§26–27): DLS sets rates from collective-bargaining patterns. Municipal fire alarm testing and maintenance contracts (Reading) and a state DMH FY2026–28 fire alarm inspection bid include prevailing wage. [Reading Post](https://thereadingpost.com/2022/03/02/fire-alarm-testing-inspection-maintenance-and-repair-services-contract-contract-no-22-31); [Mass.gov DMH bid package 2026-005](https://www.mass.gov/doc/2026-005-bid-package/download); [Mass.gov DLS opinion letters](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/prevailing-wage-opinion-letters). A vendor guide says there is no small-job exemption, which is unverified. [Miter](https://www.miter.com/resources/prevailing-wage-rates-massachusetts/)

### Inferences
- For every candidate here, a technician performing the company's core service (inspection, cleaning, testing) is squarely within "usual course of business." Those technicians must be W-2. Subcontracting is safest for **distinct licensed trades** the company does not hold itself out as performing, such as a licensed plumber for backflow repairs, a vac-truck hauler for grease and BMP cleanouts, or an electrician for EV repairs. The subcontractor must be an established business with its own customers. This is an inference, and counsel should confirm.
- Fully loaded MA technician cost is roughly the wage plus FICA, FUTA/SUTA, workers' comp, the 0.42% PFML share for firms with 25+ employees, and the MA Health Insurance Responsibility Disclosure/EMAC. EMAC was not sourced here. Pricing should assume skilled technicians at $30–$50/hr base, consistent with the ChargerHelp ≥$30/hr and NICET postings of $40–$52/hr cited above (non-MA).
- Municipal contracts (backflow, fire alarm, EV, stormwater) are steady accounts but bring prevailing-wage administration and certified payroll.

### Gaps
- MA 2026 EMAC rates, workers' comp rates by class, and MA SUTA new-employer rate were not retrieved.
- The 2024 DLS fire alarm opinion's conclusion on whether pure *testing and inspection* is covered was not read in full.

## Q10. Discarded or deprioritized candidates and why

### Takeaway
**Elevator inspection** is discarded because inspections are performed by state inspectors. The backlog is a public-sector capacity issue that private firms cannot fill under current law. **Lab cleanroom cleaning, document destruction, generic commercial cleaning and remote professional services** are deprioritized for lack of MA-specific undersupply evidence or for structural headwinds.

### Cited Findings
- A CBS Boston I-Team investigation found about 4,500 elevators operating with expired certificates, including at Boston City Hall, Logan Airport and the inspection office's own building. The report is undated. [CBS Boston](https://www.cbsnews.com/boston/news/elevators-expired-inspection-certificates-i-team-investigation). WWLP reported 68 state elevator inspectors. [WWLP](https://digital-release.wwlp.com/?p=114249)
- A 2014 State Auditor report found 14,211 of 39,461 registered elevators (36%) had expired certificates as of October 10, 2012. [Mass.gov Auditor press release](https://www.mass.gov/news/bump-finds-elevator-inspection-backlog-in-public-safety-audit)
- Bills to change elevator inspection rules (2013–2018) were not enacted. [MA Legislature H.1232](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/190/H1232); [H.2084](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/189/H2084)
- Lab vacancy was 28.7% in Q2 2026 (see Q7). [CBRE Q2 2026](https://mktgdocs.cbre.com/2299/e4f28043-8595-495e-977c-3386651e76e9-1889712718/Q2_2026_Boston_Metro_Life_Scie.pdf)

### Inferences
- The elevator backlog benefits elevator **maintenance** firms (whose mechanics accompany inspectors) only indirectly. Those firms are union-heavy and dominated by OEMs (Otis, Schindler, KONE, TK Elevator), which is unsourced background. Not a fit.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether MA has authorized private or third-party elevator inspectors since 2019.
