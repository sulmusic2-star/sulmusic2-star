## 1. Business-acquisition market in MA: volume, prices, multiples, and the "silver tsunami"

### Takeaway
Massachusetts-specific closed-deal data is thin. The best available MA figure is a Boston-metro *asking* snapshot (676 listings, $499K median ask, $200K median cash flow), but its date is uncertain. Nationally, small service businesses sell for about 2.5–2.7x seller's discretionary earnings (SDE); the 2025 service-sector median price was $340K. Multiples rise steeply with size: about 2.0x SDE under $500K of price, about 3.1x SDE at $1–2M, and about 4–6x EBITDA above $2M. The supply of retiring sellers is real: owners aged 55+ hold 53% of MA small businesses (Project Equity via Boston Globe, 2023).

### Inferences
- The Boston-metro $499K median ask with $200K median cash flow implies an asking multiple of about 2.5x. That is close to national closed multiples, so Boston asking prices do not look obviously inflated relative to cash flow. But the figure is asking-side and its date is unconfirmed.
- For a hands-off buyer the size jump matters most. Businesses under $1M of price (about 2–2.8x SDE) are cheap precisely because the SDE *includes the owner's labor*. Businesses priced above $2M are valued on EBITDA *after* management cost, at about 4–6x. A hands-off buyer is effectively buying "EBITDA after a GM," so the cheap small-deal multiple is partly illusory (see section 6).
- With 53% of MA small businesses held by owners 55+, and the 68,000 / 775,000-employee figure, there is a plausible MA pipeline of retiring-owner service companies. But only a fraction will be listed publicly, given 99 retirement-tagged MA listings on BizBuySell at one point in time. Off-market outreach (direct mail, CPAs, attorneys, trade associations) is likely necessary.
- The seller-financing mismatch (29% of sellers willing vs. 90% of buyers expecting) combines with the new SBA rules requiring seller notes to sit on full standby for the life of the loan. A buyer should expect to negotiate hard for seller paper and to structure it as full-standby or as an earnout.

- No BizBuySell service-subindustry multiples for 2026 (e.g., cleaning, HVAC, pest control, home health) were retrievable.
- The IBBA Market Pulse New England regional breakdown, if any exists, was not found.
- No MA-specific boomer-only share. The 53% figure covers age 55+ and comes from Project Equity via the Boston Globe; the primary Project Equity dataset was not accessed.

## 2. Financing: SBA 7(a) (2025 SOP 50 10 8 and new SOP 50 10 8.1 effective Oct 1, 2026), seller notes, down payments, MA programs

### Takeaway
For a full acquisition the buyer must inject at least 10% of total project costs. SOP 50 10 8 (June 1, 2025) and SOP 50 10 8.1 (issued Aug 14, 2026; effective for SBA loan numbers issued on or after Oct 1, 2026) cap "limited" sources at half of that 10%. Limited sources include seller notes on *full standby for the loan's life* and passive minority-investor equity. So at least 5% must come from the buyer's own cash or other unlimited sources. Under 8.1, debt-service coverage of at least 1.25x must be met on *historical* earnings; projections are no longer allowed. Massachusetts quasi-public lenders (MGCC, MassDevelopment) are supplements, not primary acquisition lenders. The MA SBDC provides free deal and loan-packaging advice but does not lend.

### Cited Findings

**SOP 50 10 8 (effective June 1, 2025)**
- SOP 50 10 8 took effect June 1, 2025 and requires a minimum 10% buyer equity injection for startups and changes of ownership. Before that SOP, seller notes could satisfy the 10% requirement. — [Whiteford, Taylor & Preston client alert](https://www.whitefordlaw.com/news-events/client-alert-sba-issues-sop-50-10-8-key-changes-impacting-sba-7a-lending)
- Under SOP 50 10 8, seller debt counts toward the injection only if it is on **full standby for the life of the SBA loan** (no principal or interest payments), not merely 24 months, and it may not exceed **half** of the required injection (about 5% of project cost). — [Clearly Acquired](https://www.clearlyacquired.com/blog/major-sop-50-10-8-sba-changes-effective-june-1-2025----what-they-mean-for-business-buyers); [Kumo](https://www.withkumo.com/blog/top-things-to-know-about-the-june-1-2025-us-small-business-administration-sba-significant-changes-standard-operating-procedure-sop-50-10-8)

**SOP 50 10 8.1 (issued Aug 14, 2026; effective Oct 1, 2026). Secondary summaries; SBA text not directly read**
- Issued via SBA Information Notice 5000-880695 (Aug 14, 2026). It applies to loans whose SBA loan number is issued on or after Oct 1, 2026; loans numbered through Sept 30 stay under SOP 50 10 8. — [Analytics.loan](https://www.analytics.loan/post/sop-50-10-8-1-for-lenders); [Funder Intel (Sept 2026)](https://www.funderintel.com/post/sba-sop-50-10-8-1-7-things-brokers-need-to-know-before-october-1)
- Change-of-ownership rules moved to a new **Appendix 15**, with four categories:
  - **Initial Acquisition** (default for a first-time buyer): 10% injection that *cannot be reduced*, 1.25x coverage, and a Quality of Earnings report required if the business purchase price is $3M or more.
  - **Business Expansion**: an operator with at least 2 fiscal years buying 100% of a same-NAICS business; 1.15x coverage; injection may be reduced.
  - **Owner Buyout**.
  - **ESOP/Co-op**: injection exempt.
  — [Analytics.loan](https://www.analytics.loan/post/sop-50-10-8-1-for-lenders)
- Historical coverage test: the 1.25x floor (Initial Acquisition) is measured on the last fiscal year or the average of the last two, historical or adjusted. "Projections may be reviewed but cannot be used to meet the floor." Debt service includes seller debt not on full standby, and interest-only seller notes are amortized over at most 10 years for the test. — [Analytics.loan](https://www.analytics.loan/post/sop-50-10-8-1-for-lenders); Funder Intel notes the prior standard was 1.15x with projections allowed. — [Funder Intel](https://www.funderintel.com/post/sba-sop-50-10-8-1-7-things-brokers-need-to-know-before-october-1)
- **Limited equity sources** (together capped at half the required injection): standby debt, seller debt on full standby, and equity from non-controlling minority investors (under 20%, no control). Minority investors' distributions are locked, except tax distributions, until the 7(a) loan is repaid. **Unlimited sources**: unborrowed cash, personal loans to guarantors repaid from non-business sources (owner salary does not qualify), and grants without clawback. — [Analytics.loan](https://www.analytics.loan/post/sop-50-10-8-1-for-lenders)
- Worked example: a $2.5M project needs a $250K injection, at least $125K of which must come from unlimited sources. — [Funder Intel](https://www.funderintel.com/post/sba-sop-50-10-8-1-7-things-brokers-need-to-know-before-october-1)
- Other 8.1 changes:
  - The business portion of an acquisition loan is capped at 10-year amortization.
  - Total debt is capped at the appraised business value.
  - Seller notes must be in place 36 months (was 24) before refinancing.
  - **Sellers may remain as consultants up to 24 months (was 12).**
  - Trusts must guarantee at any ownership percentage.
  - "Small loan" streamlined underwriting no longer applies to any change-of-ownership deal, including those under $350K.
  — [Funder Intel](https://www.funderintel.com/post/sba-sop-50-10-8-1-7-things-brokers-need-to-know-before-october-1); [Analytics.loan](https://www.analytics.loan/post/sop-50-10-8-1-for-lenders)
- Funder Intel's reading of 8.1 found **no explicit active-owner requirement**. — [Funder Intel](https://www.funderintel.com/post/sba-sop-50-10-8-1-7-things-brokers-need-to-know-before-october-1)
- Conflicting commentary exists on whether seller notes still count under 8.1. Lender-oriented consensus is that they do when on full standby and within the 50% cap. — [Searchfunder discussion](https://searchfunder.com/post/new-sop-changes-sop-50-10-81); [Pioneer Capital Advisory](https://www.pioneercapitaladvisory.com/post/investor-equity-sba-7a-sop-50-10-8-1-new-rules-vs-old-rules)

It lends to businesses that traditional lenders won't finance, using term loans, lines of credit, partial guarantees, over-advances, and contract financing. — [mass.gov MGCC overview](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/overview-of-the-massachusetts-growth-capital-corporation) (via search excerpt)

- **MA Small Business Development Center Network**: no-cost, confidential one-to-one advising for prospective and existing businesses, including "buying or selling a business," cash-flow analysis, and loan packaging. "The Massachusetts SBDC does not provide financing." Six regional offices; a Request for Advising form is required first. — [Salem State SBDC services](https://salemstate.edu/academics/centers/small-business-development-center/small-business-development-center-services); [MSBDC network](https://www.msbdc.org/network.html); [MSBDC About](https://www.msbdc.org/aboutus.html)
- **"MBDC" (Massachusetts Business Development Corporation)**: the only result found was a historical (1956) account. No current acquisition-loan program under that name was verified. — [Dartmouth Alumni Magazine, 1956](https://archive.dartmouthalumnimagazine.com/article/1956/1/1/creator-of-jobs)

### Inferences
- Typical first-time capital stack under 8.1 for, say, a $1.2M service business (inference from the rules above):
  - SBA 7(a) loan: about 85–90% of price.
  - Buyer cash: at least 5% of project costs.
  - Up to 5% more from a full-standby seller note or passive (<20%) investors.
  - Plus closing costs, working capital, and a reserve. The buyer should plan on $150K–$200K liquid in total.
- Because 8.1 bans projections for the 1.25x test, a hands-off buyer cannot "underwrite" future cost savings or growth. The lender will normally deduct a market manager's salary from historical SDE when the buyer won't be the operator. A $300K-SDE business that needs a $150K GM typically fails 1.25x at a 2.8x price (see section 6 math).
- The extended 24-month seller consulting window under 8.1 is very useful for a hands-off buyer. The seller can stay on paid as a consultant for up to 2 years to transfer relationships and train the GM.
- MA quasi-public money (MGCC, MassDevelopment, 504) is best used for real estate, equipment, or working-capital top-ups, not as the core goodwill loan. MGCC's historic "no start-ups" stance also makes it less useful for path (c).

### Gaps
- The SBA's own SOP 50 10 8.1 text and Appendix 15 were not read directly. All 8.1 details come from secondary lender and consultant summaries (Analytics.loan, Funder Intel). Verify before relying on them.
- No current MGCC product sheet, rates, or confirmation that MGCC will lend toward an acquisition (goodwill) was found. mass.gov returned 403.
- Current SBA 7(a) maximum loan size, rate caps, and FY2027 guaranty fees (Information Notice 5000-881797) were not retrieved.
- The SBA franchise-eligibility rules for path (b) (SBA Franchise Directory, which was eliminated or changed in recent SOPs) were not researched.

## 3. What it takes to be semi-absentee: GM cost, minimum business size, systems, and franchisee realities

### Takeaway
A competent GM in Massachusetts costs roughly $124K–$127K median base pay, before taxes, benefits, and a bonus. Broker rules of thumb put absentee-GM pay at $100K–$150K for $1–5M-revenue businesses. Practically, a service business needs about $400K+ of SDE before a GM's cost leaves enough to service acquisition debt. "Semi-absentee" franchise marketing claims 10–20 owner hours per week, but this is unsourced. The only franchisee survey data found (FBR via a 2015 magazine) shows most franchisees work 40–50+ hours. Credible operators recommend at least 20 hours per week in year 1 and say absenteeism must be "earned, not promised."

### Cited Findings

**GM cost in Massachusetts**
- BLS OEWS May 2024 (via WageDex): MA median for General & Operations Managers (SOC 11-1021) is **$123,850**, vs. $102,950 nationally; 105,350 jobs in MA. — [WageDex (BLS OEWS May 2024)](https://wagedex.com/jobs/general-and-operations-managers/massachusetts)
- BLS OEWS May 2025 (via education sites): MA median **$127,220** vs. $105,770 nationally. — [TradeCareerPath (citing BLS May 2025)](https://tradecareerpath.com/programs/online-business-and-management-degrees/massachusetts/)
- One municipal report (Marlborough, MA) lists a $152,210 *mean* for this occupation (local, not statewide). — [Marlborough EDC Wages Report 2024](https://marlboroughedc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Wages-Report-2024.pdf)
- Note: the BLS OEWS MA table (bls.gov) returned 403 to direct fetch, so these numbers were not read at the primary source.

**Business size needed to support a GM (practitioner estimates)**
- A broker on Searchfunder estimates GM pay for an absentee owner in an average market at **$65K–$100K for businesses under $1M** of revenue and **$100K–$150K for $1–5M businesses**. — [Searchfunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/unreasonably-low-owner-salary-for-ebitda-and-searcher-expectations) (forum post; one person's experience)
- Another Searchfunder practitioner argues for deducting about **$300K** from SDE to replace an active owner: about $150K for the replacement plus a similar amount for the owner's hidden roles. — [Searchfunder](https://www.searchfunder.com/post/why-you-should-substract-300000-from-sde-to-replace-an-active-owner) (opinion)
- "EBITDA is SDE minus the amount an investor would need to pay a professional manager to run the business." — [EightX](https://eightx.co/blog/what-is-sde-sellers-discretionary-earnings)

**Margins (sizing the business that can carry a GM)**
- Most residential home-services firms net 5–14%; top-quartile operators net 18% or more. A $3M company example ran 10–16% net depending on execution. — [search summary of home-services margin sources](https://www.applausehq.com/blog/profit-margins-in-home-services-how-top-companies-squeeze-more-revenue-from-every-job); [Lightning Path Partners](https://lightningpathpartners.com/blog/hvac-company-profit-margins)
- Every 10% of revenue shifted from demand service to maintenance agreements adds about 1–2 points of net margin (consultant claim). — [Lightning Path Partners](https://lightningpathpartners.com/blog/how-profitable-is-hvac-business)

**Semi-absentee franchise realities**
- FBR research (reported in Feb 2015): franchisees work an average of 40–50 hours per week across industries. Only 30% work under 40 hours, and 44% work more than 50 hours. — [Business View Magazine, Feb 2015, citing Franchise Business Review](https://businessviewmagazine.com/digital-magazines/feb-2015/files/assets/basic-html/page54.html) (old, secondhand)
- Franchise-consultant claims: semi-absentee owners can operate in 10–20 hours per week once running, with **25–40 hours per week during launch**. — [FranNet](https://frannet.com/resources/business-opportunity-2/discover-absentee-ownership); [FranChoice](https://www.franchoice.com/?p=1763) (industry marketing)
- "Semi-absentee" is "one of the most poorly defined terms in the industry": one brand may require 10 hours per week, another 20–25. — [Entrepreneur UK](https://uk.entrepreneur.com/franchises/how-to-own-a-franchise-as-a-side-hustle/487382)
- Franchise investor Erik Van Horn (Alloy podcast, about late 2025) recommends at least 20 hours per week for first-time franchisees, especially in year 1. He calls "set it and forget it" "a dangerous myth" and says semi-absentee ownership should be "earned, not promised." He also recommends a capital cushion for the first 12–24 months. — [Alloy Franchise blog](https://alloyfranchise.com/blog/why-most-franchisees-fail-at-semi-absentee-ownership/)
- Franchise consultants are usually paid by the franchisor on a closed deal, which creates a broker incentive. — [Investor Fuel](https://investorfuel.com/blog/semi-absentee-franchise-ownership/)
- Without an FDD Item 19, franchisors cannot make earnings claims outside the FDD under FTC rules. — [Budget Blinds franchise blog](https://franchise.budgetblinds.com/blog/pros--cons-of-a-semi-absentee-franchise/)
- Critical franchisee account: Phoenix Franchise Brands franchisees alleged a "semi-absentee investor franchise" pitch, including that "the company would run the business for them," depleted retirement funds. They also alleged brokers received a $35,000 referral bounty. These are unverified franchisee allegations from about 2024. — [UnhappyFranchisee.com](https://unhappyfranchisee.com/semi-absentee-investor-franchise-is-a-scam/)

**Operating systems**
- Field-service software platforms such as ServiceTitan, Jobber, and Housecall Pro publish MA licensing guides aimed at trades operators, an indicator of the target market. — [Housecall Pro MA electrical licensing](https://www.housecallpro.com/licensing/electrical/massachusetts/)
- I found no independent data on how software adoption affects absentee-owner outcomes. Sources are vendor marketing.

### Inferences
- Loaded GM cost in MA (inference): the $124K–$127K median base, plus a 10–20% performance bonus, employer FICA (7.65%), PFML employer share (0.42% if 25+ covered workers), unemployment insurance, health insurance, and a vehicle comes to roughly **$150K–$180K all-in** for a capable GM. A smaller business might hire an "operations manager" at $85K–$110K, but promoting a lead technician is the most common failure point (section 5).
- A realistic hands-off layer for a $1–3M service business is a **GM, an office/dispatch manager or CSR, and field leads**, plus outsourced bookkeeping/CPA. The owner's role becomes weekly KPI review, monthly financial close review, GM coaching, banking, and capital decisions: about 5–10 hours per week *after* a 6–24-month transition. That is "no routine case work," not "passive."
- Semi-absentee franchise claims of 10–20 hours per week come almost entirely from parties paid on the sale. The only survey data found (FBR, 2015) contradicts them for typical franchisees. Treat the claim as a brand-specific, multi-unit, well-capitalized scenario, not a default.

## 4. Massachusetts labor and legal rules that shape the model

### Takeaway
Massachusetts is among the strictest US states for the "hands-off" playbook. Several rules work together:
- The three-prong ABC test makes most service workers employees. Prong 2, "outside the usual course of the business," effectively bars 1099 technicians or cleaners doing the company's core service.
- Prevailing wage-law plaintiffs get *mandatory* treble damages.
- Corporate officers and managers are *personally* liable.

Payroll costs are layered: $15 minimum wage (unchanged since 2023), PFML at 0.88%, paid sick time at 11+ employees, and mandatory workers' comp. The 2018 noncompete law protects the buyer against the *seller* (sale-of-business carve-out). It sharply limits noncompetes on retained staff: no noncompetes for hourly/nonexempt workers, a 12-month cap, and garden-leave or other consideration required. Licensed trades need a licensed "Licensee of Record" (e.g., a master electrician), so a non-licensed owner must employ or partner with one.

### Cited Findings

**ABC test (M.G.L. c. 149, §148B). Primary statute text**
- An individual performing any service "shall be considered to be an employee … unless:
  - (1) the individual is free from control and direction in connection with the performance of the service, both under his contract for the performance of service and in fact; and
  - (2) the service is performed outside the usual course of the business of the employer; and,
  - (3) the individual is customarily engaged in an independently established trade, occupation, profession or business of the same nature as that involved in the service performed."
  — [M.G.L. c. 149 §148B](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section148B)
- Failure to withhold taxes or pay UI or workers' comp premiums "shall not be considered" in the determination. — [§148B(b)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section148B)
- Misclassification triggers "all of the criminal and civil remedies, including debarment" (via §27C), and workers'-comp penalties under c. 152 §14. "**Any entity and the president and treasurer of a corporation and any officer or agent having the management of the corporation or entity shall be liable** for violations of this section." — [§148B(d)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section148B)
- Private right of action: an employee may sue for a violation of §148B (among others) 90 days after filing with the Attorney General (or sooner with written AG assent), within 3 years. A prevailing employee "**shall be awarded treble damages, as liquidated damages**, for any lost wages and other benefits and shall also be awarded the costs of the litigation and reasonable attorneys' fees." — [M.G.L. c. 149 §150](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section150)
- AG civil citations of up to $7,500 per violation ($25,000 for repeat or intentional violations) are reported by a compliance vendor and are unverified against statute. — [Teambridge (Apr 2026)](https://www.teambridge.com/compliance/massachusetts/ago-fair-labor-division-enforcement)

**Minimum wage**
- $15.00 per hour since Jan 1, 2023 (final step of the 2018 "grand bargain"), unchanged in 2026. Tipped service rate is $6.75. A bill (S.1349) to raise it was pending but not enacted as of May 2026. — [OnPay 2026 MA minimum wage](https://onpay.com/insights/minimum-wage-by-state-summary/massachusetts/); [Yahoo/Patriot Ledger](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/minimum-wage-2026-massachusetts-know-100954543.html)

**Paid Family & Medical Leave (PFML), 2026**
- The total contribution stays at **0.88%** of eligible wages for employers with 25+ covered individuals. That breaks down as 0.70% medical (employer 0.42%, employee up to 0.28%) and 0.18% family (up to 100% withheld from employee). Employers with fewer than 25 covered individuals remit 0.46% (all employee-withholdable, no required employer share). The maximum weekly benefit for 2026 is $1,230.39. — [Seyfarth 2026 MA PFML update](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/2026-massachusetts-paid-family-and-medical-leave-updates-benefit-amount-contribution-rates-and-taxability-of-benefit-payments.html); [NFP](https://www.nfp.com/insights/2026-massachusetts-pfml-rates-and-benefits-released/)
- Reported 2027 change (single secondary source, July 30, 2026): for employers with 25+ employees from Jan 1, 2027, employers pay 100% of the medical contribution (was 60%). Family leave would shift to 60% employer / 40% employee (was up to 100% employee). No rates were given and no DFML citation was provided, so this is **unverified**. — [FNA Insurance (July 30, 2026)](https://www.fnainsurance.com/resources/insights/Insight-News/Massachusetts-to-Make-Contribution-Changes-to-its-PFML-Program-for-2027/)

**Earned sick time (M.G.L. c. 149 §148C). Primary statute**
- "All employees employed by an employer of eleven or more employees shall be entitled to earn and use up to 40 hours of earned paid sick time … in a calendar year." Employees of smaller employers are entitled to up to 40 hours of *unpaid* earned sick time. — [M.G.L. c. 149 §148C](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section148C)
- §148C is among the sections enforceable through the treble-damages private right of action in §150. — [M.G.L. c. 149 §150](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section150)

**Workers' compensation (M.G.L. c. 152 §25A). Primary statute**
- "In order to promote the health, safety and welfare of employees, **every employer** shall provide for the payment to his employees of the compensation provided for by this chapter" through insurance, a self-insurance group, or a self-insurer license. — [M.G.L. c. 152 §25A](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter152/Section25A)

**Noncompetition Agreement Act (M.G.L. c. 149 §24L, effective Oct 1, 2018). Primary statute**
- Excluded from the Act's limits are "noncompetition agreements made in connection with the sale of a business entity or substantially all of the operating assets … when the party restricted … is a significant owner of, or member or partner in, the business entity who will receive significant consideration or benefit from the sale." The Act also excludes nonsolicitation-of-employees and nonsolicitation-of-customers covenants, NDAs, and garden-leave clauses. — [M.G.L. c. 149 §24L](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section24L)
- For covered employee noncompetes, the restricted period may not exceed **12 months** (2 years if the employee breached fiduciary duty or took property). The geographic reach must be reasonable. — [§24L](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section24L)
- Noncompetes are **unenforceable against**:
  - employees classified as **nonexempt under the FLSA**;
  - students in short-term employment;
  - employees **terminated without cause or laid off**;
  - employees aged 18 or younger.
  — [§24L(c)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section24L)
- Garden leave must be at least 50% of the employee's highest annualized base salary in the prior two years, paid for the whole restricted period, unless "other mutually agreed upon consideration" is specified. — [Mass Bar summary](https://www.massbar.org/publications/section-review/section-review-article/section-review-2018-november-december-2018/massachusetts-s-new-non-compete-law-an-overview); [Winston & Strawn](https://www.winston.com/en/insights-news/massachusetts-passes-law-limiting-non-compete-agreements)

**Licensing that requires a licensed individual**
- Electrical: a master electrician license is required to serve as the **Licensee of Record** for an electrical contracting business. Master requirements: 1 year as a licensed journeyman, 150 extra education hours, and an exam. A Systems Contractor license (low-voltage, fire alarm, security) requires 1 year as a systems technician, 75 hours of education, and an exam. — [Workiz MA electrician license guide](https://www.workiz.com/blog/electrician/massachusetts-electrician-license/); [Skillplum](https://skillplum.com/license-requirements/electrical/massachusetts)
- The Board of State Examiners of Electricians' business forms must be signed by the Licensee of Record, with the corporate clerk or LLC manager certifying officers. The Board also has forms for adding or removing the master/systems contractor associated with a company. — [mass.gov: master electrician/systems contract with company form](https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2016/07/nt/master-elect-n-systems-contract-with-company.pdf); [removal form](https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2016/07/vh/master-elect-n-systems-contract-company-removal.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The ABC test is decisive for model design.** Prong 2 means a cleaning, HVAC, home-care, or pest-control company cannot treat its technicians or cleaners as 1099 contractors, because they perform the company's usual business. Many national "proven models" built on contractor labor (gig-style cleaning and handyman marketplaces, some home-care registries) must be rebuilt on W-2 labor in MA. That raises cost by roughly payroll taxes, PFML, comp, and sick time, but it is unavoidable.
- **Personal liability for absentee owners.** Because §148B(d) makes the "president and treasurer" and managing officers liable, and §150 mandates treble damages, a hands-off owner cannot delegate wage-and-hour compliance away. The owner needs a payroll provider, an annual compliance audit, and timekeeping controls as owner-level duties.
- **Buying a business and retaining staff:**
  - The *seller's* noncompete is enforceable outside §24L's limits under the sale-of-business carve-out.
  - Most service-business staff (technicians, cleaners, CSRs) are FLSA-nonexempt, so **noncompetes against them are unenforceable**. The buyer must rely on nonsolicitation and NDA covenants (outside the Act) plus retention bonuses and stay agreements.
  - A GM who is exempt can sign a 12-month noncompete with garden leave or other stated consideration.
  - Also inference: in an asset purchase, employees are technically terminated and rehired. Noncompetes are void for employees "terminated without cause or laid off," so get counsel on structuring transfers.
- **Licensed trades.** For electrical (and likely plumbing, gas, HVAC refrigeration, and others), a non-licensed owner must have a licensed master on staff or as a partner as Licensee of Record. Losing that person can halt licensed work. This is key-person risk to mitigate with redundancy (two masters), retention equity or phantom equity, and a written license-of-record agreement.
- MA payroll-cost stack per W-2 employee (inference): $15 minimum wage (market wages for skilled trades are much higher), FICA 7.65%, PFML employer 0–0.42%, state UI, workers' comp premiums (which vary sharply by class code; roofing and HVAC far exceed cleaning or office), and paid sick time at 11+ employees.

### Gaps
- Current AG Fair Labor Division guidance documents (mass.gov) were blocked (403). Per-violation civil-citation amounts were not verified against statute (c. 149 §27C).
- MA licensing qualifier rules for plumbing/gas fitting, HVAC/refrigeration, Home Improvement Contractor registration, Construction Supervisor License, home-care agency licensure (newly enacted MA licensing), pest control, and childcare were not researched.
- MA workers' comp rate levels by class code (WCRIBMA) were not retrieved.
- Whether the 2027 PFML split change has been formally adopted by DFML is unconfirmed.
- MA franchise-law specifics (whether MA requires franchise registration) were not researched.

## 5. Failure modes: why absentee owners fail in service businesses, and mitigations

### Takeaway
No systematic dataset isolates absentee-owner failure rates. Credible accounts point to a consistent set of failure modes:
- undercapitalization;
- hiring or promoting the wrong manager;
- loss of control and visibility;
- embezzlement by trusted office or practice managers;
- the GM leaving to compete with key staff;
- broker over-promising.

Acquisition data from search funds (the most rigorous ETA dataset) shows meaningful loss rates even among *full-time*, MBA-trained, investor-backed operators.

### Cited Findings
- A California business attorney has seen this pattern "perhaps three dozen times": the owner is there less and less, the manager becomes "the face seen by vendors, employees and customers," then the manager leaves for a competing business, sometimes after "meeting with key employees to discuss the new venture for months or even years." — [Stimmel Law, "Absentee Owners: Problems and Solutions"](https://stimmel-law.com/articles/absentee-owners-problems-and-solutions-part-one-stating-problem/)
- Veterinary practice: the owner learned five years after buying the practice that his long-time practice manager had funneled over $200,000. He noted that small businesses with a "family" atmosphere are the most susceptible to employee theft. — [DVM360, "Life after embezzlement" (2010)](https://www.dvm360.com/view/life-after-embezzlement)
- A funeral home employee admitted stealing $275,000, described as "absentee ownership gone bad"; the business nearly faced foreclosure over back taxes. — [Fox6 News](https://www.fox6now.com/news/contact-6-one-bad-employee-nearly-got-everyone-fired)
- A Maryland office manager with "full responsibility of managing the finances of the business for 16 years" was convicted of large-scale theft. The owners depleted personal savings and took loans to make payroll. — [Queen Anne's County State's Attorney](https://qacstatesattorney.com/?p=1955)
- The semi-absentee franchise failure list (Alloy/Erik Van Horn, about late 2025): undercapitalization and overconfidence; hiring the wrong manager; poor communication and control; brokers over-promising; inadequate franchisor support. No statistics were cited. — [Alloy Franchise](https://alloyfranchise.com/blog/why-most-franchisees-fail-at-semi-absentee-ownership/)
- Stanford GSB search fund study, 2024 edition (data through 2023): 681 first-time US/Canada search funds since 1984, aggregate pre-tax IRR 35.1%, ROI 4.5x. — [search summary citing Stanford GSB](https://www.peony.ink/blog/search-fund-guide). Secondary summaries say about 30–37% of searchers fail to acquire, and about 31–40% of acquirers lose money or fail to return full equity; the editions and figures vary. — [INSEAD Knowledge](https://knowledge.insead.edu/entrepreneurship/help-entrepreneurs-who-want-buy-business); [Chenmark via Searchfunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/chenmark-weekly-thoughts-the-stanford-search-fund-study-1)
- 2026 Stanford edition (data through 2025): 862 funds, aggregate IRR 33.9%, ROI 4.75x, 58% acquisition rate since 1996 (secondary summary). — [search summary](https://www.peony.ink/blog/search-fund-guide)
- A Yale working paper argues that individual investors did not replicate the search-fund index. One analysis puts the aggregate near 2.8x after removing 10x+ outliers. — [Searchfunder discussion of Yale study](https://searchfunder.com/post/what-are-your-thoughts-on-yales-recent-study)
- Unverified franchisee allegations: a semi-absentee "investor franchise" pitch led to depleted retirement funds. — [UnhappyFranchisee.com](https://unhappyfranchisee.com/semi-absentee-investor-franchise-is-a-scam/)
- Practitioners recommend incentivizing a good manager "to run the operations with the same level of rigor and passion," plus structural controls. — [Searchfunder absentee-ownership thread](https://www.searchfunder.com/post/does-anyone-have-experience-running-a-business-in-absentee-ownership)

### Inferences (mitigations synthesized from the failure modes above)
1. **Don't be absent at the start.** Plan 6–24 months of heavy owner involvement as "owner-manager of the manager," even in path (a). Use the seller's extended consulting window (up to 24 months under SOP 8.1).
2. **Separation of financial duties.** The owner, not the GM, controls bank logins and approves payees. Use a third-party bookkeeper or CPA, review the monthly bank reconciliation yourself, require dual approval on new vendors, and buy employee-dishonesty (crime) insurance. Each theft case above involved one trusted person controlling the money.
3. **GM economics aligned with yours.** Pay base salary plus a bonus on EBITDA (not revenue), with phantom equity or profits interest vesting over 3–5 years. Add a 12-month noncompete with garden leave or stated consideration for an exempt GM (§24L), plus customer and employee nonsolicitation.
4. **Bench depth.** Hire a second-in-command or office manager and keep two license holders in licensed trades, so the GM leaving doesn't take the business with them.
5. **KPI cadence.** Run a weekly dashboard covering leads, booking rate, average ticket, gross margin by job, technician utilization, AR days, reviews, and callbacks/rework, plus a monthly close within 15 days. Field-service software (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro) makes this visible remotely.
6. **Capitalization.** Hold a 6–12-month operating reserve beyond the SBA injection.
7. **Discount broker and franchisor time claims.** Interview 10+ existing franchisees who operate semi-absentee in high-cost markets, and read FDD Item 19, Item 20 (turnover and closures), and Item 21.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of absentee-owned vs. owner-operated service-business failure or survival rates was found.
- No franchise-level data was found comparing semi-absentee franchisee outcomes (e.g., SBA loan charge-off rates by brand) with owner-operators.
- Primary Stanford GSB search-fund reports were not read. Figures come from secondary summaries with some inconsistency.

## 6. Realistic owner income: semi-absentee service businesses at $1M–$3M revenue in MA-like high-cost markets

### Takeaway
No source reports hands-off owner income for $1–3M MA service businesses directly. Working from cited margins, multiples, GM costs, and the SOP 8.1 rules gives an illustrative result. A $2M-revenue service business with about $400K SDE, bought at about 3.0x SDE with an SBA loan and a $150K GM, leaves the hands-off owner only about **$75K–$80K per year pre-tax** during the 10-year loan, before capex and taxes. Owner income becomes meaningful (about $230K–$250K) after the loan is paid or through growth. A sub-$300K-SDE business generally **cannot carry both a GM and SBA debt** under the 1.25x historical test.

### Cited Findings (inputs)
- $1M–$2M deals price at about 3.1x SDE; $500K–$1M deals at about 2.8x; under $500K at about 2.0x (IBBA Q2 2026, approximate chart read). — [IBBA Q2 2026 Highlights](https://www.ibba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/mp-highlights-q2-2026.pdf)
- SBA 8.1: 10% injection, 1.25x historical DSCR, 10-year amortization cap on the business portion, and seller debt not on full standby counted in debt service. — [Analytics.loan](https://www.analytics.loan/post/sop-50-10-8-1-for-lenders); [Funder Intel](https://www.funderintel.com/post/sba-sop-50-10-8-1-7-things-brokers-need-to-know-before-october-1)
- MA GM median about $124K–$127K base. — [WageDex (BLS May 2024)](https://wagedex.com/jobs/general-and-operations-managers/massachusetts); [TradeCareerPath (BLS May 2025)](https://tradecareerpath.com/programs/online-business-and-management-degrees/massachusetts/)
- Absentee GM pay of $100K–$150K for $1–5M businesses (broker estimate). — [Searchfunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/unreasonably-low-owner-salary-for-ebitda-and-searcher-expectations)
- Home-services EBITDA of about 17–24% (consultant) and net margins of 5–14% typical, 18%+ top quartile. — [Lightning Path Partners](https://lightningpathpartners.com/blog/plumbing-company-profit-margins); [Hook Agency (citing ACCA 2024)](https://hookagency.com/blog/hvac-profit-margin/)
- Boston-metro median listing cash flow of about $200K at a $499K median ask (date uncertain). — [BizBuySell data tables](https://www.bizbuysell.com/insight-report-data-tables/) (via search excerpt)

### Inferences (illustrative math, author's calculation; interest rates are assumptions, not sourced)
Assumptions: 10% equity; the rest is an SBA 7(a) loan amortized over 10 years at an assumed 9.5% or 10.5% all-in rate; no seller note in debt service; GM cost is fully loaded; excludes capex, taxes, working capital, and closing costs.

| Scenario | SDE | Price (multiple) | Equity (10%) | Loan | Annual debt service (9.5% / 10.5%) | GM loaded cost | EBITDA after GM | DSCR | Owner pre-tax cash after debt service |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: about $1.5–2.5M revenue | $400K | $1.20M (3.0x) | $120K | $1.08M | $168K / $175K | $150K | $250K | 1.49x / 1.43x | **about $75K–$82K** |
| B: about $2.5–3.5M revenue | $550K | $1.705M (3.1x) | $170K | $1.53M | $238K / $249K | $175K | $375K | 1.57x / 1.51x | **about $127K–$137K** |
| C: about $1–1.5M revenue | $300K | $840K (2.8x) | $84K | $756K | $117K / $122K | $150K | $150K | 1.28x / **1.23x (fails 1.25x)** | **about $28K–$33K** |

- Effective price on an absentee basis is **4.5x–5.6x EBITDA after GM**, versus the 2.8–3.1x SDE headline. The hands-off buyer is paying an owner-operator multiple for a business that must now carry a manager. Counter-measures: negotiate price on post-GM EBITDA; target businesses that *already* have a GM or operations manager (these command higher SDE multiples but less hidden cost); or plan to be the "GM" for 12–24 months before handing off.
- After the 10-year loan, scenario A owner income rises to about $250K pre-tax. Growth (price increases, maintenance-agreement revenue, adding technicians) is the main lever before then. Each technician or crew added typically carries the GM overhead better.
- Path comparison (inference):
  - **(a) Acquire a retiring owner's business.** Fastest to cash flow; SBA-financeable at 90%. The risks are owner dependence and staff and customer attrition at transition. Best fit when the target already has a second-in-command. MA-specific advantage: a 53% 55+ owner base.
  - **(b) Semi-absentee franchise.** Brand, playbook, and KPIs come pre-built, but it is a startup: ramp-up losses for 1–3 years, ongoing royalties and ad-fund fees (rates not researched here; check FDD Item 6), and the owner must still hire and fund a GM from day one, often before revenue supports it. Operator hours data contradicts the 10–20-hour marketing.
  - **(c) Independent manager-run startup.** Lowest purchase cost, but the hardest to finance (MGCC historically avoids start-ups; SBA requires 10% injection and lenders scrutinize startup projections). Highest execution risk. Hands-off is not realistic until revenue supports a GM, likely well past $1M of revenue at MA wages.
- Bottom-line inference: "no routine case work for the owner" is realistic in MA mainly via **(a) at about $400K+ SDE (roughly $1.5M+ revenue)** with a GM in place or installed during a paid seller transition, or via **multi-unit (b)** with heavy capitalization. In either case, expect 6–24 months of intensive management involvement and modest owner cash during loan payoff.

- Current SBA 7(a) interest rates (Prime plus spread) as of Oct 2026 were not retrieved. The 9.5% and 10.5% figures in the table are assumptions.
- MA-specific SDE margins by service industry were not found. National consultant margins were used.
