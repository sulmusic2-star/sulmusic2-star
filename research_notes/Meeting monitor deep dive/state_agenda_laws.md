# State agenda-posting laws and who controls clean-energy siting

Prepared 2026-10-10 for 351 Watch expansion planning. Companion CSV: `state_agenda_laws.csv` (50 rows, 27 columns, including full citations and source URLs for every row).

## Verification labels

- **V**: read this session on an official statute site, a state agency page, or Census data.
- **V (secondary)**: confirmed only through a secondary source, such as FindLaw, a law firm, a news outlet or the Sabin Center blog.
- **[K]**: from background knowledge and *not verified this session*. Treat as a lead, not a finding.

Every Northeast row cites a primary source for its open-meeting rule. Census counts are from the 2022 Census of Governments, table CG2200ORG02 (downloaded from www2.census.gov).

## Bottom line

**Massachusetts baseline:** notice 48 hours ahead, *excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays*, with a list of topics the chair reasonably anticipates. Posting at the clerk's office is enough; no website is required and no documents are required.

**Rules as strong as Massachusetts or stronger (lead time plus a required agenda):**
- **Stronger:** CA (72 h, agenda linked from the home page), TX (72 h, subject of each item, online for cities and counties), NV (3 working days, agenda, online, supporting material), HI (6 days), DE (7 days), NM (72 h agenda, website) and VA (3 working days, agenda on the website, packets public when members get them). Only CA, TX and VA are verified this session; the others are [K].
- **Comparable:**
  - **RI:** the closest match. 48 hours excluding weekends and holidays, a statement of business, and statewide electronic filing with the Secretary of State.
  - **VT:** 48-hour agenda posted on the website, counted in calendar hours.
  - **NJ:** 48 hours with the agenda "to the extent known"; website legal notices required from Mar 1, 2026.
  - **IL:** 48-hour agenda posted on the website.
  - **ID [K]:** 48-hour agenda.
- **Mixed:** NY has 72-hour notice, website posting and online documents 24 hours ahead, but no agenda requirement. PA posts its agenda online only 24 hours ahead, but cannot act on unlisted items.
- **Weaker:** NH (24 h, no agenda), CT (24 h), ME (no fixed time, no agenda), plus most of the Midwest and South (OH, MI and MN require no agenda at all).

**Pre-meeting documents:** only a few states require them.
- NY §103(e): records posted online 24 hours ahead "to the extent practicable".
- VA: packets public when members receive them.
- NJ MLUL 40:55D-10(b): application maps and documents on file 10 days before a land-use hearing, the strongest land-use rule found.
- CA §54957.5 and NV [K].

**Siting mostly local:**
- **Northeast:** ME, NH (<30 MW), RI (<40 MW, all BESS), NJ and PA are local. NY is local for under 25 MW and standalone BESS. MA is local for under 25 MW / 100 MWh, under a state-set 12-month consolidated permit.
- **Elsewhere:** VA, IN, IA, KS, MO, NE, GA, NC, SC, TN, CO, ID, UT, NM, AZ, LA, AL, AR, MS, MT, OK, DE and TX cities.
- **Hybrids where local hearings still decide most projects:** IL (county hearings under statewide caps), OH (OPSB, but counties can veto under SB 52), MI (local first, with the PA 233 state option) and CA (CEC opt-in).
- **State-controlled:** VT (PUC §248), CT (Siting Council over 1 MW), MD, MN and WI. FL leans state for solar (siting act plus solar as a permitted agricultural use) [K].

## Northeast detail

### Massachusetts (MA)
- **Open meetings:** M.G.L. c. 30A §20(b).
  - Notice 48 hours ahead, "excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays".
  - The notice lists "topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed".
  - §20(c): local bodies file with the clerk and post "conspicuously visible to the public at all hours".
  - No website or document requirement in the statute. The AG regulation (940 CMR 29.03) lets a municipal website serve as the posting method [K].
- **Town meeting:** c. 39 §10 requires warrant notice at least 7 days before the annual meeting and 14 days before a special one. The method is set by bylaw. Town meeting is not itself an OML body [K].
- **Land use:** c. 40A §11 requires a newspaper notice twice, the first at least 14 days before the hearing, 14 days of posting at town hall, and mailing to parties in interest within 300 ft.
- **Siting:**
  - St. 2024 c. 239 and 225 CMR 29.00 create a consolidated local permit with a 12-month clock and constructive approval for projects under 25 MW or 100 MWh. Towns must accept applications from Oct 1, 2026.
  - Larger projects go to the EFSB.
  - The AG has disapproved town BESS bans and moratoria (see `../Meeting monitor validation/tracker_sources.md`).
- **Sources:** [c.30A §20](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleIII/Chapter30A/Section20), [c.39 §10](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleVII/Chapter39/Section10), [c.40A §11](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleVII/Chapter40A/Section11).

### Maine (ME)
- **Open meetings:** 1 M.R.S. §406 requires notice "in ample time to allow public attendance", "disseminated in a manner reasonably calculated to notify the general public". There is no fixed lead time, no agenda requirement and no online requirement.
  - (A secondary site, UnGovr, lists "3 days". That figure is not in the statute.)
- **Town meeting:** 30-A M.R.S. §2523 requires the warrant to be posted at least 7 days before, "unless the town has adopted a different method of notification".
- **Siting:**
  - Home-rule local control, with no state preemption for solar or BESS.
  - DEP Site Law adds a state permit for projects over 20 acres.
  - LUPC zones the unorganized territories.
  - The DOER handbook (updated July 2026) notes "no uniform set of local or municipal regulations".
  - Public Law 2025 c. 430 (LD 1777) closed net energy billing to new non-rooftop projects. That slows the pipeline but leaves local control in place.
- **Sources:** [§406](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/1/title1sec406.html), [30-A §2523](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/30-A/title30-Asec2523.html), [DOER handbook](https://www.maine.gov/energy/sites/maine.gov.energy/files/inline-files/DOER%20Solar%20and%20Energy%20Storage%20Handbook%20for%20Maine%20Communities.pdf).

### New Hampshire (NH)
- **Open meetings:** RSA 91-A:2, II.
  - Notice 24 hours ahead, "excluding Sundays and legal holidays".
  - The notice gives time and place only; no agenda is required.
  - It goes in 2 places, one of which may be the website, or in a newspaper.
  - Minutes are due within 5 business days. Most recent amendment: 2025, 112:1.
- **Town meeting:**
  - RSA 39:5 requires the warrant to be posted at least 14 days before.
  - **New:** the town website must carry the warrant (on the home page or through a prominent link), effective **Oct 18, 2026** (2026, 341:2).
- **Planning board:** RSA 676:4, I(d) requires verified mail to abutters, plus public posting or publication, at least 10 days before.
- **Siting:**
  - The SEC (RSA 162-H:2) takes generation at 30 MW and up and renewables over 30 MW, with discretion over 5-30 MW projects.
  - Storage at 30 MW and up also goes to the SEC.
  - Everything smaller is local.
- **Sources:** [91-A:2](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/VI/91-A/91-A-2.htm), [39:5](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/III/39/39-5.htm), [676:4](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIV/676/676-4.htm), [162-H:2](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XII/162-H/162-H-2.htm).

### Vermont (VT)
- **Open meetings:** 1 V.S.A. §312(d).
  - The agenda goes up 48 hours before a regular meeting and 24 hours before a special one.
  - It is posted on the website if one exists, plus "in or near the municipal office and in at least two other designated public places".
  - It must have "sufficient details". Changes are made as the first act of business.
  - Minutes go on the website within 5 days. Amended in 2023-2025, most recently Act 51 of 2025.
- **Town meeting:** 17 V.S.A. §2641 requires the warning to be posted 30-40 days before, in a newspaper at least 5 days before (or the town report), and on the website if actively updated.
- **Development review:** 24 V.S.A. §4464 requires 15 days' notice for conditional use, variance and final plat hearings, and 7 days for other reviews.
- **Siting is state-controlled:**
  - 30 V.S.A. §248 requires a PUC certificate of public good for generation and storage.
  - 24 V.S.A. §4413(b) says bylaws "shall not regulate electric generation facilities, energy storage facilities, and transmission facilities" under §248.
  - Towns get party status and "substantial deference" for energy-compliant plans.
  - **Product note:** agendas still matter. Town plan amendments and select board or planning commission positions feed the PUC record.
- **Sources:** [§312](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/01/005/00312), [§2641](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/17/055/02641), [§4464](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/24/117/04464), [§4413](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/24/117/04413), [§248](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/30/005/00248).

### Rhode Island (RI)
- **Open meetings:** R.I. Gen. Laws §42-46-6.
  - Notice 48 hours ahead, "excluding weekends and state holidays in the count of hours".
  - The notice must include "a statement specifying the nature of the business to be discussed".
  - It is filed electronically with the Secretary of State and posted at the principal office plus one other prominent place.
  - Added items are informational only, with no vote except for emergencies.
  - This is the closest match to Massachusetts, and stronger online, because one state filing point covers every public body. That makes it the cheapest state to monitor (the SOS portal URL and API are not verified).
- **Siting:**
  - The EFSB (§42-98-3) takes generation at 40 MW and up. Storage is not in the definition.
  - Everything else is local. OER issued a non-binding BESS model ordinance in 2026.
- **Sources:** [§42-46-6](http://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE42/42-46/42-46-6.HTM), [§42-98-3](http://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE42/42-98/42-98-3.HTM), [OER BESS model ordinance](https://energy.ri.gov/energy-storage/bess-model-ordinance).

### Connecticut (CT)
- **Open meetings:** Conn. Gen. Stat. §1-225 (text from the CT FOI Commission site, amended through P.A. 22-109).
  - Regular-meeting agendas are filed "not less than twenty-four hours before". Local bodies file with the town clerk.
  - Unlisted business needs a two-thirds vote.
  - Special-meeting notices go on the website if available, 24 hours ahead.
  - The statute clearly requires regular agendas online only for state agencies.
- **Town meeting:** CGS §7-3 (via FindLaw) requires the warning at least 5 days before, on a signpost and in a newspaper.
- **Siting is state-controlled:**
  - The Siting Council has "exclusive jurisdiction over the location and type of facilities" (§16-50x(a)), covering any "electric generating or storage facility" (§16-50i(a)(3)).
  - Renewables of 1 MW or less are excluded (local).
  - Projects up to 65 MW go by petition for declaratory ruling.
  - Solar of 2 MW or more on prime farmland needs DoAg/DEEP sign-off.
  - The Council handles 5-18 MW BESS petitions.
- **Not verified:** cga.ct.gov returned 503 and TLS errors, so §8-7d (P&Z hearing notice) is unverified.
- **Sources:** [FOIC §1-225](https://portal.ct.gov/FOI/Regulations/The-FOI-Act/Sec-1225--Formerly-Sec-121--Meetings-of-government-agencies-to-be-public--Recording-of-votes--Schedu), [§16-50x](https://codes.findlaw.com/ct/title-16-public-service-companies/ct-gen-st-sect-16-50x/), [§16-50i](https://codes.findlaw.com/ct/title-16-public-service-companies/ct-gen-st-sect-16-50i/), [CSC Petition 1572](https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/csc/3_petitions-medialibrary/petitions_medialibrary/mediapetitionnos1501-1600/pe1572/decision_staffreport/pe1572-opn_final_a.pdf), [CSC Petition 1579 (5 MW BESS)](https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/csc/3_petitions-medialibrary/petitions_medialibrary/mediapetitionnos1501-1600/pe1579/proceduralcorrespondence/pe1579_townltr_energy-windsorlocks-a.pdf).

### New York (NY)
- **Open meetings, notice:** Public Officers Law §104.
  - 72 hours for meetings scheduled at least a week ahead; otherwise "a reasonable time".
  - Posted on the website if the body can.
  - No agenda is required [K: Committee on Open Government position].
- **Documents:** §103(e) requires records scheduled for discussion to be posted online "to the extent practicable at least twenty-four hours prior to the meeting", where the agency has a routinely updated website.
  - **Correction to the brief:** this provision dates from 2012 [K]. The 2022 amendment was §103-a, videoconferencing [K].
- **Siting:**
  - PSL Article VIII (the RAPID Act 2024 moved ORES into DPS) covers "major renewable energy facility[ies]" of 25 MW and up, including co-located storage (§137).
  - Municipalities cannot require approvals once an application is pending (§144).
  - ORES "may elect not to apply" local laws it finds "unreasonably burdensome" (§142(5)).
  - Projects under 25 MW and standalone BESS are local [K for BESS]. NY BESS moratoria are a major local-agenda topic.
- **Sources:** [§104](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PBO/104), [§103](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PBO/103), [PSL §137](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PBS/137), [§142](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PBS/142), [§144](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PBS/144).

### New Jersey (NJ)
- **Open meetings:** N.J.S.A. 10:4-8(d) requires "written advance notice of at least 48 hours, giving the time, date, location and, to the extent known, the agenda". It must say whether formal action may be taken. It is posted publicly, sent to 2 newspapers and filed with the clerk.
- **Website notices:**
  - §10:4-9.1 made electronic notice optional, and it cannot substitute for the other methods.
  - S-4654/A-5878 (signed June 30, 2025) requires legal notices on the entity's own website from Mar 1, 2026. Each entity's link goes in the NJ Dept. of State statewide directory.
  - **Product note:** the NJ directory is a ready-made source list.
  - Not verified: the P.L. chapter number, and exactly how the bill amends 10:4-8.
- **Land use (MLUL):**
  - 40:55D-12 requires notice at least 10 days before a development hearing (newspaper plus owners within 200 ft).
  - **40:55D-10(b)** requires that "maps and documents for which approval is sought" be on file "at least 10 days before the date of the hearing".
- **Siting:**
  - Local MLUL review, with a permitted use in industrial zones for 20+ acres (40:55D-66.11 [K]).
  - No state preemption for land-based solar or BESS.
  - Bills on small-storage permitted use are pending (S219, now S625).
- **Sources:** [OPMA (nj.gov PDF)](https://www.nj.gov/njoem/serc/pdf/Open_Public_Meetings_Act_N.J.S.A._10_4-6-21.pdf), [NJ DOS legal-notice directory](https://www.nj.gov/state/statewide-legal-notices-list.shtml), [NJ Monitor on S-4654](https://newjerseymonitor.com/2025/07/01/new-jersey-removes-mandate-to-publish-legal-notices-in-newspapers/), [40:55D-10](https://codes.findlaw.com/nj/title-40-municipalities-and-counties/nj-st-sect-40-55d-10/), [40:55D-12](https://codes.findlaw.com/nj/title-40-municipalities-and-counties/nj-st-sect-40-55d-12/).

### Pennsylvania (PA)
- **Open meetings:** 65 Pa.C.S. §709(c.1), added by Act 65 of 2021.
  - The agenda, "a listing of each matter of agency business that will be or may be the subject of deliberation or official action", goes on the website "no later than 24 hours in advance".
  - It is also posted at the meeting location and principal office.
  - §712.1 bars official action on unlisted items except emergencies, de minimis items that arise within 24 hours, or items added by majority vote with reasons announced. The amended agenda is posted by the next business day.
  - Planning commissions and zoning hearing boards are covered: "agency" includes any board or commission of a political subdivision (§703).
- **MPC "public notice" (§107):** a newspaper notice once a week for 2 weeks, the first no more than 30 days and the second at least 7 days before the hearing.
- **Siting:**
  - Entirely local under the MPC. The PUC has no role.
  - The RESET Board bill (HB 502, "Lightning Plan") has sat in House Energy since Apr 23, 2025 (bill page checked Oct 10, 2026).
- **Scale:** 2,625 general-purpose governments, the largest local land-use market in the Northeast by count.
- **Sources:** [65 Pa.C.S. ch. 7](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute?txtType=HTM&ttl=65&div=0&chapter=7), [MPC §107](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/unconsolidated/law-information/view-statute?txtType=HTM&SessYr=1968&ActNum=0247.&SessInd=0&chpt=1&sctn=7&subsctn=0), [HB 502](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/hb502).

## Key out-of-region siting laws

- **Illinois:**
  - PA 102-1123 (HB 4412, 2023; 55 ILCS 5/5-12020) caps county standards for commercial wind and solar [K for details].
  - PA 104-0458 (the Clean and Reliable Grid Affordability Act, signed Jan 8, 2026, effective June 1, 2026) adds 55 ILCS 5/5-12024, setting statewide county standards for standalone storage over 1 MW ([Taft](https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/illinois-clean-and-reliable-grid-affordability-act-enacts-new-statewide-zoning-standards-for-energy-storage-systems/)).
  - County hearings still happen, so agenda monitoring still has value.
- **Michigan:**
  - PA 233 (effective Nov 29, 2024) gives the MPSC an optional siting certificate for solar of 50 MW and up, wind of 100 MW and up and storage of 50 MW and up, when the local unit has no compatible ordinance (CREO).
  - The Court of Appeals largely upheld the MPSC order in May 2026, and the case is on appeal to the Michigan Supreme Court ([Sabin Center, Jul 14, 2026](https://blogs.law.columbia.edu/climatechange/2026/07/14/michigan-court-of-appeals-decision-largely-upholds-michigan-pscs-renewable-energy-siting-process/)).
  - Local adoption of a CREO, or a moratorium, is a township-agenda event.
- **Ohio:**
  - The OPSB takes projects of 50 MW and up.
  - SB 52 (2021) lets counties designate restricted areas and reject wind and solar projects; it does not apply to storage ([Bricker Graydon](https://www.bricker.com/insights/publications/Ohio-General-Assembly-passes-S-B-52-Changes-to-wind-and-solar-siting-requirements)).
  - County commission agendas are decisive.
- **Maryland:** the Renewable Energy Certainty Act (SB 931/HB 1036, 2025) limits local zoning bans on solar and storage ([fiscal note](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2025RS/fnotes/bil_0001/sb0931.pdf), [MACo](https://conduitstreet.mdcounties.org/2025/04/30/deep-dive-marylands-pivotal-2025-actions-on-solar-energy-policy/)). The thresholds need checking against the enrolled text.
- **Minnesota:** under §216I.02, PUC permits are required for solar of 50 MW AC and up, wind of 5 MW and up and storage of 10 MW and up ([revisor](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/216I.02)).
- **Data centers:** West Virginia HB 2014 (2025) bars most county and municipal zoning of certified microgrid and data-center districts ([WVCBP fact sheet](https://wvpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HB-2014-fact-sheet.pdf)). No other state preemption of data-center zoning was checked. In most states data centers remain a local zoning matter [K].
- **Reference for later checks:** the 50-state "Laws in Order" inventory (RAP/CATF with LBNL support, June 2024) at [catf.us/resource/laws-in-order](https://www.catf.us/resource/laws-in-order/). It was not opened this session.

## Implications for 351 Watch expansion

1. **Best next markets: PA, then NJ, then NY.** All three combine many local governments, mandatory pre-meeting agendas or notices, and local siting.
   - **PA:** 2,625 governments; a 24-hour online agenda with a bar on unlisted actions; fully local siting.
   - **NJ:** 585 governments; a 48-hour agenda "to the extent known"; land-use documents 10 days ahead; a statewide notice directory since Mar 2026.
   - **NY:** 1,582 governments; documents online 24 hours ahead; local control under 25 MW and over BESS.
2. **NH and ME** fit the Massachusetts town-meeting model and are locally controlled, but meeting notices are thin (NH: 24 hours, no agenda; ME: no fixed time). Warrants (14 days in NH, now online from Oct 18, 2026; 7 days in ME) and planning-board abutter notices give more lead time than meeting agendas.
3. **RI** is small (39 governments) but cheap to cover through one Secretary of State filing point.
4. **VT and CT** decide siting at the state level. Coverage there means state dockets (PUC and Siting Council) plus town positions, not town agendas alone.
5. **Outside the Northeast, the strongest fits** combine strong agenda laws with local siting: VA (verified packets and online agendas), TX cities (72 hours plus online), IN (48 hours excluding weekends, but no agenda required), and IL counties (48-hour agendas online). OH and MI are siting-relevant despite weak agenda laws.

## Caveats and items not verified

- cga.ct.gov (CT), ilga.gov (IL) and leginfo (CA) blocked or failed automated fetches. The CT and IL rows use agency or secondary copies.
- The NJ OPMA text is a Nov 2023 nj.gov copy. The 2025 website-notice law (S-4654) is confirmed only through news, a municipal library and the NJ Dept. of State page; the statute text was not read.
- **All [K] rows and cells need statute checks before use.** This includes most non-Northeast open-meeting details (for example AL, DE, HI, NV and NM lead times) and most non-Northeast siting thresholds. The UnGovr 50-state lead-time list (an AI-drafted secondary source) was used only as a cross-check.
- Census "township" counts include New England towns (MA 295 + 56 municipal = 351 cities and towns). Counties in CT and RI have no government. Counties in MA and VT have little or no zoning role.
- Web searches used: 17 (budget ~20). Most statute pages were fetched directly.
