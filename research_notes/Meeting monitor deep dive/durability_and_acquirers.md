# 351 Watch: durability against funded competitors, and likely acquirers

Research date: 2026-10-10. Method: 25 web searches, about 25 direct page fetches, and the public Ashby job-board API for Starbridge. Several vendor sites (myhamlet.com, citizenportal.ai, theaustinbulldog.org, kingscrowd.com) returned 403 or 429 errors to automated fetches, so some facts about them come from search-engine summaries and are labelled [R].

**Labels**
- **[V] Verified.** Read this session on a primary source (company page, press release, job post, filing) or in named trade press.
- **[R] Reported.** Comes from an aggregator (P2PMarketData, PrivSource, Seedtable, Sacra), a search-result summary of a page that could not be opened, or an earlier session's notes that were not re-checked.
- **[I] Inference.** My judgment, or arithmetic on cited figures. Where an [I] rests on background knowledge that was not re-checked this session, it says so.

No number below is estimated unless it is labelled [I] and the arithmetic is shown.

## Bottom line

1. **AI summaries of meetings will not hold off competitors.** At least six funded or established players already turn recordings and minutes into text: Hamlet, Citizen Portal, GovSpend, Starbridge, InstaTrac (owned by the Boston Globe since July 2026) and Shovels/ReZone. [V] Any one of them can add Massachusetts towns. [I]
2. **The gap that is real today is timing.** Almost every competitor works from material published after the meeting:
   - Hamlet works from video. [V]
   - Shovels does not create a decision record "until the minutes are posted", and minutes often appear weeks later. [V]
   - InstaTrac's MuniTrac processes video and the agenda after the meeting. [V]
   - Citizen Portal writes articles after the meeting. [V, from earlier notes]

   GovSpend says only "around 40–50 percent of agencies provide video and audio meeting recordings", while "a much larger proportion" post agendas, minutes and agenda packets. [V] A product built on the agendas, packets and legal notices that Massachusetts towns post before a meeting can therefore cover boards that video-first tools cannot see, and flag them earlier. [I]
3. **The asset acquirers say they pay for is history and early signal, not features.** Recent buyers described what they bought as:
   - "months of early project visibility" (Shovels buying ReZone)
   - something built "over the last three decades" (the Boston Globe buying InstaTrac)
   - "1 billion+" legal documents (Clio buying vLex)
   - "thousands of renewables projects" catalogued (Enverus buying Energy Acuity)

   [V] The asset 351 Watch can build that is hard to copy is a structured, sourced and corrected **Massachusetts land-use decision history**: every hearing, continuance, vote, condition and appeal, linked to parcels and applicants. [I]
4. **The biggest new fact this session: Boston Globe Media bought InstaTrac in July 2026.** InstaTrac is a Boston company founded in 1994. It sells Massachusetts legislative tracking (MassTrac), AI meeting transcripts for towns (MuniTrac) and a daily politics newsletter. It has six employees and says it serves "380+" organizations. The Globe says the deal helps it expand coverage of local communities across Massachusetts. [V] That makes the Globe both the most dangerous new Massachusetts competitor and the most natural acquirer for 351 Watch. [I]
5. **Likely buyers, in order of fit** [I]:
   1. Boston Globe Media / InstaTrac
   2. Shovels
   3. Hamlet
   4. The Warren Group (Massachusetts real-estate records)
   5. Energy-siting data firms: SitePath, Heatmap, LandGate, Enverus, Paces
   6. Sales-intelligence firms (Starbridge, GovSpend), a weaker fit

   Every tuck-in deal found for 2025–2026 had undisclosed terms. [V] For a small company the realistic outcome is an undisclosed data-plus-team purchase. Only businesses with meaningful revenue have disclosed prices (Onvia, Glenigan, Periscope). [I]

## 3. Moat evidence from analogous businesses

### 3a. Regional players that survived national ones

- **The Warren Group (Massachusetts).** It traces its roots to *Banker & Tradesman* in 1872 and stayed Northeast-focused for most of its history. Its move into data began in 1986 (Transfer Directory) and continued with online records search (1998). In 2015 it bought NH/VT records. In 2018 it sold its events and custom-publishing units "to focus resources on Data Solutions", and in 2019 it expanded nationally. [V, company history] A Massachusetts-specific records business lasted about 150 years by owning registry-level local records, a weekly records habit among local professionals, and a local brand. It then used that base to expand. [I]
- **InstaTrac (Massachusetts).** About 32 years from founding (1994) to acquisition (2026) with **six employees**. It is Massachusetts-only and serves 380+ organizations. Its exit was to the dominant in-state media company, not a national data firm. [V; I for the takeaway] This is the closest analogue to 351 Watch: a small, single-state government-activity tracker with high retention whose buyer was a local strategic. [I]
- **BillTrack50.** An independent 50-state legislative tracker that worked with Nimonik for "over a decade" before Nimonik bought it. [V] Partnering first and selling later is a common path. [I]
- **Glenigan.** A UK-only project-leads business that sold for about 6x revenue. [V/I] The buyer called "construction project information" its "core business". [V] Regional completeness was the asset.

### 3b. Collection labor is the real moat, and nationals pay it only where the return justifies it

- **Heatmap** calls "all 3,100+" counties. [V]
- **Shovels** built Project Storm for "approximately 10,000" offline jurisdictions, using phone calls, formal requests and "many humans". [V]
- **GovSpend** says most agencies post agendas and packets but only 40–50% post recordings. [V]
- **351 Watch's crawler** could not read 6 of 30 sample towns: Cloudflare blocks, Granicus and HeyGov robots.txt rules, and one town that posts no agendas. [V, VALIDATION.md]

The long tail of small New England towns, scanned PDFs and vendor-hosted portals is expensive to cover. National firms cover it last. [I]

### 3c. Historical archives are an asset

Archive depth appears in nearly every rationale:
- vLex's corpus [V]
- InstaTrac's "three decades" [V]
- Warren's NH records since 1977 [V]
- Energy Acuity's project catalogue [V]

AI cuts the cost of producing new summaries but does nothing to produce the past. Backfilled, structured history therefore gains value as live summarization becomes a commodity. [I]

### 3d. Outcome and decision databases are where the category is heading

- Shovels plans status fields (proposed, discussed, approved, disapproved, postponed). [V]
- SitePath records the board vote and resolution number for each moratorium. [V]
- Heatmap tracks contested and withdrawn projects. [V]

Competitors agree that **outcomes**, not transcripts, are the product. [I] Whoever has the most complete and correct Massachusetts outcome history when buyers look for it has the asset. [I]

### 3e. Community corrections and human QA (evidence is thin)

- InstaTrac advertises "All transcripts are human edited." [V]
- SitePath advertises "Every figure cited to a primary document." [V]
- Shovels says scaling requires "dedicated resources for data validation and quality control." [V]

No source quantified the value of community corrections in this category. The argument rests on general data-business practice. [I] A correction log from clerks, land agents and attorneys is cheap to build. It doubles as a record of who uses the product, and a fast-moving national entrant cannot copy it quickly. [I]

### 3f. What did not protect

- **Breadth-only policy tracking churned.** FiscalNote's net revenue retention fell below 100%, and it sold several units in 2024–25. [V, earlier notes]
- **Summary and clip products are now offered free or cheap by funded players.** Hamlet has a free tier and Hamlet TV. Citizen Portal's articles are free. [V]

**Verdict** [I]:
- As an AI summaries and alerts product, 351 Watch is **not durable**. Several funded players can match it within their normal expansion pace.
- As a **pre-meeting, all-351-towns, Massachusetts land-use decision database** with parcel and entity links, backfilled history and a correction loop, it is defensible for a while. It is also attractive to buyers, because each acquirer above would otherwise have to rebuild Massachusetts town by town.
- The window is set by how fast the Globe/InstaTrac and Hamlet move. Neither has announced an all-351 land-use product. [V, negative]

## 5. Recommendations: what to build first

These are ordered by how much each makes 351 Watch hard to copy and attractive to buyers.

**1. Make pre-meeting coverage of every land-use body in all 351 towns the core metric, and publish it.**
- **What:** Ingest agendas, packets, Town Meeting warrants and legal notices for Planning Board, ZBA, Conservation Commission, Select Board/Council and Board of Health, with OCR. Publish a public coverage dashboard showing towns × bodies × freshness. Shovels publishes a coverage dashboard. [V]
- **Evidence:**
  - Recordings exist at only 40–50% of agencies. [V, GovSpend]
  - Shovels waits for minutes. [V]
  - Hamlet is video-first and covers 81 of 351. [V]
  - The 351 Watch crawler already caught items before meetings. [V]
- **Why it is hard to copy:** the work is town by town, the scans are messy, and vendor portals need permission. [I]

**2. Build a Massachusetts decision lifecycle database, then backfill it.**
- **What:** One record per application, tracked from filing to hearing, through continuances, to the vote, conditions, decision filing and appeal. Fields include body, relief type, applicant or LLC, agent or attorney, parcel and vote. Include the steps national schemas leave out: Town Meeting article votes, Attorney General review of bylaws (coverage_check.md cites SitePath writing about the AG disapproving Carver's solar/BESS moratoria), Chapter 40B comprehensive permits, and Conservation Commission orders of conditions. [I: these legal steps are background knowledge, not re-verified this session.] Backfill several years from posted minutes and decisions.
- **Evidence:** Acquirers name history as the asset (Clio, InstaTrac, Warren, Energy Acuity). [V] Competitors are converging on outcome fields: Shovels statuses are "coming soon" and SitePath records votes. [V]

**3. Keep the clean-energy and data-center moratorium tracker as the paid wedge.**
- **Evidence for demand:** It is where buyer money concentrates:
  - Heatmap and SitePath sell to energy developers. [V]
  - Hamlet has added data-center sentiment indices. [V]
  - Shovels published on data-center moratoriums in Sep 2026. [V]
  - LandGate bought Topos for BESS diligence. [R]
- **Evidence for the gap:** County-grained products miss Massachusetts events. SitePath listed Lee as having "no active moratorium" despite a 4/27/2026 BESS moratorium hearing. [V, coverage_check.md] Zoning in Massachusetts is decided by municipalities, not counties. [I, background]

**4. Link everything to parcels and entities, and ship acquirer-ready outputs.**
- **What:** Join records to MassGIS parcel IDs and resolve applicant LLCs, attorneys and engineers. Ship an API, a bulk license and a warehouse-friendly export.
- **Evidence:**
  - Shovels links each decision to property, applicant, owner and contractor, and sells an enterprise bulk license. [V]
  - Enterprise customers load Shovels tables into Snowflake, BigQuery or Databricks. [R, Sacra]
  - Warren Group sells Massachusetts deed and mortgage data that a parcel key would join to. [V]
- **Why buyers care:** clean join keys make 351 Watch easy to bolt onto a buyer's product. [I]

**5. Make provenance and a correction loop visible.**
- **What:** Cite a page-level source for every field (SitePath's "every figure cited to a primary document"). [V] Add human review on flagged items (InstaTrac's "human edited"). [V] Give clerks, land agents and residents a one-click correction form that writes a versioned log.
- **Evidence:** Weak; mostly general data-business practice. [I] It is cheap, it builds a record of who uses the product, and a national entrant cannot copy it quickly. [I]

**6. Secure source access where crawling is blocked.**
- **What:** Ask towns and vendors (Granicus, HeyGov) for permission or feeds, and offer clerks something free in return.
- **Evidence:** Shovels' Project Storm and Heatmap's calls show the long tail is solved through relationships. [V] Six of 30 sample towns need this today. [V, VALIDATION.md]
- **Why it matters:** InstaTrac shows that clerk relationships are the Globe's foothold. [V/I]

**7. Do not compete on generic AI summaries, articles or clips.**
- **Evidence:** Citizen Portal, Hamlet TV, MuniTrac and GovSpend already offer them, mostly free or bundled. [V] Keep summaries as a feature of decision records, not the product. [I]

## 6. Likely acquirers, ranked by fit [I]

1. **Boston Globe Media / InstaTrac.**
   - **Fit:** It has stated Massachusetts local-coverage intent and an existing MA municipal and legislative product line with lobbyist and newsroom customers. [V]
   - **What it would buy:** 351 Watch's pre-meeting, land-use and developer data fills InstaTrac's gaps. [I]
   - **Risk:** it builds instead of buying. [I]
2. **Shovels.**
   - **Fit:** It has already bought a meeting-decisions company and is chasing jurisdiction parity. [V]
   - **What it would buy:** Massachusetts is a self-contained block of 351 small jurisdictions that would arrive with history and parcel links. [I]
3. **Hamlet.**
   - **Fit:** It is funded, already sells to developers, and has reached 81 of 351. [V]
   - **What it would buy:** non-video boards and the clean-energy wedge. [I]
4. **The Warren Group.** A Massachusetts and New England records business that bought a regional archive in 2015 and refocused on data in 2018. Land-use decisions sit just before its deed and mortgage data. [V/I]
5. **SitePath, Heatmap, LandGate, Enverus, Paces.**
   - **Fit:** Their buyers are exactly the BESS, solar and data-center developers 351 Watch serves. LandGate (Topos) and Enverus (Energy Acuity, RatedPower) have both bought in this space before. [V/R]
   - **Likely form:** a Massachusetts town-level restrictions layer or a licensing deal before any acquisition. [I]
6. **Starbridge / GovSpend.** They have capital and scraping in place but serve the wrong buyer. Licensing is more likely than acquisition. [I]
7. **Agenda platforms (Granicus, CivicPlus).** They control the sources. Treat them as partners or gatekeepers rather than buyers. [I]
