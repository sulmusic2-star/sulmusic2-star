# Expansion markets for 351 Watch: which states to enter after Massachusetts

Prepared 2026-10-10. The question: where does a product that sends pre-meeting alerts on solar, battery storage (BESS), data center, transmission and 40B-style housing siting, sold to developers, land agents and land-use attorneys, have the most room to grow?

The scoring model, the sources and the numbers behind every score are below. Section 5 marks what is verified and what is estimated.

---

**How robust this is:**
- Nine states (PA, TX, CA, IL, OH, NY, MI, VA, IN) stay in the top 10 under all five weight sets tested (Section 2c).
- The Northeast order is the same under three of the five sets. Each of the other two swaps one adjacent pair: CT/NH (opposition-heavy) or RI/VT (no judgment factors).

**What the ranking does not measure:** it ranks market opportunity, not ease of entry. Massachusetts scores below Pennsylvania, New York and Maine mainly because its large projects now go to the state siting board (25 MW and up) and its queue is small.

**What it means for sequencing (judgment, not scored):**
- **New York** is the natural first expansion. It has the same town-board form of government, ISO-territory buyers next door, a BESS moratorium wave being decided town by town, and towns decide projects under 25 MW plus standalone storage.
- **Pennsylvania** is the bigger prize second, provided HB 502 (state siting preemption) stays stalled.
- **Connecticut, Rhode Island and New Hampshire** are worth adding only for 40B-style housing alerts.

## 1. Method

Each metric was put through log10(1 + x), then rescaled from 0 to 10 across the 48 contiguous states (Alaska and Hawaii excluded). The six factor scores were then weighted.

| Factor (weight) | Metric(s) inside the factor | Why this weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Pipeline (30%)** | 50%: active solar, storage and onshore wind capacity in interconnection queues that is decided locally. Thresholds come from each state's siting law; capacity above the threshold is counted at a reduced share where local review still applies.<br>25%: EIA-860M count of planned solar and BESS units of 20 MW or less (a proxy for distribution-scale and community projects, which the LBNL queues do not include).<br>25%: planned data centers (Pew / Data Center Map). | Volume of projects that will hit a local agenda is the revenue driver. |
| **Opposition intensity (25%)** | 35%: Sabin local restrictions.<br>15%: Sabin contested projects.<br>35%: data-center local measures (Savrn).<br>15%: a BESS-moratorium indicator from 0 to 10. | Opposition is what makes "know before the meeting" worth paying for. |
| **Fragmentation (15%)** | Estimated count of local governments with land-use power (Census 2022, adjusted, see 4.3). | More bodies means more manual monitoring pain and a bigger moat once covered. |
| **Preemption retention (15%)** | Score from 0 to 10 for how much siting stays local, from the Sabin 2026 state siting frameworks (see 4.5). | State siting boards remove local decision volume. |
| **Competitor gap (10%)** | Judgment from 0 to 10 (see 4.4). | Kept low because evidence on competitor coverage by state is thin. |
| **40B-style housing adjacency (5%)** | 10 = a state housing-appeals or builder's-remedy statute like Chapter 40B; 0 = none. | Secondary product line. |

The composite is 10 × the weighted sum, which gives a range of 0 to 100.

1. **Pennsylvania (71.8).** Local siting at any size, the most fragmented zoning map in the East (2,625 governments), 60 local restrictions, 61 data-center measures and 51 planned data centers. It ranks 1st under three of the five weight sets. The 10.1 GW of transmission-queue capacity, all of it locally decided, understates the pipeline because it leaves out community-scale projects (43 small planned units in EIA-860M). Watch HB 502.
2. **Texas (69.9).** By far the largest pipeline in the country.
   - Queues: 166 GW of active solar and 170 GW of storage across 221 counties (LBNL).
   - Data centers: 170 planned (Pew), and about 85 GW of data-center pipeline (secondary source).
   - The catch: counties have no general zoning power, so local leverage runs through commissioners-court tax abatements, resolutions and city councils. The 600 "decision units" used here is an estimate.
   - Competition: the county-level national tools (Heatmap Pro calls every county; Learnewable claims 3,000+ counties) already fit Texas's grain.
3. **California (68.2, tie).** 64 GW of solar and 100 GW of storage in queues. Siting is local unless the developer opts into the state Energy Commission process (solar of 50 MW or more, storage of 200 MWh or more). California has 35 contested projects, the 3rd most, and BESS moratoria after Moss Landing (EticaAG lists 13). It has the lowest competitor gap of any state: Hamlet is based in Orinda and started in California.
4. **Illinois (68.2, tie).**
   - Pipeline: 71 GW of locally reviewed queue capacity, the most small planned units in the US (117 in EIA-860M, mostly community solar), and 123 planned data centers (4th).
   - Rules: state law caps local standards and bars bans, but requires a public hearing within 60 days and a decision 30 days after it. That produces predictable, alertable meeting events.
   - Housing: the Affordable Housing Planning and Appeal Act gives a builder's remedy.
   - Fragmentation: Illinois townships do not zone, so only about 1,400 units count.
5. **Ohio (67.4).** 118 data-center measures (2nd), 32 local renewable restrictions and 31 contested projects. Data Center Watch places Ohio among the top states for opposition groups through March 2026. Under SB 52, counties can ban solar of 50 MW or more in restricted areas, often prompted by township resolutions, so the 1,308 townships and 88 county commissions are the decision points.
6. **New York (63.8).** 86 local restrictions, 52 contested projects (1st) and the national BESS moratorium hotspot. Towns decide solar under 25 MW and standalone storage, and EIA-860M lists 93 small planned units (2nd in the US). Opposition is the highest in the Northeast. Competition is moderate (Citizen Portal), and the town form of government matches the 351 Watch playbook.
7. **Michigan (63.7).** The most local renewable restrictions in the US (98), 104 data-center measures (3rd) and 1,240 townships. PA 233 sends solar of 50 MW or more to the state commission unless the town has a compatible ordinance (CREO). In May 2026 the Court of Appeals partly upheld the commission's CREO order and sent key sections back, so the local stage is still the first stop and the rules are still moving.
8. **Virginia (62.5).** 287 planned data centers (1st by far), 40 contested renewable projects (2nd) and 39 local restrictions. Sabin lists state roles (a DEQ permit at 5 MW or more, an SCC certificate above 150 MW), but county land-use approval still drives outcomes. That reading is my interpretation (see 4.5). Only 323 local governments, so Virginia is easy to cover but also easy for county-level competitors.
9. **Indiana (62.1).** Local siting at any size (the state only bars "unreasonable" solar restrictions), 30 GW of solar queued across 63 counties, 54 planned data centers and 36 local restrictions.
   - Heatmap (Aug 2026): Kansas, Iowa and Indiana account for most counties that restrict solar, batteries and data centers at once.
   - Data Center Watch: Indiana was among the states where key projects were blocked in 2025.
   - Townships do not zone, so there are about 660 units.
10. **Minnesota (57.6).** Very fragmented: 2,720 governments, 1,779 of them townships. 38 local restrictions and 40 data-center measures. The state utility commission takes solar and storage of 50 MW or more, so only 0.2 GW of the 26 GW queue is decided locally. This slot is unstable: **Georgia** (56.7) takes it under the pipeline-heavy, opposition-heavy and no-judgment weight sets, on 141 planned data centers and 71 data-center measures. Georgia's competitor gap is lower because OrdinanceWatch covers 13 counties and 40 municipalities there.

## 4. Factor notes and evidence

### 4.1 Development pipeline

**LBNL "Queued Up: 2026 Edition"** (data file posted May 2026, slide deck June 2026, data as of the end of 2025):
- National totals: about 8,200 active projects, 1,312 GW of generation plus about 749 GW of storage.
- Solar is down 19% to 773 GW. Only 13% of capacity requested from 2000 to 2020 had reached commercial operation by the end of 2025.
- My state tabulation of the row-level sheet ("03. Complete Queue Data", status = active) matches the solar total (772.6 GW).
- My storage total (553 GW) is lower than LBNL's 749 GW because LBNL's codebook says imputed storage for hybrids is excluded from the public rows. Hybrid rows with missing storage MW are concentrated in ERCOT (539), the West (452), MISO (207) and PJM (146).

**Biggest limitation for the Northeast.** The LBNL queues are transmission-level only. Distribution-interconnected community solar and BESS, which is the bulk of Massachusetts, New York, New Jersey and Maine local agenda items, are missing. EIA-860M partly fills the gap: it lists planned units of 1 MW and up that are close to operation.

**Data centers:**

### 4.2 Local opposition intensity

**Sabin Center, "Opposition to Renewable Energy Facilities in the US," 6th edition** (Sep 2026; data as of Dec 31, 2025):
- Headline: 888 restrictions in 48 states (865 local, 23 state).
- I parsed oppositionreport.org/reports/current and got 866 local, 23 state and 569 contested projects.
- Top states for local restrictions: MI 98, NY 86, PA 60, IA 52, NE 46, VA 39, MN 38, IN 36, ME 32, OH 32, TX 30.
- Top states for contested projects: NY 52, VA 40, CA 35, MI 33, OH 31, IN 25.
- New restrictions per year are falling: 165 in 2023, 112 in 2024, 70 in 2025 (from the earlier comparables note). Data-center and BESS measures appear to be taking their place.
- Sabin's restriction categories are wind- and solar-centric. BESS appears only incidentally (NY 15, MA 4 entries mention storage).

**Data-center local measures** (secondary trackers; definitions differ):
- **Savrn** (updated Oct 2026; counts statewide and local measures, including proposed and failed ones; about 1,235 local nationally, described as a floor): NJ 119, OH 118, MI 104, GA 71, NC 68, CA 65, PA 61, WI 58, KY 47, VA 44, IN 43, MD 43.
- **Electric Choice** (as of Oct 6, 2026; 482 active restrictions in 44 states): NJ 93, MI 63, NC 56, OH 55, GA 42.
  - Its own pages are inconsistent: North Carolina is 56 in the table but 52 on its summary card, and Georgia is 42 in the table but 52 on its card.
- **Heatmap** (Aug 24, 2026): more than 530 data-center restrictions nationally. 21 counties restrict solar, batteries and data centers at once, mostly in Kansas, Iowa and Indiana. Heatmap Pro also found nearly 20 of Iowa's 99 counties with restrictive ordinances.

**BESS moratoria:**
- **Carina Energy** (press release, Mar 10, 2026; commercial source, methodology not reviewed): at least 150 local governments in 17 states. New York has 98 moratoria, 65% of the total, and Indiana, Michigan, Iowa and Maine are "rising."
- **EticaAG database** (modified Feb 11, 2026; incomplete): NY 38, CA 13, AZ 8, TX 5, CO 3, NV 3, MA 1, MI 1, OR 1. Its Massachusetts figure is clearly low next to the 351 Watch tracker, which lists several AG-reviewed BESS moratoria (Becket, Blandford, Wendell and others).
- **2026 New York town actions seen:** Marcellus, Cambria, Walton, Beekman, Clifton Park (extension), Concord (extension hearing), Corinth, Cortlandt (extension), Moreau.
- **2026 Michigan actions seen:** Oshtemo (Nov 2025), Meridian (May 2026), Gun Plain (through Dec 2026).
- **Indicator used in the score (judgment):** NY 10; CA 6; AZ, MI, IN, IA, ME, MA 5; TX, CO, NV 3; WI 2; OR 1; all others 0. A 0 means no data, not proof of no activity.

### 4.4 Competitor coverage

| Tool | Grain and coverage evidence | Where it bites |
|---|---|---|
| Hamlet (Orinda, CA; about $10M VC) | Since Feb 2026 it says it covers "3,000+ city councils, planning commissions and additional governing bodies" nationally, with keyword alerts and data-center sentiment indices.
