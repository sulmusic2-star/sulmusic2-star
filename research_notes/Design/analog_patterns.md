# Analog patterns: what Agenda Ahead should borrow

Draft in progress (2026-10-12). Sections being filled in as sites are visited.

Screenshots: `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-sulmusic2-star/815cfdd0-e090-5147-aee8-a5db345f6b5d/scratchpad/analogs/` (desktop 1440 px and 390 px phone, Playwright Chromium).

## Early findings

- PlanningAlerts (https://www.planningalerts.org.au/) is the closest analog. Home is one question ("What is being built and knocked down in your area?"), one address box with "or use my current location", and a 3-step "Search / Get notified / Comment" strip. The results page puts "Save this search as an email alert [Save]" directly above the list. Each item: address as the link, then the description, then "3 days ago, 890 m away".
- PlanningAlerts also shows the failure we have: it prints the council's raw description in full ("Prune one (1) Lilly Pilly ... Annexure B3-2 Tree Canopy Replacement Planting List ..."), 10 to 15 lines per item. The results page is 15,408 px tall at 390 px.
- Utah's Public Notice Website (https://www.utah.gov/pmn/) opens with "Meetings and Events Around Me" filtered to a ZIP code: Notice title, event date, address, public body. Most titles are just "Agenda", which tells a visitor nothing.
- Chicago Councilmatic (https://chicago.councilmatic.org/) uses the tagline "Chicago City Council, demystified" and puts "Next City Council meeting: Wed 10/14/2026 10:00a.m." plus a short "Tue 10/13 - Committee on Zoning..." list on the home page.
- GovTrack bill page (https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/119/hr1): status in one bold line ("Enacted — Signed by the President on July 4, 2025") followed by a plain sentence, then "Source: Congress.gov". The History list labels each step with what happens next: "Passed House (Senate next). The bill was passed in a vote in the House. It goes to the Senate next."
- FlightAware (https://www.flightaware.com/live/flight/UAL1, 390 px): the first screen is a status headline in green ("EN ROUTE AND ON TIME / Arriving in 2 hours 14 minutes"), then a one-line origin-to-destination bar with "14h 30m elapsed / 2h 14m remaining". The cookie banner and an "Unlimited Flight Alerts" upsell cover the rest of that first screen.
- CPSC recalls (https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls): every recall uses the same headline formula ("[Company] Recalls [Product] Due to [Hazard]") and the same labeled fields: Hazard, Remedy, Units, Consumer Contact. A visitor never has to read the legal notice.
- Plural/Open States (https://openstates.org/ma/bills/ redirects to pluralpolicy.com): status tag (INTRODUCED), "LATEST ACTION: OCTOBER 13, 2026", and a TRACK button on every row. The newest 19,518 results start with routine "communication from the Comptroller" filings, which shows what happens without a routine-item filter.
- Chicago Councilmatic events (https://chicago.councilmatic.org/events/): list of "Tue 10/13/2026 10:00 a.m. Committee on Zoning, Landmarks and Building Standards", an RSS link, and a "What are these meetings?" box beside the list. On the home page, legislation is tagged "Non-Routine" so the few real items stand out from disabled-parking permits.
- FixMyStreet (https://www.fixmystreet.com/): "Enter a nearby UK postcode, or street name and area: e.g. 'B2 4QA' or 'Tib St, Manchester'" plus "Use my current location"; a 4-step "How to report a problem" list; and three live counters ("22,713 reports in past week"). The /around results page returned 503 (maintenance) on 2026-10-12.
- USPS tracking (https://tools.usps.com/go/TrackConfirmAction?tLabels=...) rendered a blank page in headless Chromium at both widths; treated as blocked and skipped.
- NYC ZAP (https://zap.planning.nyc.gov/projects): opens with a modal about a "New CEQR Filter Option" over the results. Left rail has 12 filters including "RACIAL EQUITY REPORT APPLICABILITY", "ULURP TYPE", "CEQR". Item rows are good: plain title ("31-10 & 31-16 Hunters Point Ave Rezoning"), applicant + one-sentence summary cut at about 150 characters with "...", tags "Queens | CD 2", and a date. Map legend uses four stages: Noticed, Filed, In Public Review, Completed.
- Boston Planning (https://www.bostonplans.org/projects/development-projects): each row is name + status chip (UNDER REVIEW, LETTER OF INTENT, CONSTRUCTION COMPLETE) + use type (RENTAL / RESIDENTIAL / RETAIL) + "OCT 09 2026 Latest Filed Date". Projects since 2016 have a milestone timeline.
- Toronto AIC (https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/planning-development/application-information-centre/): "Search by address", "data is refreshed daily", and "Subscribe to receive updates of new development applications in a specific Toronto ward."
- UK PlanIt (https://www.planit.org.uk/): "Find planning applications based on a ... Rectangle Circle Postcode Boundary Area Identifier" plus an A-to-Z of 417 authorities; honest coverage note ("more than 30 of these are not up to date").
- Seattle LUIB: both https://web.seattle.gov/sdci/luib/ and https://www.seattle.gov/sdci/permits/land-use-information-bulletin returned 404 on 2026-10-12.
- PlanningAlerts application page (https://www.planningalerts.org.au/applications/3571765): provenance in a sentence, "We found this application on the planning authority's website on 10 October 2026, 3 days ago. It was received by them 12 days earlier.", plus "View source", the council reference number, and "949 people were notified of this application via Planning Alerts email alerts".
- Boston project page (https://www.bostonplans.org/projects/development-projects/74-ashton-street): one-line plain description ("Four story apartment building with 24 units and 16 parking spaces"), a fact strip (Neighborhood, Address, Land Sq. Feet, Gross Floor Area, Contact), and a vertical "Project Phase" stepper (Letter of Intent > Under Review > Board Approved > Under Construction > Construction Complete) with the current step marked. "Comment period ends Nov 09, 2026" sits above the comment form.
- NYC ZAP project (https://zap.planning.nyc.gov/projects/2023Q0364): "Project Brief" is one long sentence of zoning codes ("from M2-1 to M1-5 ... 83,500 zsf"); the Milestones list has 14 steps (CEQR Fee Paid ... Approval Letter Sent to Responsible Agency) with Completed / In Progress / Not Started states. Too many steps for a resident.
- Councilmatic legislation page (https://chicago.councilmatic.org/legislation/o2026-0029019/): status chip "Active", tags "Non-Routine", a History table (Date, Legislative body, Action), "RSS updates for this legislation", and "View on the Chicago City Clerk website".
- weather.gov point forecast (https://forecast.weather.gov/MapClick.php?lat=42.3732&lon=-72.5199): glance strip first (Tonight / Tuesday / Tuesday Night, icon, "Low: 52 °F", 3-word phrase), then "Detailed Forecast" in plain sentences, then "Text Only Forecast" and "Printable Forecast" links, "Last update 12 Oct 3:55 pm EDT".
- AirNow (https://www.airnow.gov/?city=Amherst&state=MA&country=USA): when current data is missing it says "Not Available" and still shows "Forecast AQI: Today Good / Tomorrow Good" as colored dots, an action sentence ("Enjoy your outdoor activities."), and credit ("Forecast courtesy of Massachusetts Dept. of Environmental Protection").
- USGS Earthquake Notification Service (https://earthquake.usgs.gov/ens/): states the three typical setups in plain words ("Large earthquakes anywhere in the world / Moderate earthquakes in the U.S. / Earthquakes of any size near you") and links an "Example Event Message" for every message type. Warns that one earthquake "can generate up to five notifications".
- Google Alerts (https://www.google.com/alerts): the whole page is one box, "Create an alert about...".
- Zillow search (https://www.zillow.com/amherst-ma/, 390 px): top row is search box, then "For sale | Filters | Save search" with Save search as the only filled button. Cards: price, then "4 bds | 4 ba | 2,787 sqft | House for sale", address, agent in small type. Change badges sit on the card: "Price cut: $49,900 (9/29)". Bottom tab bar has an "Updates" tab.
