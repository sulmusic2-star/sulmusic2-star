# How websites should be: the rules, the measurements, and what they mean for Agenda Ahead

Written Oct 28, 2026. This note adds to two earlier studies and repeats neither: `research_notes/Design/civic_rules.md` (GOV.UK, USWDS, Boston.gov, Documenters, the newsletters) and `research_notes/Design/ai_design_rules.md` (designing with AI). Where a rule already lives in one of those, it is not restated here. The site under study is https://agendaahead.vercel.app as it stood today, after the redesign to `research_notes/Design/human_first_spec.md`.

## Method

Every quotation below was read on the page linked next to it, today. Each source is cited with a name, a link and a year. Live-site figures come from the page HTML and `styles.css` saved to `scratchpad/design3/live/`, from `curl` with and without `Accept-Encoding`, and from Playwright with the preinstalled Chromium at 390×844, 1366×900 and 320×700 (`scratchpad/design3/measure.js`, `clicks.js`; results in `measurements.json`, `measure.log`, `clicks.log`; screenshots in `shots/`). Section 3 gives the method per number.

Pages that could not be read, and were not bypassed: design.theguardian.com, theguardian.design (returned only "zeroheight") and www.theguardian.com (the fetch tool refuses the domain); open.nytimes.com and www.nytimes.com (refused); bbc.co.uk and radiotimes.com (refused); eventbrite.com (HTTP 405 twice); old.reddit.com (refused); atlassian.design (pages render only with JavaScript, empty to a fetch); m3.material.io and m2.material.io (404 and a bare title); polaris.shopify.com (redirects to shopify.dev, which has no content guidelines; the guidelines were read from the Polaris GitHub repository instead); sensible.com (no excerpt of Don't Make Me Think; the Peachpit excerpts were read instead); edwardtufte.com book pages (topic lists only; the notebook was read instead); berkshirehathaway.com (HTTP 502 twice; Buffett's words come from a news report and are marked second-hand); starlingbank.com (no tone-of-voice page found; Starling is not cited); bettermotherfuckingwebsite.com (503); Erin Kissane's A List Apart piece (404). ## Section 1: the rules

Eighty rules. Format: **rule.** Who said it (link, year). **Here:** yes / no / partly, with the page and element.

### First screen and purpose

1. **Put the most important points in the first two paragraphs; "First lines of text on a page receive more gazes than subsequent lines."** Kara Pernice, NN/g, [F-Shaped Pattern](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/), 2017, reviewed Aug 2026. **Here:** yes on home (h1, one sentence, the town box at 365 px); partly on /ma/carver, where the lead answers the question but the first dated row is at 872 px and the first future item at 1,722 px (390 px wide).
2. **"users spent about 57% of their page-viewing time above the fold"; "Reserve the top of the page for high-priority content"; "Keep major CTAs above the fold"; "Beware of false floors".** Therese Fessenden, NN/g, [Scrolling and Attention](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/), 2018. **Here:** yes for the one button on every page (home 365 px, Carver about 590 px); partly for the first row (/upcoming 575 px, home 651 px, Somerset 736 px, Carver 872 px at 390).
3. **"start at the epicenter and design the most important piece of content first"; ignore "the navigation/tabs, footer, colors, sidebar, logo" at the start.** 37signals, [Getting Real: Epicenter Design](https://basecamp.com/gettingreal/09.2-epicenter-design), 2006. **Here:** yes: the meeting page's h1 is the matter ("Temporary ban on new data centers (a moratorium)"); the list row is the subject.
4. **The blank slate must "Answer key questions that first-time viewers will ask: What is this page? What do I do now?"** 37signals, [The Blank Slate](https://basecamp.com/gettingreal/09.4-the-blank-slate), 2006. **Here:** yes: Carver's lead says nothing is on, what moved, and when the next agenda is; /watch step 2 says "expect about that many alerts a month".
5. **"We put useful stuff on the page so people have to scan it"; search is a fallback, not the plan.** Jared Spool, [The Secret Lives of Links](https://archive.uie.com/brainsparks/2012/12/12/jared-spool-the-secret-lives-of-links/), UIE, 2012. **Here:** yes: home offers three example towns and a town box; Search is third in the header.
6. **"When critical parts of an application are made more visible, usage of them can increase"; hidden menus stop people "moving between the major sections"; and a footer nav so "the lack of options at the end of a content page" doesn't "leave people hanging on mobile devices".** Luke Wroblewski, [Obvious Always Wins](https://www.lukew.com/ff/entry.asp?1945), 2015, and [Off Canvas Multi-Device Layouts](https://www.lukew.com/ff/entry.asp?1569), 2012. **Here:** yes: five header links visible at 390 px, each 44 px tall, no hamburger; Explore and About lists in the footer.
7. **"Order content by relative importance ... place the most important items near the top and leading side"; "Align elements to make them easier to scan".** Apple, [HIG: Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout), undated. **Here:** partly: one left edge and a date column, but on the meeting page "Add this meeting to my calendar" sits at 1,323 px under the Follow box.

### Reading and scanning

8. **"People rarely read Web pages word by word; instead, they scan"; 79% scanned; scannable text means highlighted keywords, meaningful subheads, one idea per paragraph and "half the word count (or less)"; the concise version measured "58% better"; "promotional language imposes a cognitive burden on users" (objective text 27% better).** Jakob Nielsen, NN/g, [How Users Read on the Web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/), 1997. **Here:** partly: rows have a subject heading and one sentence and no promotional words, but Carver's lead is one 59-word, three-idea paragraph, and Carver has 21 sentences over 25 words.
9. **"Start headings and subheadings with the words carrying most information" (Pernice); headers of "3-7 word headers over any full sentence" (Speckyboy).** Pernice, NN/g, 2017 (link above); Speckyboy, [8 Super Common Mistakes](https://speckyboy.com/8-super-common-mistakes-beginner-designers-make/), updated Jul 2026. **Here:** partly: "Temporary ban on new data centers" yes; "001- 0555 Morses Lane Solar 1 LLC at 001-0555 Morses Lane" (/upcoming, Acushnet) and a 17-word 40B subject (Holliston) no.
10. **Hard text "took longer on mobile versus computer"; "the need for brevity and prioritization is still critical".** Kate Moran, NN/g, [Reading Content on Mobile Devices](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-content/), 2016. **Here:** partly: 57- and 48-word write-up sentences sit inside Details on Carver and Somerset.
11. **Interaction cost is "the sum of efforts — mental and physical": clicking, scrolling, reading, waiting, remembering; cut each.** Raluca Budiu, NN/g, [Interaction Cost](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/interaction-cost-definition/), 2013, reviewed 2024. **Here:** partly: see Section 5 (two tasks need a page change and a back-tap for what the row could hold).
12. **"Relevance trumps page length"; "people prefer scrolling the page for content over pagination when the topics within that page answer the right questions".** Hoa Loranger, NN/g, [Accordions Are Not Always the Answer](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/accordions-complex-content/), 2014. **Here:** yes: /upcoming is one page of 44 meetings, no pagination.

### Writing and words

13. **"Copywriting is interface design. Great interfaces are written."; "every letter matters"; "Do you label a button Submit or Save or Update or New or Create? That's copywriting."** 37signals, [Copywriting is Interface Design](https://basecamp.com/gettingreal/09.7-copywriting-is-interface-design), 2006. **Here:** yes: "Find my town", "Follow Carver's meetings", "Read the Oct 28 agenda".
14. **"Use plain language"; "Aim for a 7th grade reading level"; "Start sentences with verbs so they feel like actionable instructions"; "Only add content that's necessary for clarity".** Shopify Polaris, [Content fundamentals](https://github.com/Shopify/polaris/blob/main/polaris.shopify.com/content/content/fundamentals.mdx), read from the repository. **Here:** partly: the row sentences are plain; Somerset's write-up sentence appears in five variants of 28 to 48 words ("a Planned Development under Section 6.10 of the zoning bylaw").
15. **"Never use 'click here' or 'here' for link text"; sentence case for headings and buttons; "Use the 12-hour clock, followed by am or pm"; "Don't write dates with numerals only".** Shopify, [Grammar and mechanics](https://github.com/Shopify/polaris/blob/main/polaris.shopify.com/content/content/grammar-and-mechanics.mdx). **Here:** yes: "Wed Oct 28, 6 pm"; sentence case throughout; no "click here".
16. **"We are plainspoken"; "We are translators": explain jargon, don't hide it; "Don't market at people; communicate with them."** Mailchimp, [Voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/) and [Writing principles](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/writing-principles/), 2023. **Here:** partly: "a temporary ban on new data centers (a moratorium)" translates; "a Planned Development under Section 6.10" does not.
17. **Read it aloud: "Does it sound like language you'd ever actually use face to face?"; "no room at all for unexplained financial jargon"; "write in the active voice".** Monzo, [Tone of voice](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice/), undated. **Here:** partly: "Moved from Oct 21. The agenda for that day isn't posted yet; the date comes from the Oct 21 agenda." is a system note, not a sentence a neighbour would say.
18. **"No score can accurately predict whether an actual person would be able to understand your content"; "Only write for 8th graders if your audience is, in fact, an 8th-grade class"; test with people reading aloud. And "user needs come first, then comes the format".** Ginny Redish and Caroline Jarrett, [Readability Formulas: 7 Reasons to Avoid Them](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2019/07/readability-formulas-7-reasons-to-avoid-them-and-what-to-do-instead.php), UXmatters, 2019; Sarah Winters, [What is content design](https://contentdesign.london/blog/what-is-content-design), 2019, reviewed 2024. **Here:** unknown: nothing on the site or in the notes records a read-aloud test with residents.
19. **"Use plainspoken words, the shorter the better"; "Use short sentences"; "Mainly write in the active voice"; "Aim at an 8th-grade reading level if targeting a broad consumer audience".** Nielsen, NN/g, [Legibility, Readability, and Comprehension](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/legibility-readability-comprehension/), 2015. **Here:** partly: 18 sentences over 25 words on /upcoming, 21 on /ma/carver, 6 on /ma/somerset (Section 3).

### Typography

20. **"Point size should be 10–12 points in printed documents, 15–25 pixels on the web"; "Line spacing should be 120–145% of the point size"; "The average line length should be 45–90 characters"; "Use curly quotation marks"; the web's bad habits are "Tiny point sizes for body text", "Huge point sizes for headings" and "Page edges crammed with inscrutable wads of navigational links".** Matthew Butterick, Practical Typography, [Summary of key rules](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html) and [Websites](https://practicaltypography.com/websites.html). **Here:** yes for size (18 px), measure (66ch), curly quotes, h1 32/40 px against 18 px body, three header links; line height 1.55 is just above his range (partly).
21. **"it's very important to use a relative unit like rem for typography"; ask "Should this value scale up as the user increases their browser's default font size?"; "Let your main text size be decided by the browser or the user settings"; and never disable zoom: "Why would you ever do this on a page designed for readability?"** Josh W. Comeau, [The Surprising Truth About Pixels and Accessibility](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/surprising-truth-about-pixels-and-accessibility/), 2022, updated 2025; Andrés Galante, [Accessible Font Sizing, Explained](https://css-tricks.com/accessible-font-sizing-explained/), CSS-Tricks, 2020; kibibu, [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=6791297), 2013; neilv, [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40956192), 2024 ("It doesn't respect the body text size set in the browser"). **Here:** yes: `--t-body: 1.125rem`, all sizes in rem, `initial-scale=1` with zoom allowed; gutters and rules in px, as Comeau's rule suggests.
22. **"The optimal line length for body text is 50–75 characters"; set "max-width to about 70ch or 34em"; users "are more likely to avoid reading" overlong lines.** Edward Scott, Baymard, [Line Length Readability](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability), 2022. **Here:** yes: `--measure: 66ch`.
23. **Body text at "the browser default of 100%, if not larger"; "fit about 10-20 words per line"; contrast "Benchmark: #333 on #fff", not full black; "Web design is not about picking great typefaces, it is how we use them"; treat "text as a user interface". Carbon's body styles are 14 px/20 px and 16 px/24 px, "always left-aligned".** iA (Oliver Reichenstein), [The 100% Easy-2-Read Standard](https://ia.net/topics/100e2r) and [Web Design is 95% Typography](https://ia.net/topics/the-web-is-all-about-typography-period), 2006; IBM Carbon, [Type sets](https://carbondesignsystem.com/guidelines/typography/type-sets/), updated Sep 2026. **Here:** yes: 18 px, ink #1f2724 on #ffffff (15.3:1), left aligned, one family, no icons, nothing visible under 16 px.
24. **"Minimize the number of typefaces you use"; "In general, avoid light font weights"; iOS body is 17 pt; "Make sure your app's layout adapts to all font sizes"; "Prioritize important content when responding to text-size changes." (On which face: Butterick says "Avoid goofy fonts, monospaced fonts, most free fonts, and system fonts"; an HN commenter, zzo38computer 2024, wants the browser to "choose the font": the voices disagree.)** Apple, [HIG: Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography), undated. **Here:** yes: Regular and SemiBold only, 18 px body, rem sizes and a ch measure, no fixed-height rows; Public Sans is one free professional face, a defensible middle.
25. **"Lines of text should be about 45–90 characters long"; "This page just needs max-width set somewhere on the text container"; "Don't specify line widths in px".** Hacker News commenters ender7, gabemart, dredmorbius, [Motherfucking Website thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=6791297), Nov 2013. **Here:** yes: measure in ch; the outer frame is 760 px (partly on the px point).

### Layout and spacing

26. **"each page should have a clear visual hierarchy"; "The more important something is, the more prominent it is"; "Things that are related logically are related visually"; "A good visual hierarchy saves us work by preprocessing the page for us"; and "links and buttons that aren't obviously clickable" put question marks over heads.** Steve Krug, Don't Make Me Think, Revisited, 3rd ed., [excerpts at Peachpit](https://www.peachpit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2209310) ([and](https://www.peachpit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2209309)), 2014. **Here:** partly: in a row the subject (20 px semibold) outranks the who-line (muted 16 px) and every link is underlined, but on the meeting page the grey panel outranks the lead sentence.
27. **"Above all else show the data"; sparklines follow "maximize data density, minimize or zero-out non-data".** Edward Tufte, [Notebook: Sparklines history](https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/), quoting VDQI (1983). **Here:** partly: dots and badges are gone, but each Details panel repeats the row ("Boston · City Council: Discusses battery storage Discussion New on an agenda") and the meeting page's grey "ticket" box is non-data ink.
28. **"UI density is the value a user gets from the interface divided by the time and space the interface occupies."** Matthew Ström, [UI Density](https://mattstromawn.com/writing/ui-density/), 2024. **Here:** partly: /upcoming rows are 223 to 486 px tall at 390 (median 267), so about three meetings fit a screen and 44 meetings run 8,935 px.
29. **Limit content width: studies put reading lines "between 40 and 75 characters per line"; Wikipedia's Vector 2022 settled on "a max-width in the range of 800–1000px" (960 px content).** Wikimedia, [Limiting content width](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Reading/Web/Desktop_Improvements/Features/Limiting_content_width), 2022 to 2023. **Here:** yes: 760 px frame, 66ch measure.
30. **"You don't have to use media queries to make these things happen"; columns "will squish, and then another set will squish"; "It's okay to use pixels again"; "respect the medium of the web" (Simmons). "Be the browser's mentor, not its micromanager"; type scales "that respond to the viewport" (Bell).** Jen Simmons, [Intrinsic Web Design transcript](https://zeldman.com/2018/05/02/transcript-intrinsic-web-design-with-jen-simmons-the-big-web-show/), The Big Web Show, 2018; Andy Bell, [Build Excellent Websites](https://buildexcellentwebsit.es/), Set Studio, 2022. **Here:** partly: one column below 760 px and a date column above, px for gutters and rem for type (yes); fixed rem steps with one breakpoint (h1 32 to 40 px at 641 px) rather than a fluid scale (Bell's point, no).

### Colour

31. **"Insufficient text contrast" is on 83.9% of home pages, the commonest failure.** WebAIM, [The WebAIM Million](https://webaim.org/projects/million/), 2026. **Here:** yes: ink 15.3:1, muted #545f5a 6.6:1, plum #7a2c80 8.4:1 on white; dark mode ink 14.6:1, muted 8.1:1; white on the dark button #9b4aa2 5.4:1 (computed from `styles.css`, WCAG formula).
32. **"None of the default text colors we were using for small text (except for black) met the contrast threshold"; build the palette so that "Any two colors are guaranteed to have sufficient contrast for small text if they are at least five levels apart" (Stripe). "reduce visual noise, maintain visual alignment, and increase the hierarchy"; a "more neutral and timeless appearance" (Linear).** Daryl Koopersmith and Wilson Miner, Stripe, [Designing accessible color systems](https://stripe.com/blog/accessible-color-systems), 2019; Linear, [How we redesigned the Linear UI (part II)](https://linear.app/blog/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui), 2024. **Here:** yes: the seven roles all clear 4.5:1 in both modes; the underline colour `--rule-strong` is 4.46:1 and only decorates.
33. **"color and underline the link text"; "Use different colors for visited and unvisited links"; "Reserve underlining for links."** Nielsen, NN/g, [Guidelines for Visualizing Links](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/guidelines-for-visualizing-links/), 2004, with a Mar 2026 note calling the visuals a starting point. **Here:** partly: links are underlined in ink, not a link colour, and `styles.css` has no `a:visited` rule, so on a 44-row list nothing shows what you have read.

### Navigation and links

34. **"The big problem with the 3-click rule is that it has not been supported by data"; what matters is "strong information scent" and that "3 clicks that each have a long load time lead to a worse experience than 5 clicks that quickly load"; "Hardly anybody gave up after three clicks"; "Fewer clicks do not make more satisfied users."** Page Laubheimer, NN/g, [The 3-Click Rule for Navigation Is False](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/3-click-rule/), 2019; Joshua Porter, UIE, [Testing the Three-Click Rule](https://articles.centercentre.com/three_click_rule/), 2003. **Here:** partly: pages load in under a second, but one Carver link leads somewhere other than it names (Section 5).
35. **"It doesn't matter how many times I have to click, as long as each click is a mindless, unambiguous choice"; "three mindless, unambiguous clicks equal one click that requires thought."** Krug, [excerpt at Peachpit](https://www.peachpit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2170656), 2014. **Here:** partly: the subject link and "Details" on the same row are two paths to overlapping information, a choice that requires thought.
36. **"the most helpful link text describes the page that's being linked to"; "the best links start with the most important words"; avoid "Read more, or Click here" (McCloskey). "It's always good to be as specific as possible with the link names"; "whenever you promise something with a label or an image, you will deliver" (Budiu). "Typical culprits are cute or clever names, marketing-induced names, company-specific names, and unfamiliar technical names" (Krug).** Marieke McCloskey, NN/g, [Writing Hyperlinks](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/writing-links/), 2014; Budiu, NN/g, [Information Scent](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-scent/), 2020; Krug, 2014 (link above). **Here:** partly: "Read the Oct 28 agenda" yes; "Zoning change" links to nine different meetings on /upcoming; "Agenda" four times on /search; "Notice of Intent", "FRCOG", "Section 6.10" still appear bare in some rows.
37. **"always ensure the accessible name ... matches the visual label"; extra text "best come after what's visually shown".** Sara Soueidan, [Accessible Text Labels For All](https://www.sarasoueidan.com/blog/accessible-text-labels/), 2021. **Here:** yes: `Read the Oct 28 agenda<span class="sr-only">: Boston City Council</span>`.
38. **"Users expect navigation to lead to new pages"; in-page links "need to be differentiated from other links on the page".** Amy Schade, NN/g, [Anchors OK? Re-Assessing In-Page Links](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/in-page-links/), 2017. **Here:** no: Carver's "Also coming up" rows are anchor links to past meetings on the same page and look like every other link.
39. **"Use links for navigation to other pages or sections, and buttons for actions performed"; "Links disguised as buttons won't show up in the link list".** Moritz Glantz, [HTMHell #34: a button is not a link](https://www.htmhell.dev/34-a-button-is-not-a-link/), Manuel Matuzović's site, 2024. **Here:** yes: `<a>` for pages, `.link-btn` buttons only for Copy and Share.
40. **"Failing to indicate the current location is probably the single most common mistake we see on website menus"; the hamburger "is not appropriate for desktop".** Laubheimer, NN/g, [Menu-Design Checklist](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/menu-design/), 2024. **Here:** yes: `aria-current="page"` with an underline; breadcrumbs on town and meeting pages.
41. **"Do not move pages around but keep them at the same URL"; "hackable" URLs let people "move to higher levels of the information architecture by hacking off the end".** Nielsen, NN/g, [URL as UI](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/url-as-ui/), 1999. **Here:** yes: /ma/carver, /m/somerset-board-of-selectmen-2026-10-28, and /upcoming?town=Carver%2C+Somerset%2C+Plymouth after filtering.
42. **Pogo-sticking "is an indication that people are struggling to find relevant content"; fix it by improving "the information scent of each item's description on the routing page" (Harley). Without pogo-sticking "55 percent success rate", with it "an 11 percent success rate"; "The back button predicts failure" (Spool).** Aurora Harley, NN/g, [No More Pogo Sticking](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/pogo-sticking/), 2015; Spool, 2012 (link above). **Here:** partly: add-to-calendar and the public-comment line live only on the meeting page, so two tasks pogo (Section 5).

### Forms and inputs

43. **"Every input needs a label"; "Placeholders suck"; "Use hint text to tell users why you're asking"; "Use verbs for button text"; "Only ask for information you need"; "Align the primary button to the left edge of the inputs"; "Forms with multiple buttons are problematic".** Adam Silver, [Form design: from zero to hero](https://adamsilver.io/blog/form-design-from-zero-to-hero-all-in-one-blog-post/) and [Where to put buttons on forms](https://adamsilver.io/blog/where-to-put-buttons-on-forms/), 2019. **Here:** partly: home and /watch have visible labels, hints and one full-width button; the /upcoming Town filter uses `placeholder="Type a town"` and hides the comma rule in a `title` tooltip nobody sees on a phone; /watch step 2 shows three buttons.
44. **"Every time you cut a field or question from a form, you increase its conversion rate"; "Present fields in a single column layout"; "Avoid placeholder text."** Kathryn Whitenton, NN/g, [Website Forms Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-form-design/), 2016. **Here:** yes on /watch (one field, then Continue); partly on /upcoming (placeholder).
45. **One thing per page because "low-confidence users find them easier to use", "they work well on mobile devices", and "they're better at handling things like errors, branches, loops and saving progress"; "User research will tell you when you can merge pages together."** Tim Paul, GDS, [One thing per page](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2015/07/03/one-thing-per-page/), 2015; [GOV.UK Service Manual: Structuring forms](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure), 2016, updated 2018. **Here:** yes: /watch asks for the town, then shows the rest.
46. **Older adults meet "tiny type", small "buttons, dropdowns, and links", and interfaces "inflexible and unforgiving of errors"; error messages should "Focus on the error, explain it clearly, and make it as easy as possible to fix."** Lexie Kane, NN/g, [Usability for Older Adults](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-for-senior-citizens/), 2019. **Here:** yes: 48 px inputs and buttons; the town box accepts "Carver" and matches "Carver, MA".

### Lists of dated items

47. **"Comparison is one of the most critical activities users perform on the web"; "options as columns, attributes as rows"; "When more than 5 items need to be compared, add other mechanisms such as filters".** Kate Moran and Taylor Dykes, NN/g, [Comparison Tables](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/comparison-tables/), 2024. **Here:** no: filtering /upcoming to three towns merges them into one list ("4 of 44 meetings", all Somerset) and says nothing about the two towns with nothing.
48. **"Return Users to the Same Place in the Product List When Returning from the Product Page"; 13% of sites fail, "a direct cause of site abandonments".** Mark Crowley, Baymard, [Return same place](https://baymard.com/research-articles/return-same-place), 2020. **Here:** yes for the browser Back; partly for the page's own way back, which goes to the town page ("Everything coming up in Boston"), not to /upcoming.

### Performance and weight

57. **A 5-second first load on a 75th-percentile phone allows "2.4MiB of HTML, CSS, images, and fonts, plus 100KiB of JavaScript"; "the global baseline budget should be enforced".** Alex Russell, [The Performance Inequality Gap, 2024](https://infrequently.org/2024/01/performance-inequality-gap-2024/), 2024. **Here:** yes: 54 KB of JS (17 KB gzipped) per page; no on /search (6.2 MB of index files).

59. **"A performance budget is where you set a limit for a specific feature of your page", set "as early as possible", testing "on a wide range of devices".** [GOV.UK Service Manual: How to test frontend performance](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/technology/how-to-test-frontend-performance), 2018, updated 2019. **Here:** partly: no budget is written down; /search is what a missing budget looks like.
60. **"Sometimes the smarter way to build things is to try and take some pieces away"; "the potential for error directly increases with its complexity" (Böck). "Generally, the more code you write, the worse web pages get" (ryandrake).** Max Böck, [On Simplicity](https://mxb.dev/blog/on-simplicity/), 2019; Frank Chimero, [Everything Easy Is Hard Again](https://frankchimero.com/blog/2018/everything-easy/), 2018; ryandrake, [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40956192), 2024. **Here:** yes: static HTML, one stylesheet, one script, a strict CSP.

### What to leave out

76. **"Do not use an accordion for content that all users need to see"; "Do not put accordions within accordions"; for "one or two short, less important pieces of content, the details component is more suitable".** [GOV.UK Design System: Accordion](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/accordion/). **Here:** partly: filters in one details is right; /towns nests details in details; the meeting page nests "Agenda wording" details inside an item reached from a row's Details.
77. **"Progressive disclosure defers advanced or rarely used features to a secondary screen"; "designs that go beyond 2 disclosure levels typically have low usability".** Nielsen, NN/g, [Progressive Disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/), 2006. **Here:** partly: row → Details is two levels; row → meeting page → Agenda wording is three.
78. **"Only show what users need right now"; "Use whitespace effectively to separate sections" (Pixel Mosaic). "Knowing what to leave out is as important as understanding what to explain" (Andrew).** Pixel Mosaic, [Common UX mistakes beginners make](https://dev.to/pixel_mosaic/common-ux-mistakes-beginners-make-and-how-to-fix-them-tips-for-branding-and-web-development-4d32), dev.to, undated; Rachel Andrew, [More than words](https://rachelandrew.co.uk/archives/2026/09/30/more-than-words/), Sep 30 2026. **Here:** partly: "New on an agenda" and "(first seen Oct 27)" tell the reader when the crawler saw a thing, which only the crawler cares about; /how-it-works is 815 words with 7 sentences over 25 words.
79. **"Accordions increase interaction cost"; "Hiding content behind navigation diminishes people's awareness of it"; avoid them "when your audience needs most or all of the content on the page".** Loranger, NN/g, 2014 (link above). **Here:** yes: the sentence, the when/where and the agenda link stay open; only item rows fold.
80. **"When all brands are beige, the beigest one will not win but will be forgotten"; type should "be legible, pleasant to look at, work technically across platforms".** Erik Spiekermann, [Re-branding or de-branding?](https://spiekermann.com/en/re-branding-or-de-branding), 2023, and [interview](https://webdesignerdepot.com/?p=35671), 2011. **Here:** partly: the one plum bar in the wordmark and the one plum button are the only things that are not beige; the rest is deliberately plain.

## Section 2: the don'ts most voices agree on

Ranked by how many independent sources, read first hand, say it.

1. **Don't ship heavy pages, trackers or needless JavaScript.** 11 sources: HTTP Archive 2024 (page weight; fonts), Alex Russell 2024, Dan Luu, Max Böck 2019, Jeremy Keith 2016, Frank Chimero 2018, Pinboard, Kagi, motherfuckingwebsite.com, HN 2016 (frik: "25+ third party trackers with 4+MB of waste"; ryandrake 2024), GOV.UK Service Manual (performance budget).
2. **Don't set type small, in px, or override the reader's size and zoom.** 10 sources: Butterick, iA 2006, Comeau 2022, Galante 2020, Allsopp 2000 ("Don't use points"), Kane 2019, Apple HIG, HN 2013 (kibibu on `user-scalable=no`), HN 2016 (kevin_thibedeau: "Nobody should be specifying fonts in pixel sizes"; eevee on 12 px), HN 2024 (neilv).
3. **Don't write long, complex, promotional or jargon-filled sentences.** 9 sources: Nielsen 1997, Nielsen 2015, Krug 2014 ("cute or clever names, marketing-induced names"), Budiu 2020 (jargon lowers scent), Laubheimer 2019 ("vague, unfamiliar, or branded terms"), Polaris, Mailchimp, Monzo, Rachel Andrew 2026.
4. **Don't hide what people need behind clicks: accordions, subpages, drill-downs, hamburgers.** 8 sources: Loranger 2014, Budiu 2017, Budiu 2015 (mobile accordions, with conditions), GOV.UK accordion guidance, Baymard Scott 2020, Wroblewski 2015, Laubheimer 2024, Nielsen 2006 (two levels at most).
5. **Don't use vague or repeated link text; don't promise what the link won't deliver.** 7 sources: McCloskey 2014, Budiu 2020, Harley 2015, Spool 2012, Laubheimer 2019, Shopify grammar ("Never use 'click here'"), Krug 2014.
6. **Don't let lines run past about 75 to 90 characters, or under 45.** 7 sources: Butterick, Baymard 2022, iA 2006, Wikimedia 2022, HN 2013 (ender7, gabemart), Rendle 2016 (reports the 75-character rule), Apple HIG (layout guides "restrict the width of text for optimal readability").
7. **Don't put the important thing below the fold or after chrome.** 7 sources: Pernice 2017, Fessenden 2018, Budiu 2015, 37signals (epicenter), Spool 2012, Apple HIG layout, Krug 2014 (hierarchy).
8. **Don't use low contrast or a muddle of colours.** 6 sources: WebAIM 2026, Stripe 2019, iA 2006, Speckyboy, Linear 2024, Pixel Mosaic.
9. **Don't break the Back button or make an in-page change look like a new page.** 5 sources: Baymard Holst 2020, Friedman 2022, Budiu 2015, Schade 2017, Nielsen 1999 (permanent URLs).
10. **Don't make tap targets small or crowded.** 4 sources: Harley 2019, Google Android help, Kane 2019, Budiu 2015.
11. **Don't use many typefaces or light weights.** 4 sources: Apple HIG, Speckyboy, Butterick, Reichenstein 2006.
12. **Don't trust a rule of thumb over evidence: the three-click rule, readability scores.** 4 sources: Laubheimer 2019, Porter 2003, Redish and Jarrett 2019, Krug 2014.
13. **Don't use placeholders as labels or ask for what you don't need.** 3 sources: Silver 2019, Whitenton 2016, Paul 2015.
14. **Don't paginate or infinite-scroll a list unless it helps the task.** 2 sources: Loranger 2014, GOV.UK pagination.

## Section 5: click cost and dense displays

### 5.1 What the evidence says

- **Clicks are not the cost; thought, waiting and lost scent are.** The three-click rule "has not been supported by data in any published studies to date" (Laubheimer, NN/g, [2019](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/3-click-rule/)); UIE's 2003 test found "Hardly anybody gave up after three clicks" and "it isn't until 15 clicks that we see 80% of our tasks completed" (Porter, [2003](https://articles.centercentre.com/three_click_rule/)). Krug's second law: "It doesn't matter how many times I have to click, as long as each click is a mindless, unambiguous choice" ([2014](https://www.peachpit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2170656)). Interaction cost adds up "Clicking or touching", "Scrolling", "Reading", "Page loads" and "Attention" (Budiu, NN/g, [2013](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/interaction-cost-definition/)).
- **Information scent decides where people go.** Scent is "the user's imperfect estimate of the value that the source will deliver", and vague labels such as "Learn more" make it "difficult to predict where a link leads" (Budiu, NN/g, [2020](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-scent/)). Spool's clickstreams: "The back button predicts failure"; without pogo-sticking "55 percent success rate", with it "an 11 percent success rate"; "two-thirds of all purchases happen with no pogo sticking whatsoever" ([2012](https://archive.uie.com/brainsparks/2012/12/12/jared-spool-the-secret-lives-of-links/)). Pogo-sticking is "the hub-and-spoke pattern of navigating from a routing page" into a page and straight back; the fix is to "Improve the information scent of each item's description on the routing page" (Harley, NN/g, [2015](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/pogo-sticking/)).
- **Expand in place versus a separate page.** Accordions "increase interaction cost" and "diminish people's awareness" of hidden content; avoid them "when your audience needs most or all of the content on the page"; "On the desktop it is easier to simply scroll the page than to decide on which topics to click on" (Loranger, NN/g, [2014](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/accordions-complex-content/)). On mobile they are "one of the most useful design elements" because users "get the big picture before focusing on details", but "prevent the illusion of a new page by not scrolling the page when the accordion is expanded", or users press Back and leave (Budiu, NN/g, [2015](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-accordions/)). Progressive disclosure: "designs that go beyond 2 disclosure levels typically have low usability" (Nielsen, [2006](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). Baymard: "avoid using subpages for product page sections"; use collapsed sections, because "a subgroup of their users will never find" subpage content, and 26% of mobile sites get this wrong (Scott, [2020](https://baymard.com/research-articles/avoid-using-subpages)). GOV.UK: "Do not use an accordion for content that all users need to see"; for "one or two short, less important pieces of content, the details component is more suitable"; "Do not put accordions within accordions" ([Design System](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/accordion/)).
- **Drill-down versus expand in place on mobile.** Sequential menus "can disorient users" and "cause users to accidentally make mistakes" with Back; "we generally do not recommend them"; accordions suit "fewer than 6 subcategories" (Budiu, NN/g, [2017](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-subnavigation/)). Hidden navigation cuts use: "People were no longer moving between the major sections of the app" after Polar hid its menu (Wroblewski, [2015](https://www.lukew.com/ff/entry.asp?1945)).
- **In-page links and Back.** "Users expect navigation to lead to new pages"; in-page links "do not meet users' mental model for a link", so they "need to be differentiated" (Schade, NN/g, [2017](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/in-page-links/)). "if users are jumped down the page, then tapping Back should return them to the previous position"; filters, overlays and accordion steps that feel like new views need `history.pushState()`, while "Truncated content should in general never be implemented as a new page" (Holst, Baymard, [2020](https://baymard.com/blog/back-button-expectations)); "we should avoid adding states to the user's history stack" for toggles and injected sections (Friedman, Smashing, [2022](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2022/08/designing-better-back-button-ux/)). Users "will often try to guess or remember the site name instead of using a bookmark"; "keep them at the same URL" (Nielsen, [1999](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/url-as-ui/)).
- **List to detail.** Send people back to where they were: 13% of sites return users to the top of the list, "a direct cause of site abandonments" (Crowley, Baymard, [2020](https://baymard.com/research-articles/return-same-place)). Pagination only when a single page is slow or "most users will only need the content on the first page" ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/pagination/)); "Endless scrolling is not recommended for goal-oriented finding tasks" (Loranger, NN/g, [2014](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/infinite-scrolling/)).
- **One thing per page versus a long page.** GOV.UK splits forms so people can "focus on the specific question", "use the service on a mobile device" and "recover easily from form errors", and says "User research will tell you when you can merge pages together" ([Service Manual](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure)). That is a rule for forms, not for reading lists; for reading, NN/g's "Relevance trumps page length" applies (Loranger 2014).
- **Comparison.** "Comparison is one of the most critical activities users perform on the web"; "options as columns, attributes as rows"; "When more than 5 items need to be compared, add other mechanisms such as filters" (Moran and Dykes, NN/g, [2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/comparison-tables/)).
- **Density.** "UI density is the value a user gets from the interface divided by the time and space the interface occupies"; Bloomberg's Terminal is "perhaps the most common example of this kind of density", and its advantage is that "the data loads almost instantly" (Ström, [2024](https://mattstromawn.com/writing/ui-density/)). Tufte: "maximize data density, minimize or zero-out non-data" ([notebook](https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/)).

## Summary

1. 62 voices were read first hand; the Guardian, NYT, BBC, Radio Times, Eventbrite, Reddit, Atlassian and Material pages could not be read and are not quoted.
2. The don'ts with the most independent backing are: don't ship weight or trackers (11 sources), don't set small or px type (10), don't write long or jargon-filled sentences (9), don't hide needed content behind clicks (8), don't repeat vague link text (7), don't let lines run long (7), don't bury the important thing (7).
3. The site keeps most of them: 5 requests and about 104 KB on the wire for the home page, zero third-party requests, 18 px rem-based type at a 66ch measure, 15.3:1 contrast, 44 to 52 px controls, no horizontal scroll at 320 px, visible navigation, permanent URLs.
4. It breaks them in four places: /search opens at 217 requests and 6.6 MB; 18 to 21 sentences over 25 words on /upcoming and /ma/carver; "Zoning change" links to nine different pages; Carver's "Also coming up" rows are anchor links that jump the reader up the page to a past meeting.
5. The first row sits at 575 px on /upcoming and 651 px on home at 390 wide, which is inside the first screen; on /ma/carver the first future item is at 1,722 px because the past meeting comes first and the lead recites what the rows should show.
6. Click cost: finding whether anything is coming up costs 2 taps and no scrolling; reading an agenda costs 1 tap; adding a meeting to a calendar costs a page change and a back; the solar-hearing question costs 2 taps, 2 scrolls, a back and still no sentence about speaking.
7. The row's two paths are the wrong way round: Details (in place) repeats the row, while the meeting page (a load and a back) holds the public-comment line, the quote and the calendar link.
8. The best-regarded dense lists (Hacker News, Craigslist, Legistar, Boston.gov, Wikipedia's current events) put every deciding fact in the row, group by day where time matters, never nest disclosures, and reserve the click for the source document.
9. The fifteen changes that matter most, in order: lazy-load the search index; fix the Carver anchor rows; calendar link in the row; Details holds the extras and no empty Details; unique link text; first future row in the first screen; say truthfully what Follow gives; group filtered results by town; cap generator sentences at 25 words; visited-link style; visible hint on the Town field; flatten /towns; calendar and agenda at the top of the meeting page without the grey box; shorter generic subjects; two 44 px targets.
10. None of the fifteen needs a framework, a second script or a new colour; all are generator rules, a few CSS lines, and one change in `search.js`.
