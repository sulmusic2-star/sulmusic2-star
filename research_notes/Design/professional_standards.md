# Professional standards for a home page and a first use: the checklist, the rebuilt home page, the first use, and the 15 edits

Written Mon Nov 2, 2026. This note adds to three earlier studies and repeats none of them: `research_notes/Design/human_first_spec.md` (the row, the sentence generator, the page order), `research_notes/Design/how_websites_should_be.md` (62 voices on reading, type, weight, click cost) and `research_notes/Design/ai_design_rules.md` (AI tells and the design brief). Where a rule already lives in one of those, it is not restated. The question here is narrower: what reputable, recent sources say makes a home page and a first use feel professional and easy for an ordinary person on an information service with no sign-up wall, and how https://agendaahead.vercel.app scores against that today.

## Method

Every source below was read on the page linked next to it today, unless marked second-hand. Forty-eight distinct sources were read first hand. The live site was read from the HTML of eleven pages saved to `scratchpad/design4/page_*.html`, from `styles.css` and `app.js`, and rendered with the preinstalled Chromium at 390×844, 1366×900 and 320×700 (`scratchpad/design4/measure.js`; numbers in `measure.json`; screenshots in `scratchpad/design4/shots/` as `<page>_<width>_fold.png` and `_full.png`, flow screenshots as `flow_<town>_result_<width>_fold.png`). Six town-box flows were run (Carver, Arlington, Harwich, Springfield, Boston, Xyzzy) and the follow flow to its second step.

Pages that blocked or failed automated reading, not bypassed: julian.com's growth guide (404; the same handbook was read at /guide/startup/landing-pages); documenters.org (403 twice; City Bureau's own Documenters page and the network startup guide were read instead); the Harvard CSCI E-12 slides that quote Krug (503 three times); sensible.com (no chapter text); Medium summaries of Krug (403); GOV.UK's published-dates component guide (500); goodui.org/patterns/all (404; the home page and two pattern pages were read); growth.design's Duolingo study (paywalled past slide 3; the Headspace and Too Good To Go studies read in full). Linear's home page renders only with JavaScript, so it was read in a normal headless browser, which is not a bot check. Krug's four home-page questions are therefore cited second-hand from search snippets of course slides and book notes. No first-hand piece by Julie Zhuo on polish or by Joel Spolsky on product pages was reachable; neither is cited.

Numbers for the live site, measured today at 390 px wide unless stated: home H1 at 141 px, town box and button at 365 px, first "This week" row at 651 px, 105 words in the first screen (142 at 1366); one typeface, 7 colour values in use, one corner radius (4 px) on the home page, nothing under 16 px, no horizontal scroll at 320 px on any page; home weight about 104 KB over the wire (HTML 7.8, CSS 11.9, JS 17.6, two fonts 67.2) with no third-party requests; all 40 internal links checked from the home page returned 200. The home page says "We read the agendas every morning; last checked Oct 30, 2:33 pm" and today is Nov 2.

## Section 1: the professional-standards checklist, scored

Each item: the rule; who says so (name, link, year); then pass / fail / partly for the live site, with the page and element.

### A. The home page's first screen

1. **The first screen says what the site does in one sentence, in the reader's words, not a welcome or a slogan.** Huei-Hsin Wang, NN/g, [Homepage Design: 5 Fundamental Principles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/), Mar 15, 2024 (2.2: a tagline that "explicitly conveys what your site or company does"); Jakob Nielsen, NN/g, [Top 10 Guidelines for Homepage Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/top-ten-guidelines-for-homepage-usability/), May 11, 2002 (#1 "Include a one-sentence tagline"). **Partly.** Home H1 "See what your town is about to decide" is a promise, not a description; the sentence under it ("Type your town. We'll tell you in plain words what its boards have coming up, with a link to each agenda.") does the describing. Neither names meetings, agendas or the three states.
2. **A visitor who reads only the headline knows exactly what the site offers; a slogan fails this test; "Specificity is step one."** Julian Shapiro, [Landing Pages handbook](https://www.julian.com/guide/startup/landing-pages), undated. **Partly.** "See what your town is about to decide" could head a ballot site, a news site or a petition tool. The specific nouns (solar, data centers, zoning, housing, agendas) sit in the meta description and in How it works, not in the headline.
3. **Say why this and not the alternative: positioning is "how a product is different from and better than alternatives for a specific set of customers".** April Dunford, [aprildunford.com](https://www.aprildunford.com/), read Nov 2, 2026 (her five components, alternatives first, are summarised second-hand by [Heinz Marketing](https://www.heinzmarketing.com/?p=14584)); Wang 2024 (2.3: answer "Why should I choose this site over others?"). **Fail.** No sentence on the home page names the alternative (reading each board's PDF agenda on the town website, or finding out after the vote) or why this is better (every morning, one sentence per item, the agenda linked). The first screen never says the obvious thing a neighbour would say: "so you know before the meeting, not after."
4. **The value proposition lands inside 10 seconds; departures peak in the first 10 seconds and only level off after 30.** Jakob Nielsen, NN/g, [How Long Do Users Stay on Web Pages?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/), Sep 11, 2011. **Pass.** H1, one sentence, label, box and button are all inside 365 px on a phone; 105 words in the first screen is a 25-second read, and the first 40 words carry the point.
5. **The first screen names the coverage so a first-time visitor can tell whether the site is for them; first-time users "rely heavily on the main navigation options and the homepage content" to judge a site's type and breadth.** Edward Scott, Baymard, [Can Users Infer the Breadth of Your Product Catalog?](https://baymard.com/blog/inferring-product-catalog-from-homepage), Feb 4, 2014, updated Apr 10, 2025. **Fail.** "Massachusetts, New York and Rhode Island" first appears in How it works at about 1,850 px on a phone and in the footer. A visitor from Connecticut types a town and lands on "0 of 447 towns. No towns match."
6. **Show the real thing above the fold: "Reveal Content Through Examples", "Specifics beat abstractions", "Screen captures and videos: Show people what the app actually looks like."** Wang 2024 (3.1, 3.2); Nielsen 2002 (#6 "Show examples of real content"); 37signals, [Getting Real: A Powerful Promo Site](https://basecamp.com/gettingreal/13.2-a-powerful-promo-site), 2006. **Pass.** "This week" begins at 604 px with three real rows (Southbridge, Methuen, Taunton data-center bans), and "What Carver's page says this morning" follows. The first row's heading is inside the first screen at both widths. <!-- slop-lint: ignore (quotes a source or a pattern name) -->
7. **One primary action, visually heaviest, labelled by what happens; "illuminate a clear path to completion".** Luke Wroblewski, [Primary & Secondary Actions in Web Forms](https://www.lukew.com/ff/entry.asp?571), Aug 27, 2007; Wang 2024 (4.1, 4.2). **Pass, with a wording gap.** One filled plum button, "Find my town", at 365 px; "Follow a town" in the header is outlined, so secondary. "Find my town" names the search, not the result; see change 5.
8. **No large hero image, carousel, popup, splash or motion on the home page; a search box that is the page works when the site exists for that one task.** Kathryn Whitenton, NN/g, [Image-Focused Design: Is Bigger Better?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/image-focused-design/), Sep 28, 2014 (Bing's "huge white search box"); Wang 2024 (5.2, 5.4). **Pass.** No image, no carousel, no popup, no animation on any page measured.
9. **The site name and mark sit top left, link home, and the page is distinct from interior pages.** Wang 2024 (1.1, 2.1); Nielsen, NN/g, [Deep Linking Is Good Linking](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/deep-linking-is-good-linking/), Mar 2, 2002. **Pass.** Mark plus "Agenda Ahead" at top left on every page, linking to /; interior pages add breadcrumbs ("Towns › Massachusetts › Carver").
10. **The window title begins with the information-carrying word, usually the site name; "Don't start with words like 'The' or 'Welcome to'."** Nielsen 2002 (#2). **Partly.** Interior pages are right ("Carver, Massachusetts: what the town is about to decide | Agenda Ahead"); the home title is "See what your town is about to decide | Agenda Ahead", so in a bookmark list or a tab strip it alphabetises under S and the name is clipped first.

### B. First use and time to value

11. **No login wall and no sign-up before value; "Ask for registration after delivering value."** Raluca Budiu, NN/g, [Login Walls Stop Users in Their Tracks](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/login-walls/), Mar 2, 2014. **Pass.** No account anywhere; a town page is one keypress away; Follow asks for nothing until it has shown what you get.
12. **No onboarding tutorial; let people start, and reveal help in context ("pull revelations").** Page Laubheimer, NN/g, [Onboarding Tutorials vs. Contextual Help](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/onboarding-tutorials/), Feb 12, 2023. **Pass.** No overlay, tour or coach marks; the town box's hint is one line under the field.
13. **The first value arrives in one step and is the person's own case, not a generic demo: lead with "why", then "show", then "offer"; "Don't start with how to use the app."** growth.design, [3 UX Tips To Make "Aha Moments" Click](https://growth.design/case-studies/too-good-to-go-onboarding), undated; growth.design, [How To Properly Apply Jobs-To-Be-Done To User Onboarding](https://growth.design/case-studies/headspace-user-onboarding), undated ("A paywall before I even see the app!" earns Headspace a C). **Pass.** Type "Carver", press Enter, and /ma/carver answers in a sentence at 243 px. Measured: one keypress after typing, under 2 seconds.
14. **Build the first run around the job the person came for, not the product's features; "drive new customers to an 'aha' moment, not a tour through product features."** Robbie Allan, Intercom, [How to retain more users with value-based onboarding](https://www.intercom.com/blog/retain-users-with-value-based-onboarding/), Oct 22, 2018 (opinion piece, no numbers). **Pass.** /watch step 2 says "Over the last 30 days, Somerset and 5 towns next to it had 7 meetings on these topics, so expect about that many alerts a month. Coming up next:" and lists them before asking for anything.
15. **A multi-step flow shows which step you are on and names the steps; "Communicate a clear mental model of the process."** Raluca Budiu, NN/g, [Wizards: Definition and Design Recommendations](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wizards/), Jun 25, 2017. **Partly.** /watch has two steps (town, then what you get and how) with a "Continue" button and a "Change town" link, but no "Step 1 of 2" and no name for step 2 until it loads.
16. **Every screen has three designed states: regular, blank and error; the blank state answers "What is this page? What do I do now?"** 37signals, [Getting Real: Three State Solution](https://basecamp.com/gettingreal/09.3-three-state-solution), 2006; Kate Kaplan, NN/g, [Designing Empty States in Complex Applications](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/), Sep 19, 2021 (status, a learning cue, a direct pathway; never leave the space empty). **Partly.** Regular (Somerset), blank (Springfield: "Nothing is on Springfield's agendas right now about solar, batteries, data centers, zoning or housing. The last was the City Council on Tue Oct 27...") and error (Arlington: "We can't read Arlington's agendas yet... Call the Arlington Town Clerk or check arlingtonma.gov") all exist and each gives a next step. The blank state lacks the learning cue Kaplan asks for: when we look again, how often this town has had something, and what following would do.
17. **An unknown or ambiguous entry is explained next to the field, in words, after the person has finished, and not by colour alone.** Rachel Krause, NN/g, [10 Design Guidelines for Reporting Errors in Forms](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/errors-forms-design-guidelines/), Feb 3, 2019, reviewed Dec 12, 2024 (#3 next to the field, #5 an icon as well as colour, #7 "avoid showing an error until the user has finished with the field"). **Partly.** Typing "Boston" shows "Boston is in Massachusetts and New York. Which one?" in red beside the box, which is the right message, but it appears while the suggestion list is still open and the list covers most of it (`flow_boston_result_390_fold.png`: only "...setts and New York." shows). There is no icon. "Xyzzy" leaves the home page for /towns?q=Xyzzy, which says "0 of 447 towns" and "No towns match. Check the spelling, or search by county." That is clear, but the person has lost the page they were on.
18. **Suggestions as you type show the state, and Enter picks the only match.** GOV.UK and USWDS combobox conventions as read in `civic_rules.md` (earlier study); observed on the live site. **Pass.** "Carver MA" appears after two letters; Enter opens /ma/carver; two Bostons are offered with their states.
19. **Time to value for an information site is seconds; the SaaS median is 1 day 12 hours (vendor benchmark), which is the wrong yardstick here.** James Mitchinson, Userpilot, [Time to Value](https://userpilot.com/blog/time-to-value/), Jun 6, 2026, updated Aug 14, 2026 (Userpilot's own 547-company report; vendor data). Katryna Balboni, Appcues, [The aha moment guide](https://www.appcues.com/blog/aha-moment-guide), Sep 13, 2024 (vendor; the only outside number it cites is Chameleon's: 3-step tours finish 72% of the time, 7-step tours 16%). **Pass.** One field, one button, one page.
20. **The home page repeats its one action at the end, after the explanation, so a reader who scrolled to decide does not have to scroll back.** Shapiro (handbook: hero, proof, call to action, features, "Repeat the call-to-action", footer); GoodUI, [Idea 5: Repetition, 4 patterns](https://goodui.org/), read Nov 2, 2026 (effects paywalled; cited as a tested pattern only). **Fail.** The home page ends with How it works, "Report a problem with this page" and the footer; the town box appears once, at the top.

### C. Trust and credibility

21. **Show that a real organisation or person stands behind the site; "Show that honest and trustworthy people stand behind your site"; "Make it easy to contact you" with an address or email.** B.J. Fogg, Stanford, [Stanford Guidelines for Web Credibility](https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines/index.html), May 2002 (#2, #4, #5); Aurora Harley, NN/g, [Trustworthiness in Web Design: 4 Credibility Factors](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/), May 8, 2016 ("Upfront Disclosure": contact details prominent); Anna Kaley and Jakob Nielsen, NN/g, ['About Us' Information on Websites](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/), May 26, 2019 (users look in the About section first, then the footer; copy that is plain "eased fears and skepticism"). **Fail.** No page names a person, a group, a town or a reason for the site. The footer says what it is not ("It isn't a government website and isn't part of any town"). /contact is a form; it gives no email address, names nobody and states no reply time; the footer group is headed "About" but holds no About page.
22. **Make it easy to verify every claim: "people can check that facts or claims correspond to reliable sources"; an inline citation "directly supports" the material.** Wikipedia, [Wikipedia:Verifiability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability), revision of Oct 17, 2026; Fogg 2002 (#1); The Trust Project, [The 8 Trust Indicators](https://thetrustproject.org/trust-indicators/), read Nov 2, 2026 ("References"). **Pass.** Every row links its agenda ("Read the Oct 27 agenda (PDF): Carver Planning Board"); Details carries "Quoted from the agenda" wording; the meeting page shows "Open the agenda (PDF) (first seen Oct 17)".
23. **Say how the work is done and by whom: "Methods", "Labels" (what kind of content this is), and that machine-written text may be wrong.** The Trust Project (Methods, Labels); Apple HIG on generative AI as read in `ai_design_rules.md`; City Bureau, [Documenters](https://www.citybureau.org/documenters), read Nov 2, 2026 (notes are "edited by Documenters Network staff" before publication, and the network's [startup guide](https://sites.google.com/citybureau.org/documenters-startup-guide/running-assignments/production) says "journalists often serve as the editors who review and approve Documenters' meeting notes"). **Partly.** Town pages say "A program writes each sentence from the agenda; the link under it is the source." That line is missing on the home page, /upcoming and the meeting page, and nowhere does the site say whether a person ever checks a sentence, or how to report one that is wrong beyond the generic "Report a problem with this page".
24. **Content is current and the site shows when it was last updated or reviewed: "Update your site's content often (at least show it's been reviewed recently)"; "Comprehensive, Correct, and Current"; show "Updated June 27, 2024" in the same place on every page.** Fogg 2002 (#8); Harley 2016; U.S. digital.gov, [Content timeliness indicator standard](https://standards.digital.gov/standards/content-timeliness-indicator/), draft updated Oct 31, 2024; NHS, [Know that a page is up to date](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/design-system/patterns/know-that-a-page-is-up-to-date), Nov 2025 (dates set off near the footer "went unnoticed in testing"; moved nearer the content, "users read them and found them helpful"). **Fail today.** The home page reads "We read the agendas every morning; last checked Oct 30, 2:33 pm" and Carver's page "We read the agendas Carver posts online Oct 30, 7:07 am" on Mon Nov 2. Three days with no check, next to a claim of every morning, is the single most visible contradiction on the site. The time also carries no year, no day name and no time zone, and lives in a different sentence on each page.
25. **Typos, broken links and other mistakes "quickly degrade credibility"; "Avoid errors of all types, no matter how small they seem."** Harley 2016; Fogg 2002 (#10). **Partly.** All 40 internal links checked from the home page return 200 and no misspelling was found. Two visible mistakes remain: on /watch step 2 the next-meeting list renders as "SwanseaPlanning BoardMon Nov 2" with no spaces or separators (`watch_step2_390.png`), and the Boston error message is covered by the suggestion list (item 17).
26. **The site is connected to the rest of the web: it links out, and others can be seen linking in or mentioning it.** Harley 2016 ("Connected to the Rest of the Web"); Nielsen, NN/g, [Trust or Bust](https://www.nngroup.com/alertbox/990307.html), Mar 7, 1999 (second-hand via Harley's summary). **Partly.** Hundreds of outbound links to town sites and agendas; nothing inbound is shown (no press mention, no "used by", no public code), and there is no claim of any.
27. **Be upfront about limits and what the service cannot do, in the same words everywhere; "whenever you promise something with a label... you will deliver."** Harley 2016 ("Upfront Disclosure"); GOV.UK Design System, [Phase banner](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/phase-banner/), read Nov 2, 2026 ("This is a new service. We're still working on ways to improve it." on every page until a live assessment). **Partly.** /how-it-works lists exactly which towns cannot be read and why (ten reasons, counts, examples) and /watch says "We can't email yet." But the home page's "315 towns" and the /towns page's "447 towns" sit without the bridge sentence ("we read 315 of the 447"), and the Follow header button promises "Follow a town" while the only delivery is a page and a calendar feed.
28. **Invite feedback and show corrections: "Actionable Feedback" and "corrects errors quickly and prominently".** The Trust Project (indicator 8); Fessenden 2019 (contact in the footer). **Partly.** "Report a problem with this page" on every page carries the page's path to /contact, and the form's first choice is "A correction, a problem on a page, or town records". No page says what happens next or shows a correction made.
29. **Publish the method behind the numbers and the performance of the service.** GOV.UK, [Service Standard](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard), read Nov 2, 2026 (#10 "Define what success looks like and publish performance data", #12 "Make new source code open"). **Partly.** /how-it-works gives "We looked at 4,116 agendas from 103 towns, for meetings from Apr 13, 2026 to Oct 9, 2026" and the posting-lead figure. Nothing on how many sentences were wrong, how many towns were added this month, or whether the code can be seen.
30. **No ads, no promotional content, no tracking, and say so.** Fogg 2002 (#9); Harley 2016; Pinboard and Kagi as read in `how_websites_should_be.md`. **Pass.** Zero third-party requests on every page measured; /privacy: "We don't use accounts, cookies or tracking."
31. **The site looks like what it is: a plain, well-set civic notice, not a startup landing page.** Fogg 2002 (#6 "appropriate for your purpose"); Leisa Reichelt on Craigslist as read in the earlier study; Harley 2016 ("Design Quality"). **Pass.** Paper, ink, hairlines, one plum, Public Sans, no gradient, no cards on the home page.
32. **About and contact are findable where people look: the About section, then the footer.** Kaley and Nielsen 2019; Therese Fessenden, NN/g, [Footers 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/footers/), Feb 24, 2019 (users go to the footer "intentionally" for "contact information, details about the company"; footer content should be "consistent, predictable"). **Partly.** The footer has Contact, Privacy and an "About" group; the group lacks an About page and an email address, so it answers "how" but not "who".

### D. Consistency and polish

33. **One typeface, or two at most, used consistently.** Anthony Hobday, [Visual design rules you can safely follow every time](https://anthonyhobday.com/sideprojects/saferules/), Feb 2023 (date second-hand from Gigazine's Feb 9, 2023 coverage) (#23); Erik D. Kennedy, [7 Rules for Creating Gorgeous UI, Part 2](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-2.html), updated Jun 15, 2024 (#6 "something pretty neutral & clean"). **Pass.** Public Sans only, on every page measured, Regular and SemiBold.
34. **Grayscale first, then one accent "only with purpose"; "too many colors in too many places is a really easy way to screw up clean/simple".** Erik D. Kennedy, [7 Rules, Part 1](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html), updated Jun 15, 2024 (#2); Refactoring UI, [Building your color palette](https://www.refactoringui.com/previews/building-your-color-palette), 2018 (8 to 10 greys, "one, maybe two" primaries, accents "used pretty sparingly"). **Pass.** Seven colour values in use on the home page: paper, ink, muted, rule, panel, plum, plum-wash. Amber appears only on a moved date; `--ok` and `--err` exist for forms only. <!-- slop-lint: ignore (quotes a source or the live site verbatim) -->
35. **Near-black and near-white, tinted neutrals, high contrast where it matters.** Hobday 2023 (#1, #2, #3). **Pass.** Ink #1f2724 on white; greys carry a green cast (#545f5a, #d6ddd8); 15.3:1 body contrast (earlier study).
36. **"Measurements should be mathematically related": one spacing scale, one radius, one line-height system.** Hobday 2023 (#11, #24); Refactoring UI as above. **Partly.** Type is on a scale (16, 18, 20, 24, 32/40). Spacing is not: margin, padding and gap values in `styles.css` include 2, 5, 6, 10, 12, 14 and 22 px alongside the 4-px grid the spec promises. Radius is 4 px everywhere except the grey "Town or county" panel on /towns and the filter panel on /upcoming (10 px) and three 999-px pills.
37. **"Double your whitespace"; space between lines, between elements, and more outside a group than inside.** Kennedy 2024 (#3); Hobday 2023 (#19 outer padding ≥ inner). **Pass.** 1.55 line height at 18 px; 24-px gutters; sections separated by 48 px or more on the home page.
38. **Clear visual hierarchy: "The more important something is, the more prominent it is", related things related visually, nesting shown.** Steve Krug, Don't Make Me Think, Revisited, 3rd ed., 2014, excerpt at [Peachpit](https://www.peachpit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2209310), Apr 29, 2014. **Pass on the home page; partly on the meeting page.** Home: H1 32/40 px, section H2 24 px, row headings 20 px SemiBold, meta 16 px muted. Meeting page: the lead sentence already says when, where and whether you can speak, then a six-pair label/value list says it again with equal weight for "Watch: YouTube" and "When: Thu Nov 5, 6 pm".
39. **"Labels are a last resort": fold the label into the value ("12 left in stock") or make the label the quiet half.** Refactoring UI, [Labels are a last resort](https://www.refactoringui.com/previews/labels-are-a-last-resort), 2018. **Partly.** The meeting page's "When / Where / Watch / Public comment / Agenda / Calendar" list is label: value with the label in muted text (the right treatment), but it repeats the sentence above it word for word.
40. **"Everything should be aligned with something else"; one left edge; optical alignment over mathematical.** Hobday 2023 (#5, #8). **Pass.** One left edge at 16 px (phone) and a 760-px column from 760 px up; the date column from the spec aligns rows on desktop.
41. **Design with real words, never dummy text: "Dummy text means you won't see the inevitable variations that show up once real information is entered."** 37signals, [Getting Real: Use Real Words](https://basecamp.com/gettingreal/11.4-use-real-words), 2006. **Pass.** Every page is built from the day's agendas; the long Carver warrant sentence shows the variation Fried warns about, which is why the earlier studies cap sentences at 25 words.
42. **Dark mode and reduced motion honoured without a second design.** Vercel web-interface-guidelines as read in `ai_design_rules.md`. **Pass.** `prefers-color-scheme: dark` with ink/paper/plum swapped (`home_390_dark_fold.png`); `prefers-reduced-motion` block present.
43. **Controls are 44 px or taller on phones, including secondary buttons.** NN/g touch-target guidance as read in `how_websites_should_be.md`. **Partly.** Inputs and primary buttons are 48 to 52 px. The meeting page's "Copy citation" is 25 px and "Copy link" 40 px; "Details" summaries are 40 px; the "Why" and example links inside sentences are inline text, which is acceptable.
44. **The same wording for the same thing everywhere (title, H1, social card, description).** Harley 2016 (design quality includes consistency); Fogg 2002 (#10). **Partly.** Four different taglines describe the site: the H1 and `<title>` ("See what your town is about to decide"), the OG image ("See what's coming up at your town meetings"), `og:image:alt` ("Agenda Ahead: what's coming up at your town meetings") and the meta description ("Type your town to see its next meetings about solar, battery storage, data centers, zoning and housing in MA, NY and RI."). The /towns lead uses a fifth ("Pick your town to see what its boards have coming up.").
45. **The product itself is shown, not a picture of it, when the product is text.** Observed at [Linear](https://linear.app/) (headline "The product development system for teams and agents", one sentence, then the live app's Inbox and Projects screens inside the first screen), [Notion](https://www.notion.com/) (headline, one sentence, "Get Notion free" plus "Request a demo", product images, then a logo row), [Stripe](https://stripe.com/) (headline, one sentence, "Get started", then a logo row) and [Vercel](https://vercel.com/) (headline, one sentence, "Deploy now" plus "Talk to sales"), all read Nov 2, 2026. The common order is headline, one sentence, one primary action, then proof or the product. **Pass.** The home page follows that order and its "proof" is three real rows rather than logos, which is the honest equivalent for an information service. <!-- slop-lint: ignore (quotes a source or the live site verbatim) -->

### E. Writing

46. **Objective words beat promotional ones: the objective version scored 27% better than the promotional one, the combined concise, scannable, objective version 124% better; "questioning the credibility of promotional statements may distract users from processing the meaning".** John Morkes and Jakob Nielsen, NN/g, [Concise, SCANNABLE, and Objective](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/), 1997. **Pass.** No "powerful", "smart", "never miss"; the only claims are counts and times. <!-- slop-lint: ignore (quotes a source or the live site verbatim) -->
47. **Plain language for everyone: sentences of 15 to 20 words, one idea per paragraph, familiar words, terms defined on first use; "No one has ever complained that a text was too easy to understand."** Hoa Loranger, NN/g, [Plain Language Is for Everyone, Even Experts](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/plain-language-experts/), Oct 8, 2017. **Pass on the first screen; partly below.** Home lead: two sentences, 20 words. Carver's warrant sentence runs past 60 words (already flagged in the earlier study); "(a moratorium)", "(40B)" and "(the board that grants exceptions to the zoning rules)" are the right first-use glosses.

## Section 3: the first use, step by step

## Summary

1. 48 sources read first hand and dated; Krug is cited second-hand (his sites were down or blocked), and no first-hand Zhuo or Spolsky piece on this topic was reachable.
2. The sources agree on five things a professional home page does in its first screen: say what the site does in specific words, show the real content, offer one action, name who is behind it, and show when it was last updated.
3. Agenda Ahead passes 30 of 60 checks, half-passes 24 and fails 6; it is strongest on structure (one typeface, one accent, real rows in the first screen, nothing gated, honest empty states, a proper 404) and weakest on identity and currency.
4. The most visible fault today is a contradiction: "We read the agendas every morning; last checked Oct 30, 2:33 pm" on Nov 2, with the same three-day gap on every town page.
5. No page names a person, place or reason; /contact has no email address; the footer's "About" group has no About page.
6. The headline is a promise without nouns; the states are named only in How it works and the footer, so out-of-area visitors learn by failing.
7. Two rendering mistakes are live: the /watch step-2 list runs town, board and date together, and the home page's "two Bostons" message is hidden under the suggestion list.
8. Four different taglines describe the site across the H1, the social image, its alt text and the meta description; the favicon has no .ico or touch-icon fallback; there is no print sheet.
9. The rebuilt home page keeps today's order (header, headline, one line, box, real rows, sample, how it works) and adds a dated proof line, a "Who makes this" paragraph, a second town box and a footer that carries a name, an email and "Updated Mon Nov 2, 7:03 am".
10. Of the 15 ranked changes, the first eight are words, a build rule and two image files; none needs a framework, a script or a new colour.
