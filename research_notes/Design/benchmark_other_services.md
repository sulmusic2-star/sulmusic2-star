# Benchmark: Agenda Ahead against services that do each job well

2026-10-13. Evidence: the preview build read as HTML, its five CSS and JS files, and headless-Chromium screenshots at 390×844 and 1440×900; every benchmark fetched or screenshotted the same day. Sources are numbered at the end. GovTrack answered "502 Bad Gateway" to three requests and is left out.

## 1. Where we stand

**Where we excel.** Nobody else in this set writes the item for the reader. Councilmatic shows "Ordinance O2026-0029019", "Active", "9/29/2026 - Referred" [5]; CivicPlus shows "Regular Meeting Agenda (PDF)" and "Posted Oct 8, 2026 2:41 PM" [8]; we show "One-year ban on new data centers: Town Meeting vote (Article 16)" with a "Before this:" history line and the warrant's own wording one tap away (our meeting page). Our home page is 152 words, one question, three rows and one link; only Pinboard, Hacker News and GOV.UK are as spare [12][1][9]. Body type is 18 px Barlow with dark mode and a reduced-motion rule; Hacker News, Councilmatic and weather.gov sit at 13 to 16 px [1][5][7]. Alerts need no account and say so; Nixle asks for email, password, full name and two phone numbers on one form [11]; Nextdoor shows nothing without signing up [7]. Search is static (Pagefind 1.5.2, 3,001 pages indexed) and costs no server [10]. The "Next door: Bourne (nothing coming up)" line and "No towns match. Check the spelling, or search by county." are better empty states than any benchmark showed.

**Where we lack.** The town picker is a native `<datalist>` of 447 options, which MDN calls "not Baseline", unstylable and unannounced by some screen readers [14]; GOV.UK's own autocomplete exists because of that [9]. The /towns filter is broken on this build: `form[data-list-filter]` has no `[data-status]` element, so `app.js` throws "Cannot read properties of null (reading 'addEventListener')" and all 447 towns stay visible whatever you type, including the home page's `/towns?q=` fallback. On a phone, /trending spends the first 627 px of an 844 px screen on eleven 30 px chips and a "near your town" box before the first story; Hacker News gets 16 stories into the same height [1]. The filters only sift the 20 rendered rows ("1 of 20 stories"), so "Cell towers" shows one story rather than every cell-tower story. We never say when an agenda was posted, which CivicPlus does on every row and Hacker News does in every sub-line [8][1]. Lists repeat the date on every row where Luma and Meetup group by day [3][4]. Nobody can type a ZIP code, which weather.gov, Patch and Zillow all accept [7]. The /topics table wraps its headers to "Coming / up" and "In / all" at 390 px.

## 2. The services

**Hacker News (ranked list).** One list, 30 rows, two lines a row: "11 year old unsoundness bugs found in Rust borrow checker (github.com/rust-lang)" then "325 points by formerly_proven 3 hours ago | hide | 167 comments". Verdana 10 pt titles, 7 pt grey sub-lines, a triangle for the vote, ages instead of dates, and no filters; it fits a 390 px screen with no horizontal scroll [1]. Better than us: density and the age ("3 hours ago"). We do better: a sentence a reader can act on, the stage word, real dates. Borrow: the sub-line pattern, one grey line under the title carrying posted-age and meeting-day.

**Product Hunt (ranked list by period).** Four stacked lists headed "Top Products Launching Today", "Yesterday's Top Products", "Last Week's Top Products", "Last Month's Top Products", each with "See all"; rows are rank, logo, name, tagline, topic tags with "•", two counts, and a "Promoted" badge on every row [2]. Better than us: period is a heading, not a control. We do better: nothing is promoted. Borrow: when "Any date" is chosen on /trending, render period headings ("This week", "Next week", "Later") rather than a chip row.

**Luma and Meetup (event lists with date filters).** Luma's Boston page is a vertical timeline: a dot, "Today Tuesday", then "3:00 PM / Lovable: Hackathon x Startup Trek / By Owen Hacker… / 1 Lincoln St / +45", then "Tomorrow Wednesday". The city is picked from tiles with counts ("New York 86 Events") under region tabs; an email box ("me@email.com" / "Subscribe") sits in the hero [3]. Meetup puts four dropdowns in one row ("Any day", "Any size", "Any type", "Within 18 miles") above rows reading "Tue, Oct 20", "10:00 AM EDT", "Online", "7 attendees" [4]. Better than us: day grouping and the single filter row. We do better: we skip the hero photo and the app push. Borrow: Luma's day headings with a thin left rule.

**Councilmatic, Chicago and LA Metro (civic trackers).** "Chicago City Council, demystified", a search box with placeholder "police, zoning, O2015-7825, etc.", a "Next City Council meeting Wed 10/14/2026 10:00a.m." card, and a plain summary: "council members took action on 54 pieces of legislation, including 30 that are non-routine" [5]. LA Metro adds "Agenda will be posted no later than 72 hours prior to the start of the meeting" [6]. No follow or alert control is visible on either. Stack from the repo: Django on the Open Civic Data models, "Search infrastructure powered by django-haystack and Solr", scrapers in pupa, MIT [5]. Better than us: the one-sentence count line and the posting promise. We do better: static, no Solr, write-ups per item. Borrow: the "posted no later than" line, computed per town from our own 4,116-agenda sample.

**Open States, now Plural (legislation lookup).** "Find Your Legislators." with one box, "Enter your address", and "Look up a bill"; DM Sans 18 px body, a serif display heading, a chat bubble saying "Hi!" [7]. The repo is "the website and v2 API (graphql)" in Django; Plural "adopted" the project "in 2021" and now sells the tracker [7]. Better than us: address-first. We do better: free, no chat widget. Borrow: address or ZIP as the first question.

**Legistar (San Francisco) and CivicPlus Agenda Center (Plymouth) (the incumbents).** Legistar's calendar is `<select>`s: years "All Years, 2026 … 1998, Last Year, Last Month … Next Year", and a body list with dozens of "(inactive)" committees; "##LOC[OK]##" leaks into the page text [8]. CivicPlus offers "RSS" and "Notify Me®", a "Search Agendas by:" panel (Time Period: Last Week, Last Month, Last 6 Months, Last Year; start and end dates; terms; category), and rows of "Oct 17, 2026", "Posted Oct 8, 2026 2:41 PM", "Regular Meeting Agenda (PDF)", "Download ▼" (Agenda, Previous Versions, HTML, PDF, Packet) [8]. Better than us: posted time, previous versions. Borrow: "Posted Oct 8" on each meeting.

**weather.gov, Patch, Zillow, Nextdoor (place pickers).** weather.gov: "Local forecast by 'City, St' or ZIP code", with "Sorry, the location you searched for was not found" and "Multiple locations were found. Please select one of the following:"; the page is 990 px wide on a 390 px phone [7]. Patch: one card, "Your town or ZIP code" + "Email address" + "Subscribe", for "30,000 U.S. communities" [7]. Zillow: "Address, neighborhood, city, ZIP" [7]. Nextdoor: "Discover your neighborhood" over a sign-up wall [7]. Better than us: ZIP everywhere; Patch's two-field sign-up. We do better: responsive, no wall. Borrow: ZIP in the box, and the "Multiple locations" disambiguation sentence for names shared across states (Amherst, Boston, Warwick).

**GOV.UK (one question, accessible autocomplete).** "Find your local council" asks "Enter a postcode / For example SW1A 2AA / Find" and nothing else [9]. The design system says a select "should only be used as a last resort in public-facing services" [9]. Its autocomplete component takes `source`, `minLength`, `showAllValues`, `confirmOnBlur`, uses an aria-live region, can enhance a server-rendered `<select>` "for lists of a few hundred options or fewer", and "advises against using placeholder text" [9]. The address pattern says to accept postcodes "in upper or lower case and with or without spaces" and "provide a manual option" [9]. Borrow: the component, or its behaviour, on / and /watch.

**Google Alerts and Nixle (alert sign-up).** Google: a box, "Create an alert about…", then "Show options": How often "As-it-happens / At most once a day / At most once a week", "Create Alert" [11]. Nixle: "Sign Up (It's easy and anyone can join)" over email ("Public safety messages are sent here"), password, full name, two phones and "I Accept. Sign me up!" [11]. Better than us: Google's frequency choice. We do better: one question per step, "At most one email a day, only when something new is posted", no password. Borrow: "At most once a week" as a second option.

**Pagefind and DocumentCloud (document search).** Pagefind: "fully static search library", "search index is split into chunks", "a 10,000 page site with a total network payload under 300kB… closer to 100kB"; the UI shows "Load more", filters with zero counts unless `showEmptyFilters` is false, and 1.5.0 replaced the Default UI with a Component UI [10]. We run 1.5.2 with a hand-written UI in search.js. DocumentCloud: "Search documents… Ctrl+K", rows of thumbnail, a globe "Public" mark, title, "1 page - Documenters App (documenters-ea-org) - Tue Oct 13 2026", checkboxes with "Select All / Actions" [10]. Better than us: filter counts, page counts. We do better: highlighted excerpts, "61 agendas match", "Email me when a new one is posted" beside the results. Borrow: counts next to each filter value from `pagefind.filters()`.

**Pinboard, Kagi, Cloverleaf (craft, and its opposite).** Pinboard: "Welcome to Pinboard! / Social Bookmarking for Introverts", Helvetica 13 px, "There are no ads and no trackers of any kind. Users pay a modest yearly fee.", two buttons, "Sign up for $22 / year" [12]. Kagi: "Reclaim the web, restore your privacy", then tool cards, testimonials, FAQ and a mascot: a standard product page [12]. Cloverleaf, the nearest commercial neighbour: "Intelligence built for the full B2G sales cycle, tailored to your patch.", "70,000+ government agencies", "49,900% ROI" ("sounds ridiculous"), "ONE DAILY EMAIL", stat blocks, four benefit cards with photos and icons, "Request a demo" [13]. We already read like Pinboard, not Cloverleaf. Borrow from Pinboard: say the price (free) and the limit (one town and its neighbours) in one sentence on the home page.

## 3. Ranked changes

1. **Fix the /towns filter.** `setupListFilter` in app.js assumes a `[data-status]` select the form no longer has; the first `addEventListener` throws and every keystroke throws on `status.value`. Typing "ply" leaves 447 towns visible. Guard the missing element. Evidence: console errors captured 2026-10-13. 1 h.
2. **Replace the datalist on / and /watch with a visible suggestion list.** Up to 8 rows under the box (Baymard: "4 to 8" on mobile, "the return key should submit the currently focused suggestion" [15]), town in bold, state in grey, built from the same 447 options; keep the datalist as the no-JS fallback. Model: GOV.UK accessible-autocomplete, `minLength: 1`, `confirmOnBlur`, aria-live [9]; MDN on why the datalist cannot be styled or reliably announced [14]. 8 h.
3. **Accept ZIP codes in the same box.** Add a ZIP-to-town table for MA, NY and RI to the suggestion source so "02360" offers "Plymouth, MA". weather.gov, Patch and Zillow all take a ZIP [7]; GOV.UK asks for the postcode first [9]. 4 h.
4. **Shrink the /trending filter block on phones.** Replace the chip rows with one line of two dropdowns, "Any date" and "All topics", as Meetup does ("Any day", "Any type") [4], and move "Put stories near your town first" under the list. Today the first story starts at 627 px on an 844 px screen; chips are 30 px tall against the brief's 44 px rule. 4 h.
5. **Filter the full list, not the top 20.** trending.js filters the rendered rows ("1 of 20 stories"); /data/trending.json already holds 100. Filter that and render the matches, so a topic chip shows every story on that topic. 3 h.
6. **Group rows by day.** On /trending ("Any date"), /ma/<town> and the home list, print a day heading once ("Tue Oct 13") and drop it from the rows beneath, as Luma ("Today Tuesday") and Meetup do [3][4]. In build.py's list renderers and trending.js's `storyNode`. 3 h.
7. **Say when the agenda was posted.** Add "posted Thu Oct 8" to the facts line on /m/* and /ma/*, and to the alert email. CivicPlus prints it on every row [8]; Hacker News prints age on every sub-line [1]. The first-seen date exists in the tracker. 2 h.
8. **Per-town posting lead.** On /ma/<town>, under "Next meeting on our list", add "This town usually posts agendas N days ahead" where the 4,116-agenda sample covers it, after LA Metro's "Agenda will be posted no later than 72 hours prior" [6]. build.py. 6 h.
9. **Filter counts on /search.** Open the "Filters" disclosure when a query is present and show counts beside each value ("Massachusetts 48, New York 9") from `pagefind.filters()`; Pagefind's own UI shows them and hides zeros with `showEmptyFilters: false` [10]. 4 h.
10. **Make /topics a list on phones.** Below 640 px render each topic as one line, "Solar: 42 coming up, 252 in all, 83 towns", instead of a four-column table whose headers wrap to "Coming / up". styles.css and build.py. 1 h.
11. **Offer "At most once a week".** On /watch's email step add a second frequency under the daily default, in Google Alerts' words [11]; store it with the alert list and batch in the Vercel sender. 5 h.
12. **One grey count line on the home page.** Under "This week", "164 items at upcoming meetings in 312 towns; see everything coming up", as Councilmatic's "took action on 54 pieces of legislation, including 30 that are non-routine" [5]. build.py. 1 h.
13. **Copy: one name per action.** On /trending the label says "Put stories near your town first" and the button says "Sort by my town"; use the label's words on both. 0.5 h.

## 4. Trends, 2025 to 2026

What reads as machine-made, from people who looked: indigo as the default colour, purple-to-blue gradients, Inter everywhere, "soft gray shadows on floating cards", emoji as feature icons, and coloured left borders on cards, "almost as reliable a sign of AI-generated design as em-dashes for text" (Jonathan, Robots on Payroll, 21 July 2026) [16]. Anthropic's own front-end guidance names "content chopped into identical rounded cards", "gradient washes as decoration" and "defaults rather than choices" [17]. A review of 100 vibe-coded sites (6 Aug 2025) found the technical tells: "the majority of sites had incomplete or missing OpenGraph metadata", missing alt text, default favicons, "2024 copyright dates", and heading levels "jumping from H1 to H4" [18]. In copy: em-dashes, "delve", the "bullet-point sandwich" [16]. Cloverleaf's stat blocks and photo cards are the civic version [13].

What reads as crafted: a typeface chosen for the job (GDS Transport, Barlow, Verdana) rather than Inter; one accent used once ("Spend your boldness in one place" [17]); motion only "that answers a person's action" [17]; dense, uniform rows (Hacker News, Luma's timeline); a plain sentence where others put a badge. A September 2026 reality check found bento grids and dark mode held, while kinetic type, glassmorphism and organic shapes were "more polish than substance", and brutalism "is not retro nostalgia. It is positioning." [19]. We pass every tell above except two: /trending opens on a block of chips, and no sentence says the price and the limit together.

## 5. The town picker

The job is to choose one of 447 towns across three states on a phone, where suggestions sit "sandwiched between the search field at the top and the mobile keyboard below" [15]. The evidence points to one pattern: a single text box that accepts a town name or a ZIP, with a visible list of at most eight suggestions under it, each a full tap row, and a sentence for the two failure cases.

Why not the alternatives. A `<select>` of 447 is Legistar's committee list, and GOV.UK calls a select "a last resort" [8][9]. A native `<datalist>` is what we have: MDN says it is "not Baseline", that "targeting the list of options with CSS is very limited to non-existent", and that "some screen reader/browser combinations… do not announce the contents of the autosuggest popup" [14]. Luma's tile grid works for 30 cities, not 447 [3]. An A–Z page is a fallback, which /towns already is (9,253 px tall on a phone), once its filter is fixed.

What the good ones do. GOV.UK asks for a postcode first, with a hint ("For example SW1A 2AA") instead of a placeholder, and accepts it "in upper or lower case and with or without spaces" [9]. Its autocomplete confirms on blur and announces results; Baymard asks to "emphasize the predictive portion" [9][15]. weather.gov handles the two failures in words: "Sorry, the location you searched for was not found" and "Multiple locations were found. Please select one of the following:" [7]. Patch pairs the place box with the email box, two fields and a button [7].

For Agenda Ahead: keep "Type your town" on /, add "or ZIP code" to the label, show suggestions as "Plymouth, MA (1 meeting)" rows with the state in grey, let Enter take the first row, write "Amherst is in Massachusetts and New York. Which one?" when a name is shared, and keep "Ask us to add this town" for the 50 towns we cannot read. Changes 1 to 3 above are this pattern in order.
