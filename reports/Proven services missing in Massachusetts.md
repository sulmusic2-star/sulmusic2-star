# Licensed Staff Unlock Massachusetts' Underserved Service Markets

For a bootstrapped owner who wants to manage rather than do the work, the best fit is a **private-pay, non-medical home care agency in Plymouth and Bristol counties (South Shore and South Coast)**. The timing works because Massachusetts' first home care agency licensure law (Chapter 180 of the Acts of 2026, signed August 6, 2026) takes effect through 2027, and a franchise entry costs about **$89K–$172K**. A close and lower-drama second choice is a **business-to-business "kitchen compliance" route**: code-required hood cleaning, fire-suppression and extinguisher inspection, and backflow testing. It costs about **$184K–$232K** to enter, and private-equity fire-safety roll-ups give it a built-in exit. With **$250K–$700K**, two under-represented consumer franchises fit Worcester County's thin personal-care supply: Great Clips, which has no salons in Worcester city or the Boston core, and Scenthound, which had no Massachusetts location in the research. If you buy instead, the numbers produce owner income while a paid general manager runs operations only for **retiring-owner businesses with roughly $400K or more of seller's discretionary earnings (SDE)**, meaning profit before the owner's own pay. Infant and toddler child care has the best-documented shortage in the state (70% of infants live in "access deserts"), but it needs the most capital. Massachusetts law shapes every option. The **ABC independent-contractor test** makes the people who deliver your core service W-2 employees, and getting that wrong brings treble damages and personal liability for officers. Most of these businesses also need a licensed person on payroll. Two cautions apply throughout. Most franchise cost and earnings figures come from third-party aggregators whose numbers conflict by up to about 2x, and you must check them against current Franchise Disclosure Documents (FDDs). And in most categories the research found strong demand and labor shortages, not hard proof that providers are missing.

## Worcester and Plymouth counties pair fast growth with thin supply

Massachusetts barely grew last year: **15,524 people between July 2024 and July 2025, about 0.2%**. So a "growth market" here means the few places where people, income and new homes are concentrating ([Census V2025 county estimates](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/datasets/2020-2025/counties/totals/co-est2025-alldata.csv)). The research team scored the twelve mainland counties on population growth, income, building permits, establishment supply and wage cost. **Plymouth County (0.751) and Worcester County (0.722) came first and second**, and both stayed in the top three under every alternative weighting tested. Norfolk, Middlesex and Bristol followed ([Census CBP 2023](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23co.zip); [BLS QCEW 2025](https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2025/a/area/25000.csv)). The composite is the team's own construction from federal bulk files, not a published index. The underlying pattern still repeats in both the 2023 Census establishment counts and the 2025 BLS data.

Worcester County is the clearest case of supply lagging demand. It grew fastest of any mainland county, **+3.06% (+26,409 people) since 2020**, and permitted 9,430 housing units from 2021 to 2025, the most outside the Boston core ([Census V2025](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/datasets/2020-2025/counties/totals/co-est2025-alldata.csv); [Census Building Permits](https://www2.census.gov/econ/bps/County/co2025a.txt)). Yet per resident it has well below the state's supply of personal care (0.65–0.72x the state rate), beauty salons (0.60–0.68x), child care (0.71–0.81x), fitness (0.73–0.76x) and bookkeeping (0.67–0.77x) ([CBP 2023](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23co.zip); [QCEW 2025](https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2025/a/area/25027.csv)). Most of its growth comes from international immigration; net domestic migration was −8,298. That makes Worcester city (median household income $70.1K) a younger, price-sensitive volume market. The ring of Shrewsbury, Westborough, Grafton and Northborough, with incomes of $131K–$153K, is where premium services sell ([ACS 2020–24](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/summary_file/2024/table-based-SF/data/5YRData/)). Costs favor Worcester as well. Office space there asks about **$22.79 per square foot, against $66.32 in downtown Boston** ([CommercialCafe Worcester](https://www.commercialcafe.com:443/office/us/ma/worcester); [CBRE Q2 2026](https://mktgdocs.cbre.com/2299/cff9e2d3-dcdc-4fab-a746-f83ade7f1460-414677795/Q2_2026_DT_Office_Figures_%28Sho.pdf)). Wages for front-line service work run only 8–14% below the state rate ([QCEW 2025](https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2025/a/area/25000.csv)). The main saving from leaving Boston is rent, not labor.

Plymouth and Bristol counties draw a different crowd: domestic movers priced out of Greater Boston. Plymouth combines 3.02% growth, **$114.2K median household income**, the highest permitting rate of any mainland non-urban county (12.8 per 1,000 residents, mostly single-family homes) and an older population: 19.8% are 65 or older county-wide and 25.2% in Plymouth town ([Census V2025](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/datasets/2020-2025/counties/totals/co-est2025-alldata.csv); [ACS 2020–24](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/summary_file/2024/table-based-SF/data/5YRData/)). It is short on dentists, home health and child care, but already crowded with plumbing and HVAC contractors (1.36x the state rate). Bristol County covers the Taunton–Raynham corridor and the South Coast suburbs. It has lower incomes ($85.6K) but rising permits, with a five-year high of 1,156 units in 2025. Its suburbs are old: 21–25% of residents are 65 or older in Dartmouth, Westport, Somerset and Swansea. And it has among the thinnest home-health supply in eastern Massachusetts, 0.57–0.75x the state rate ([Census Building Permits](https://www2.census.gov/econ/bps/County/co2025a.txt); [QCEW 2025](https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2025/a/area/25000.csv)).

The rest of the map is less attractive than the raw counts suggest. Cape Cod has the oldest population (33.1% aged 65+). It looks oversupplied only because the counts divide by year-round residents. The real constraint there is labor and housing: **47% of Cape jobs are held by people who live elsewhere** ([Cape Cod Chamber](https://www.capecodchamber.org/articles-business/post/ceo-corner-framing-the-housing-challenge-on-cape-cod/)). Hampden, Franklin and Berkshire counties are thin in many categories, but they are shrinking and have the lowest incomes, so their gaps sit mostly in services paid by MassHealth (the state Medicaid program) or child-care subsidies. Suffolk County's low contractor counts are structural, since suburban firms serve Boston. One limitation applies everywhere. These counts include only businesses with paid employees, so sole proprietors such as stylists, cleaners and landscapers are invisible, and the shortfall in those categories is overstated.

## Seven proven models, ranked for an owner who manages but does not serve

The ranking weighs four things: how strong the Massachusetts-specific gap evidence is, whether the model is proven elsewhere, whether a non-licensed owner can legally stay out of service delivery under Massachusetts law, and whether the capital fits a bootstrapped individual. A warning about the numbers applies to every franchise figure below. Nearly all Item 7 (initial investment) and Item 19 (financial performance) figures came through aggregator websites (franchiseinvestordata.com, Franzy, SharpSheets, consultancy blogs), not from the FDD PDFs themselves. One of those sites is internally inconsistent. Another warned that its state unit counts are population-based estimates, not real counts ([franchiseinvestordata – Scenthound](https://franchiseinvestordata.com/franchise/scenthound)). Treat every franchise number as something to confirm against the brand's 2026 FDD.

### 1. Home care agencies get a licensing reset in 2027

Demand is the clearest in the state. Massachusetts' 85-and-older population is projected to grow from **172,034 today to 274,527 by 2040**, while **34 nursing facilities with 3,515 beds have closed since 2020** ([Dorchester Reporter/State House News](https://www.dotnews.com/2025/08/20/care-infrastructure-under-pressure-state-s-oldest-population-explodes/)). The state's median home health aide price was **$86,944 a year in 2024, about $38 an hour** at 44 hours a week, against $77,792 nationally ([CareScout via Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250301706133/en/)). Workers, not agencies, are what is scarce. 7 Investigates reported more than 4,000 older adults waiting for home care mainly because of staff shortages, though the report is undated. Human-services providers had a **15% vacancy rate** in client-facing roles as of January 2026 ([WHDH](https://whdh.com/news/7-investigates-home-healthcare-worker-shortage/); [UMass Donahue Institute](https://donahue.umass.edu/news-events/institute-news/report-massachusetts-human-services-workforce-stretched-to-capacity)). County data show where coverage is thinnest. Statewide there are 7.13 home-health establishments per 10,000 residents aged 65+, but only 5.96 in Plymouth, 5.32 in Bristol and 5.21 in Barnstable ([CBP 2023](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23co.zip) with [ACS age data](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/summary_file/2024/table-based-SF/data/5YRData/)). Franklin (2.86) and Berkshire (4.63) are thinner still, but their median incomes of $74.9K and $76.0K make private pay a harder sell.

The timing case rests on the new law, and the research notes agree on how it got there. The House passed H.4706 on November 5, 2025, and a Senate amendment (S.3183) is dated July 16, 2026 ([H.4706](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H4706.pdf); [S.3183](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/S3183.pdf)). The Legislature then enacted the reconciled bill, **H.5627, on July 31, 2026** ([MA Legislature press release](https://malegislature.gov/PressRoom/Detail?pressReleaseId=1473)). A private-pay agency's blog reports that Governor Healey **signed it on August 6, 2026 as Chapter 180 of the Acts of 2026**, after 157–0 and 40–0 votes ([Care Remedy, Aug 2026](https://www.careremedy.com/massachusetts-to-start-licensing-home-care-agencies-just-not-yet-and-that-gap-is-worth-understanding-before-you-hire)). One research note could not confirm enactment, and a compliance site still said Massachusetts had no licensure; both relied on sources written before the signing ([Vitable Health](https://www.vitablehealth.com/home-care-compliance/massachusetts)). Because the signing date comes from a partly promotional blog, confirm the chaptered text and its effective date on malegislature.gov.

Under the law, the Executive Office of Health and Human Services (EOHHS) will license agencies. Each agency will need workers' compensation and liability insurance, annual training that includes dementia care, background checks, and written service plans that state the total cost of care. **Everyone holding 5% or more of the ownership must pass a suitability review, and the agency must show sufficient financial capacity.** Unlicensed businesses may not advertise as home care agencies ([MA Legislature](https://malegislature.gov/PressRoom/Detail?pressReleaseId=1473)). EOHHS must issue temporary regulations and licenses within 180 days of the effective date and final regulations within a year ([CarePolicy.us](https://carepolicy.us/blogs/blog/massachusetts-enacts-first-statewide-licensing-system-for-home-care-agencies)). That puts final rules around mid-to-late 2027. About **400 home care companies operate in Massachusetts, and only about one-third currently answer to any regulator** ([Care Remedy](https://www.careremedy.com/massachusetts-to-start-licensing-home-care-agencies-just-not-yet-and-that-gap-is-worth-understanding-before-you-hire)). The law therefore raises the bar for the unregulated two-thirds and explicitly lets a non-clinician own an agency.

The proven model is a franchise, and the economics are known only roughly. Aggregators put Home Instead's average gross sales at about $2.75M (FY2025) or about $2.39M (2024 FDD), with initial investment of about $112.5K–$156.5K ([FranchiseInvestorData](https://franchiseinvestordata.com/franchise/home-instead); [Franzy](https://franzy.com/franchises/home-instead)). Right at Home is about $1.34M average unit volume on $89K–$165K ([Franzy](https://franzy.com/franchises/right-at-home)). For Visiting Angels, a low-reliability consultancy blog cites a $1.3M median on $125K–$172K ([PulseRevOps](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/q14735)). Those are revenue figures. **The research found no verified Massachusetts home care operating margin**, so the owner's share has to be built from Item 19 and local caregiver wages. In SBA lending data, Home Instead charged off 3 of 170 loans (1.8%), and franchised home health as a category charged off 6.9% ([MMCG Invest](https://www.mmcginvest.com/post/best-franchises-to-own-in-2026-what-the-sba-loan-data-actually-says)).

The risks are real. National brands are already in Massachusetts; Senior Care Authority entered in 2025 ([Franchising.com MA news](https://www.franchising.com/Massachusetts_opportunities/news)). A new agency adds capacity only if it recruits caregivers better than incumbents do. The financial-capacity test is a hurdle for a thinly capitalized owner. And registry-style models built on 1099 caregivers fail the ABC test, discussed below. A low-capital add-on is senior move management, which runs $55–$100 an hour nationally and $2,500–$5,000 per downsizing move ([Kiplinger](https://kiplinger.com/article/retirement/t013-c000-s004-a-move-manager-helps-you-downsize-in-retirement.html)). A Massachusetts franchise of this type listed for sale claims only $175,000 in revenue with three employees, so it works as a complement, not a standalone ([BizBuySell listing](https://www.bizbuysell.com/business-opportunity/trusted-senior-transition-franchise-strong-affluent-ma-territory/2409925)).

### 2. Kitchen and fire compliance routes sell visits the code requires

Massachusetts has **15,294 restaurant locations** ([National Restaurant Association](https://www.restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/industry-statistics/massachusetts-state-fact-sheet)). The fire code requires their exhaust hoods to be cleaned on a schedule set by cooking volume: monthly for solid-fuel cooking, quarterly for high volume, semiannually for moderate volume and annually for low volume. That schedule comes from an older state Department of Fire Services (DFS) document, so verify it against the current code ([DFS](https://www.mass.gov/doc/cleaning-or-inspection-of-commercial-cooking-exhaust-systems/download)). Boston adds city registration and colored compliance stickers, and Boston's water commission requires grease traps and maintenance logs in every food establishment ([Boston sticker requirements](https://d8-dev2.boston.gov/sites/default/files/embed/s/sticker-requirements.pdf); [BWSC](https://www.bwsc.org/business-customers/programs-guidelines-assistance/commercial-kitchen-grease-trap-guidelines)). The licensing structure suits an owner who does not do the work. **Each technician holds a State Fire Marshal Certificate of Competency**, and only Type 1 holders may work for the public ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/service-details/commercial-cooking-exhaust-systems)). For extinguisher and suppression servicing, the *company* holds a DFS Certificate of Registration that lists the certificate number of every technician it employs ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/how-to/apply-for-licenses-to-service-fire-extinguishers-and-fire-extinguishing-systems)). The research did not confirm whether DFS also requires a designated licensed principal, so ask DFS before committing.

The model is proven in two ways. HOODZ, a hood-cleaning franchise, reported average unit revenue of about **$577,400** in its 2024 FDD, a range of roughly $422K–$2.9M, total investment of **$184K–$232K** and royalties of 7–10% ([Franzy](https://franzy.com/franchises/hoodz); [FranchiseOverview](https://franchiseoverview.com/company/hoodz)). The wider fire and life safety industry is being bought up. **Pye-Barker completed 57 acquisitions in 2025**, and Altus Fire & Life Safety has bought Massachusetts firms, including Priority Fire & Security of Clinton ([Security Systems News](https://www.securitysystemsnews.com/article/pye-barker-fire-safety-accelerates-growth-strategy-with-57-acquisitions-in-2025); [SSN – Altus](https://www.securitysystemsnews.com/tag/altus-fire-and-life-safety)). A sell-side M&A guide puts the implied EBITDA margin for fire protection firms at **18.8%** and says private-equity buyers want at least $1M of EBITDA and contract renewal above 90% ([Ad Astra Equity](https://www.adastraequity.com/sell-a-business/fire-protection-companies)). An analyst blog quotes APi management saying every $1 of inspection revenue generates $3–$4 of follow-on repair work at higher margins ([Kairos Research](https://kairosresearch.substack.com/p/apg-a-unique-fire-safety-company)). Applying the 18.8% margin to a HOODZ-average route would yield roughly $108K before owner costs. That is an extrapolation, not a disclosed figure.

Backflow testing adds a second recurring line. State rules require certain backflow preventers (reduced-pressure devices) to be tested **twice a year** by a MassDEP-certified tester. A failed device must be repaired by a licensed plumber or sprinkler contractor within 14 days, which you can subcontract to an independent plumbing firm ([MassDEP manual](https://www.mass.gov/doc/cross-connection-control-program-manual/download); [MassDEP test form](https://www.mass.gov/doc/backflow-prevention-device-inspection-maintenance-report-form-0/download)). Town bids budget only about $50–$100 per test, so backflow works as a bolt-on rather than a standalone business ([BidBanana](https://bidbanana.thebidlab.com/bid/4rUWgtNUFDVv2ROWKK6u)).

The honest weakness is that Massachusetts is not missing this service. The research found no count of certified hood cleaners, so the opening is a **labor and service-quality gap**. An industry site citing a 2025 survey reports a 14–20% skilled-labor shortfall ([FireProtectionFinder](https://fireprotectionfinder.com/news/fire-protection-workforce-shortage-nicet-2026)). Small restaurants may also be underserved as roll-ups consolidate. Municipal fire-alarm contracts carry prevailing-wage requirements ([Reading Post](https://thereadingpost.com/2022/03/02/fire-alarm-testing-inspection-maintenance-and-repair-services-contract-contract-no-22-31)). A sensible plan is to base the route in Worcester County, close to the I-495, I-90 and Route 9 restaurant corridors and cheaper to staff, and to add Boston only after the city's registration is in hand.

### 3. Infant and toddler care has the best-proven gap and the highest bar

No category has better shortage evidence. State data presented in August 2025 showed **about 59,000 infants (70%) and 43,000 toddlers (43%) living in child-care "access deserts"**, defined as areas with three or more children per licensed slot. Only 5% of preschoolers live in such areas. Some parts of Central Massachusetts have more than 10 children per slot, and seats go mainly to the highest-income families ([CommonWealth Beacon](https://commonwealthbeacon.org/newsletter/dl-08-06-25/)). Worcester County has 5.20 child-care centers per 1,000 children under five, against 6.61 statewide. Worcester city has 1.6 centers per 10,000 residents, against 2.99 statewide ([CBP 2023](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23co.zip); [ZBP 2023](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/zbp23detail.zip)). Prices are the highest in the country: **$27,067 a year for center-based infant care** (Child Care Aware data via First Trust), and over $26,000 in Middlesex and Norfolk ([First Trust](https://www.ftportfolios.com/Blogs/InvestBlog/2026/9/24/factoid---thursday,-september-24,-2026); [NBC Boston](https://nbcboston.com/news/local/2-mass-counties-rank-among-the-most-expensive-for-infant-care/2959511)).

The economics are tight. The state's cost study estimated Metro Boston infant care at **$136.55 a day to provide, against a $97.00 subsidy rate** (FY2023) ([Harvard Zaentz Navigator](https://zaentznavigator.gse.harvard.edu/policy-strategies/massachusetts-cost-estimation-model/)). C3 is the state's operating grant for child-care providers. It stayed flat at $475M for a third straight year in FY2027, and about 600 newer programs have been unable to get it ([MassBudget](https://massbudget.org/2026/07/21/massbudget-in-depth-analysis-of-fy-2027-gaa/)). A new entrant should therefore plan on private-pay tuition and **zero C3 revenue**. The owner does not need to teach: centers are licensed by the Department of Early Education and Care (EEC), and the business must employ a qualified director, though the research did not confirm the exact director rules ([Mass.gov EEC licensing](https://www.mass.gov/how-to/apply-for-a-large-group-and-school-age-child-care-license)).

Capital is the obstacle. Kids 'R' Kids had no Massachusetts academy in the research, which relied on searches, and runs about 160–170 academies in 16 states. A consultancy blog cites $700K to more than $6M of investment (unverified), and Franchising.com showed the brand "not currently accepting new applicants" there ([Franchising.com](https://www.franchising.com/kidsrkids/); [PulseRevOps](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/fr0923)). Primrose runs $2.2M–$8.0M per an aggregator, and Celebree is already claiming Boston with nine planned schools ([franchiseinvestordata – Primrose](https://franchiseinvestordata.com/franchise/primrose-schools); [Franchising.com](https://india.franchising.com/news/20250226_celebree_school_bolsters_boston_footprint_9_new_schools_to_open_throughout_.html)). Lenders like the category: franchised child care charged off only 2.6% of SBA loans, and Primrose 0 of 142 ([MMCG Invest](https://www.mmcginvest.com/post/best-franchises-to-own-in-2026-what-the-sba-loan-data-actually-says)). A lower-capital alternative is a network of licensed family child care homes, in which the owner supplies enrollment, marketing and business services. The state has more than 5,400 such homes ([MassBudget](https://massbudget.org/2024/08/05/testimony-eec-task-force/)), but no Massachusetts cost data was found for this model. Bright Horizons' 19% growth in backup care to more than $725M shows employer-paid demand that a local operator could pursue ([Bright Horizons annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001437578/000119312526162618/d98247dars.pdf)).

### 4. Great Clips and Scenthound leave Worcester County nearly untouched

Great Clips, the most established semi-absentee haircare franchise, has **31 Massachusetts salons, and none in Boston, Cambridge, Somerville, Quincy, Newton, Brookline, Lowell, Brockton, Framingham or Worcester proper** ([Great Clips MA directory](https://salons.greatclips.com/us/ma)). Thirty-one salons is about 0.75% of a system of roughly 4,150. That is about a third of what Massachusetts' share of the U.S. population would predict, by the research team's estimate. Per an industry publication's summary of the 2026 FDD, average salon sales were **$410,783, and average operating cash flow was $83,504** for a smaller reporting group ([1851 Franchise](https://1851franchise.com/great-clips-franchise-costs-fees-profit-and-data-2731798)). SBA charge-offs were 4 of 221 (1.8%) ([MMCG Invest](https://www.mmcginvest.com/post/best-franchises-to-own-in-2026-what-the-sba-loan-data-actually-says)). Great Clips' initial investment and royalty were not retrieved. The research also could not explain why the Boston core and Worcester city have no salons; developer territories or real estate are possible reasons. Ask franchise development which Massachusetts territories are open before going further.

Scenthound is a dog hygiene membership concept with about 141–154 units in roughly 25 states. **No Massachusetts location turned up in searches.** That check was search-based because the brand's locator page returned an error ([XMap](https://www.xmap.ai/brand-location-intelligence-reports/number-of-scenthound-locations-in-the-united-states-of-america)). Per an aggregator citing the 2026 FDD, investment is **$323K–$551K**. Average revenue was $489,150 across 69 mature units, and **average net operating income (NOI) was $84,406 (about 15%)** across a 52-unit subset. The top quartile averaged $208,890, while the bottom quartile lost $9,374. Members supply 84% of revenue ([franchiseinvestordata](https://franchiseinvestordata.com/franchise/scenthound); [BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/franchise-for-sale/scenthound/)). Sources disagree on whether Scenthound sells Massachusetts territories, and on whether it is semi-absentee or simply allows a hired manager. A related option, Woof Gang Bakery & Grooming, opened its first Massachusetts store in Boston only in December 2025 and reports about $740K average revenue in its 2024 FDD ([Woof Gang](https://woofgangbakery.com/blogs/news/woof-gang-expands-boston-and-fargo); [Franzy](https://franzy.com/franchises/woof-gang-bakery-grooming)).

For Great Clips, the best fit is Worcester city and its suburbs, where salons run 0.60–0.68x the state rate and personal-care wages are 14% below the state average. Scenthound fits high-income Worcester-ring or Merrimack Valley suburbs better than Plymouth County, where pet care already runs 1.33x the state rate ([CBP 2023](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23co.zip); [QCEW 2025](https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2025/a/area/25000.csv)). Two cautions apply. Labor is about 47% of Scenthound's revenue per the aggregator, so Massachusetts wages will pull results below national averages. And neither brand's average single-unit cash flow (about $84K) can pay a full general manager. Hands-off ownership of these concepts therefore means managing store managers across multiple units.

### 5. Buying a retiring owner's trade firm swaps startup risk for debt

Plenty of owners are ready to sell. **Owners aged 55 and over hold 53% of Massachusetts small businesses, about 68,000 companies with 775,000 employees** (Project Equity, reported in 2023) ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2023/06/14/business/worker-cooperatives-in-massachusetts)). Only a fraction are publicly listed: a point-in-time count found 99 Massachusetts listings on BizBuySell giving retirement as the reason for sale ([BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/massachusetts/retiring-owner-businesses-for-sale/)). So you will need to reach owners off-market. Nationally, the median service business sold for **$340,000 at 2.52x cash flow in 2025** ([BizBuySell Q4 2025](https://www.bizbuysell.com/news/bizbuysell-2025-fourth-quarter-insight-report/)). In Q2 2026, deals priced at $1M–$2M traded at about **3.1x SDE**, and $2M–$5M deals at about **4.0x EBITDA** ([IBBA Market Pulse Q2 2026](https://www.ibba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/mp-highlights-q2-2026.pdf)). Boston-metro listings asked a median of $499,000 against $200,000 of cash flow, though the date of that snapshot is unconfirmed ([BizBuySell data tables](https://www.bizbuysell.com/insight-report-data-tables/)).

HVAC and heat-pump service is a strong target because it has recurring demand that subsidies do not drive. Massachusetts had **133,753 heat-pump-heated homes at the end of 2025**, while new installations fell from 40,116 in 2024 to 30,567 in 2025 ([WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2026/02/13/massachusetts-climate-report-card-2025-heat-pumps-ev); [Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/2025-massachusetts-climate-report-card-clean-buildings)). Servicing the existing base is steadier than installing new systems. A consultant reports HVAC EBITDA of 20–24% and says each 10% of revenue moved into maintenance agreements adds 1–2 points of net margin ([Lightning Path Partners](https://lightningpathpartners.com/blog/plumbing-company-profit-margins); [Lightning Path – HVAC](https://lightningpathpartners.com/blog/how-profitable-is-hvac-business)). Contractor margins vary widely, though: the ACCA benchmark, as cited by a marketing agency, puts median HVAC net margin at 5.8%, against 13.2% for the top quartile ([Hook Agency](https://hookagency.com/blog/hvac-profit-margin/)). Contractor supply is thinnest in Hampden (0.61x the state rate for plumbing and HVAC) and Worcester (0.86x) and crowded in Plymouth (1.36x) ([CBP 2023](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23co.zip)). Licensing creates key-person risk. Mass Save heat-pump rebates of up to $10,000 require an installer-network contractor with trade licensure ([Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/enn/massachusetts-heat-pump-installer-network-has-momentum-in-second-year)). An electrical contractor must have a master electrician serving as its Licensee of Record ([Workiz](https://www.workiz.com/blog/electrician/massachusetts-electrician-license/); [Mass.gov form](https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2016/07/nt/master-elect-n-systems-contract-with-company.pdf)). Other Massachusetts businesses are also for sale. Express Employment Professionals staffing offices are listed for resale in Braintree ($450K), Worcester ($150K) and Springfield ($75K) ([Express – Braintree](https://expressfranchising.com/resales/braintree/); [Express – Worcester](https://expressfranchising.com/resales/worcester/); [BizQuest – Springfield](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/express-employment-professionals-in-springfield-ma/BW2440927/)). The section on scale below shows what owner income these deals produce.

### 6. Winter 2026 proved an ice dam shortage worth pairing

Ice dams are the one home-service category with direct evidence of a Massachusetts shortage. After the storms of early 2026, Wrentham's Connell Roofing missed more than 500 calls in its first week. Local roofers lacked steamers and could only remove snow. **Minnesota-based RTD Ice Dam Removal flew in crews, booked 30–40 Massachusetts jobs a day and charged $1,000 an hour with a two-hour minimum** ([Boston Globe, Feb 2026](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/02/10/metro/massachusetts-ice-dams-overwhelming-contractors)). National rates are $400–$600 an hour ([Fixr](https://fixr.com/costs/ice-dam-removal)). The Globe also called it the first winter since 2015 with problems this widespread, so peak demand comes and goes. The proven fix is to pair seasons with the same crews and trucks. Certified Christmas Lights grew out of a pest-control company into $5.8M of holiday-lighting revenue, and landscapers add lighting to keep crews employed year-round ([NALP](https://blog.landscapeprofessionals.org/business-smarts-adding-holiday-lighting-as-a-service/)). Mosquito control is the usual summer leg, but it is a poor gap play in Massachusetts. Mosquito Joe already has 9 Massachusetts locations, about 5% of its system ([Mosquito Joe](https://www.mosquitojoe.com/locations/)). And state pesticide certification requires two years of experience, which a new owner cannot meet personally ([MDAR bulletin](https://www.mass.gov/doc/pesticide-examination-and-license-information-bulletin/download)). Work on owner-occupied 1–4 unit homes requires Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration ([Mass.gov HIC](https://mass.gov/info-details/hic-contractor-resources)). No startup-cost figures for steaming equipment were found.

### 7. Christian Brothers is proven and absent, but you would be its GM

Christian Brothers Automotive's locator lists **31 states, with none in New England** ([CBAC locations](https://www.cbac.com/locations/)). Per an aggregator, its 2025 FDD shows **average net sales of $2,877,457 and average "owner benefit" of $314,559** across 280 shops. Owner benefit is the franchisee's half of a 50% profit split, on top of a $60,000 base salary. Investment is $550K–$680K, and SBA charge-offs were 0 of 137 ([franchiseinvestordata – CBAC](https://franchiseinvestordata.com/franchise/christian-brothers-automotive); [MMCG Invest](https://www.mmcginvest.com/post/best-franchises-to-own-in-2026-what-the-sba-loan-data-actually-says)). The catch is the owner role. The same source rates the brand "not absentee-friendly" and says owners work 45–55 hours a week as general manager. Massachusetts auto repair is also already well supplied: 1.11x the state rate in Worcester and Bristol and 1.20x in Plymouth ([CBP 2023](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23co.zip)). The owner would not fix cars, but this is a manager's job, not a hands-off investment.

### Bench options worth a look, and categories to skip

Several other models fit an owner who manages but does not serve, but the evidence for them is thinner:

- **Veterinary clinic with a hired vet.** A non-veterinarian may own a practice by appointing a licensed veterinarian as medical director ([MassVet](https://massvet.org/faqs)). Hampden County has 0.62–0.68x the state rate of veterinary practices. The shortage evidence is mostly from 2021–2024, and hiring the vet is the bottleneck.
- **Radon mitigation.** An estimated 650,000 Massachusetts homes exceed the EPA action level, and Worcester, Middlesex and Essex counties have the highest radon potential. Most systems cost $1,200–$2,000 ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/ma-epht-residential-radon); [DPH fact sheet](https://www.mass.gov/doc/fixing-radon-in-your-home/download)).
- **Boston and Cambridge building-emissions compliance.** Covered buildings must hire a credentialed third-party verifier, and fines run $150–$1,000 a day ([Boston.gov BERDO webinar](https://www.boston.gov/sites/default/files/file/2026/03/BERDO%20Data%20Verification%20Webinar%20-%2003.09.26_2.pdf)).
- **Commercial EV charger maintenance.** The state counted more than 9,400 public charging ports in May 2025 and needs about 46,300 by 2030 ([EVICC](https://www.mass.gov/doc/1-executive-summary/download)).
- **Two Maids residential cleaning.** It costs $93K–$150K and reports $637K median revenue, but the research found no Massachusetts location to confirm, and Boston is the home turf of competitor MaidPro ([Two Maids](https://twomaidsfranchise.com/the-maids-franchise); [franchiseinvestordata](https://franchiseinvestordata.com/franchise/two-maids)).

None of these has stronger Massachusetts gap evidence than the top seven.

Several categories are best avoided:

- **Services paid at MassHealth rates.** The basic adult day health rate fell from $106.32 to $99.49 ([101 CMR 310.00](https://www.mass.gov/doc/rates-for-adult-day-health-services-effective-july-1-2025/download)). Only 43% of dental offices accept MassHealth ([BINJ](https://binj.news/2026/03/19/more-than-half-of-mass-dental-offices-no-longer-accept-masshealth-due-to-low-reimbursement-rates)). Medicaid rates for applied behavior analysis (ABA, an autism therapy) are frozen ([Mass.gov testimony](https://www.mass.gov/doc/staff-testimony-for-rates-for-applied-behavior-analysis-presented-at-the-remote-public-hearing-on-june-17-2026-0/download)).
- **Commercial snow contracts.** One contractor's insurer dropped it after a $365,000 slip-and-fall settlement ([State House News via AgencyChecklists](https://agencychecklists.com/2025/05/16/liability-woes-leave-snow-contractors-on-slippery-ground-75811/)).
- **Brands with high SBA charge-off rates.** Window Genie charged off 55.6% of its SBA loans, The Grounds Guys 37.7% and Fantastic Sams 36.1% ([MMCG Invest](https://www.mmcginvest.com/post/best-franchises-to-own-in-2026-what-the-sba-loan-data-actually-says)).
- **Categories already crowded in Massachusetts.** Goldfish Swim School has 11 locations and Mosquito Joe 9 ([Goldfish](https://goldfishswimschool.com/boston)).

## Massachusetts law turns crews into employees and licenses into key people

The independent-contractor statute decides the design of every model above. Under M.G.L. c. 149 §148B, anyone performing a service is an employee unless all three prongs are met. The second prong, which requires the work to be "**performed outside the usual course of the business of the employer**", rules out 1099 caregivers, technicians, groomers or cleaners doing the company's core service ([M.G.L. c. 149 §148B](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section148B)). The statute makes "the president and treasurer of a corporation and any officer or agent having the management" **personally liable**. A worker who wins a case "shall be awarded **treble damages**" plus attorneys' fees ([M.G.L. c. 149 §150](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section150)). Courts apply the test widely. The state's highest court held in *Patel v. 7-Eleven* (2022) that it can reach franchise relationships, and in *Awuah v. Coverall* (2010) a federal court held that janitorial "unit franchisees" were misclassified employees ([Boston Bar Journal](https://bostonbar.org/journal/patel-v-7-eleven-sjcs-answers-to-certified-questions/); [Lathrop GPM](https://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/massachusetts-federal-court-compares-franchising-to-a-ponzi-scheme-and-labels-franchisees-as-employees)). The safe structure is W-2 staff for the core service. Subcontract only distinct licensed trades, such as a plumber for backflow repairs or a licensed grease hauler, and only to established firms with their own customers. Have counsel confirm each arrangement.

Licensing then sets which person you must hire before you can open:

| Business | Who must hold the credential | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Home care agency | The agency is licensed by EOHHS; owners of 5% or more pass a suitability review; the agency must show financial capacity | [MA Legislature](https://malegislature.gov/PressRoom/Detail?pressReleaseId=1473) |
| Hood cleaning and inspection | Each technician holds a Type 1 State Fire Marshal Certificate of Competency | [Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/service-details/commercial-cooking-exhaust-systems) |
| Extinguisher and suppression servicing | The company holds a DFS registration listing each technician's certificate | [Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/how-to/apply-for-licenses-to-service-fire-extinguishers-and-fire-extinguishing-systems) |
| Backflow testing | MassDEP-certified tester; repairs by a licensed plumber or sprinkler contractor | [MassDEP](https://www.mass.gov/doc/instructions-fact-sheet/download) |
| Electrical and EV charger work | Master electrician serving as Licensee of Record | [Mass.gov form](https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2016/07/nt/master-elect-n-systems-contract-with-company.pdf) |
| Home improvement (owner-occupied 1–4 unit homes) | HIC registration; Construction Supervisor License (CSL) for structural work | [Mass.gov](https://mass.gov/info-details/hic-contractor-resources) |

Several payroll costs apply on top of wages:

- **Minimum wage** is still **$15.00** (unchanged since 2023), with a bill to raise it pending ([OnPay](https://onpay.com/insights/minimum-wage-by-state-summary/massachusetts/)).
- **Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFML)** contributions total **0.88%** of wages in 2026. Employers with 25 or more covered workers must pay at least 0.42% themselves ([Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/2026-massachusetts-paid-family-and-medical-leave-updates-benefit-amount-contribution-rates-and-taxability-of-benefit-payments.html)). One source reports that from 2027 those larger employers will pay the full medical share, but this is unverified ([FNA Insurance](https://www.fnainsurance.com/resources/insights/Insight-News/Massachusetts-to-Make-Contribution-Changes-to-its-PFML-Program-for-2027/)).
- **Earned sick time** must be paid once you have 11 or more employees ([M.G.L. c. 149 §148C](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section148C)).
- **Workers' compensation** coverage is mandatory for every employer.

The 2018 noncompete law helps a buyer in one way and limits it in another. A noncompete signed by the *seller* of a business is enforceable. But noncompetes are void against hourly, non-exempt staff and capped at 12 months for others ([M.G.L. c. 149 §24L](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section24L)). You will have to keep technicians and caregivers with nonsolicitation agreements and retention pay.

Several rules changed in 2025–2026:

- **Home care licensure (Chapter 180 of the Acts of 2026).** Temporary licensing comes in 2027 and final rules around mid-to-late 2027.
- **SBA lending rules (SOP 50 10 8.1)**, in effect for loans numbered from October 1, 2026.
- **C3 child-care grants** level-funded for FY2027.
- **MassHealth ABA** rates frozen, with accreditation required by 2027–2028 ([Behavioral Health Business](https://bhbusiness.com/2024/10/04/massachusetts-mandates-accreditation-for-medicaid-aba-providers/)).

Finally, **Massachusetts is not a franchise registration state**, so no state examiner reviews a franchisor's FDD before it sells here. Independent franchise counsel matters more as a result ([Franchise Law Solutions](https://franchiselawsolutions.com/franchising/massachusetts-franchise-registration)).

## A $125K manager means hands-off income arrives only at scale

The figure that decides whether hands-off ownership works is the manager's salary. The median base pay for a general and operations manager in Massachusetts was **$123,850 in May 2024 and about $127,220 in May 2025**, against about $103K–$106K nationally ([WageDex/BLS](https://wagedex.com/jobs/general-and-operations-managers/massachusetts); [TradeCareerPath/BLS](https://tradecareerpath.com/programs/online-business-and-management-degrees/massachusetts/)). With bonus, payroll taxes and benefits, a capable GM costs roughly $150K–$180K all-in, by the research team's estimate. That is why a single franchise unit earning about $84K cannot be hands-off. It is also why "semi-absentee" marketing deserves skepticism. A 2015 Franchise Business Review survey found that most franchisees work 40–50 or more hours a week ([Business View/FBR](https://businessviewmagazine.com/digital-magazines/feb-2015/files/assets/basic-html/page54.html)). Franchise investor Erik Van Horn recommends at least 20 hours a week in year one, calls "set it and forget it" "a dangerous myth" and says absentee status must be "earned, not promised" ([Alloy Franchise](https://alloyfranchise.com/blog/why-most-franchisees-fail-at-semi-absentee-ownership/)).

Buying an existing business runs into the same arithmetic, now shaped by the SBA's new rules. Under SOP 50 10 8.1, effective for loans numbered from October 1, 2026, a first-time buyer must inject **10% of project costs, and that amount cannot be reduced**. At least half of it must come from "unlimited" sources such as the buyer's own unborrowed cash; seller notes on full standby and passive minority investors together can cover no more than the other half. **Debt-service coverage of 1.25x must be met on historical earnings, not projections.** The seller may stay on as a paid consultant for up to **24 months, up from 12** ([Analytics.loan](https://www.analytics.loan/post/sop-50-10-8-1-for-lenders); [Funder Intel](https://www.funderintel.com/post/sba-sop-50-10-8-1-7-things-brokers-need-to-know-before-october-1)). These details come from lender summaries, not the SOP text, so verify them with an SBA lender. Seller financing is scarce: only 29% of sellers plan to offer it, while 90% of buyers expect it ([Sundance/BizBuySell Q2 2026](https://sundancefg.com/resources/bizbuysell-q2-2026-insight-report)). The research team's illustrative math assumes a 10-year SBA loan at an assumed 9.5–10.5% rate and a fully loaded GM:

| Scenario | SDE | Price | GM cost | EBITDA after GM | Debt-service coverage | Owner pre-tax cash during loan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ~$1–1.5M revenue | $300K | $840K (2.8x) | $150K | $150K | 1.23–1.28x (can fail 1.25x) | ~$28K–$33K |
| ~$1.5–2.5M revenue | $400K | $1.2M (3.0x) | $150K | $250K | 1.43–1.49x | **~$75K–$82K** |
| ~$2.5–3.5M revenue | $550K | $1.705M (3.1x) | $175K | $375K | 1.51–1.57x | **~$127K–$137K** |

Inputs come from [IBBA](https://www.ibba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/mp-highlights-q2-2026.pdf) multiples and the SOP 8.1 summaries above. The interest rates are assumptions. In effect, a hands-off buyer pays **4.5–5.6x post-GM EBITDA** for a business priced at 2.8–3.1x SDE, because SDE includes the departing owner's own labor. Either negotiate on post-GM earnings, target businesses that already have a second-in-command, or plan to act as GM yourself for 12–24 months. Owner income in the $400K-SDE scenario rises to about $250K after the loan is repaid. A practical capital stack for a ~$1.2M deal needs about **$150K–$200K liquid** once closing costs and reserves are included.

Across capital levels, the options sort as follows. Figures are as cited in the sections above, mostly from aggregators, and must be verified.

|---|---|---|---|

Absentee owners also fail in predictable ways, and each has a control. A California business attorney has seen roughly three dozen cases in which a manager became "the face seen by vendors, employees and customers," then left to compete and took key staff ([Stimmel Law](https://stimmel-law.com/articles/absentee-owners-problems-and-solutions-part-one-stating-problem/)). A veterinary practice owner learned five years after buying that his long-time practice manager had funneled more than $200,000 ([DVM360](https://www.dvm360.com/view/life-after-embezzlement)). The controls follow directly from these cases:

- Keep bank logins and new-vendor approval with the owner.
- Use an outside bookkeeper.
- Buy employee-dishonesty insurance.
- Pay the GM a bonus tied to EBITDA, with phantom equity vesting over 3–5 years.
- Keep two license holders in any licensed trade.

## Verify the gap and the license holder before signing anything

Most of the shortlisted models rest on demand evidence, not proof that providers are missing, and on aggregator financials. The first 90 days should turn those assumptions into facts before any money is committed.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "what service is missing in Massachusetts?" to "**what labor and licensing bottleneck can I solve better than incumbents?**" In nearly every promising category the evidence is the same: seniors waiting for caregivers, a 15% vacancy rate among care workers, a 14–20% shortfall of fire-safety technicians, out-of-state ice dam crews filling local demand. The categories have providers; they lack staff. So the owner's real product in Massachusetts is recruiting, retaining and keeping licensed W-2 people compliant. The rules that look like obstacles to a hands-off owner work as protection for one who masters them: the ABC test, personal officer liability, credentials held by individual technicians, and the 2027 home care licensure rollout. A licensure transition that removes non-compliant operators is a rare opening. An owner who files early, budgets for the financial-capacity test and builds a caregiver pipeline in Plymouth or Bristol County enters as the market is being cleared.

The second takeaway is about sequencing. With a $125K-plus manager salary and SBA rules that now count only historical earnings, no realistic single-unit start or small acquisition in Massachusetts is passive from day one. The workable path is to choose a model whose license holders you can hire, manage it closely for 6 to 24 months, and grow to enough units, routes or SDE (roughly $400K or more) to carry a general manager. Recurring, code-required B2B revenue such as kitchen and fire compliance is the easiest of these to hand off later, and private-equity roll-ups are actively buying it. That buyer demand gives a bootstrapped owner something most of these businesses lack: a defined exit.
